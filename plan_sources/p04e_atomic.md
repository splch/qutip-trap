### 4.5 Atomic structure and the light-atom coupling chain

Every optical quantity in Sections 4.2 to 4.4 and 8 (Rabi frequencies, Stark shifts, scattering rates, branching ratios, repump and detection rates, the qubit frequency and its field sensitivity) is a function of a small set of atomic constants per species and of the beam geometry. The research runs of 2026-09-03 extracted those quantities as the papers quote them, per species and per experiment, and Section 12 recorded that the angular algebra behind them had been worked in the source set for 9Be+ only. This section specifies the layer that derives them for any species, so that a species module holds only measured constants with citations and everything hyperfine-resolved is computed. The conventions are Steck's (Quantum and Atom Optics, and the alkali D-line data notes), chosen because they are stated with every phase and normalization written out and are the ones most of the trapped-ion literature follows. The formulas below are standard atomic physics **[background]** whose transcription against those sources is checked in Run 4 (Appendix D); every number quoted is **[recomputed here]** with the scripts of Appendix D and compared with anchors that earlier runs verified independently.

#### 4.5.1 Fine, hyperfine and Zeeman structure at the operating field

A species module lists, per fine-structure level (n, L, S = 1/2, J): the energy, the lifetime, the magnetic-dipole and electric-quadrupole hyperfine constants A and B, and the Landé factor g_J; per isotope, the nuclear spin I and magnetic moment μ_I. From these the atomic module builds, for every level, the hyperfine-plus-Zeeman Hamiltonian in the |m_I, m_J⟩ basis,

H/h = A I·J + B [3(I·J)² + (3/2) I·J − I(I+1)J(J+1)] / [2I(2I−1)J(2J−1)] + (μ_B/h)(g_J J_z + g_I I_z) B,

with A and B in Hz and μ_B/h = 1.399624 MHz/G, the atomic layer working in Hz throughout and converting to rad/s at its boundary (Section 5.6),

with the quadrupole term present only for I ≥ 1 and J ≥ 1, and the nuclear factor g_I = −(μ_I/(I μ_N))(m_e/m_p) in Steck's sign convention, so that both g factors enter with one sign and g_I is of order 10⁻³ g_J; it diagonalizes H numerically at the configured field. The eigenvectors give the mixing between hyperfine states that the dipole matrix elements of Section 4.5.2 need at intermediate fields; the eigenvalues give every transition frequency, its first and second derivatives with respect to B (the sensitivities that H_int of Section 5.7 and the dephasing model of Section 6.3 consume), and the field-independent points. For J = 1/2 the closed Breit-Rabi form is the unit test of the diagonalization:

E(F = I ± ½, m_F)/h = −ΔE_hfs/(2(2I+1)) + (g_I μ_B/h) m_F B ± (ΔE_hfs/2)[1 + 4m_F x/(2I+1) + x²]^{1/2},  x = (g_J − g_I) μ_B B/(h ΔE_hfs),  ΔE_hfs = A(I + ½) with A in Hz (every term in Hz; the second revision added a joule to two Hz terms; 2026-09-04 theorist critique),

with the stretched states m_F = ±(I + ½) exactly linear in B. Recomputed anchors (Section 9.13): the sixteen 43Ca+ ground levels agree between the two methods to 10⁻¹² MHz at 0, 10, 146.0942 and 500 G; the 43Ca+ |4,0⟩ ↔ |3,+1⟩ transition is stationary at 146.0942 G with f₀ = 3,199,941,076.93 Hz and curvature 2.415 mHz/mG², matching Harty et al. (Section 4.3.3; A = −806.4020716 MHz, g_J = 2.00225664, μ_I = −1.31535 μ_N); 9Be+ |2,0⟩ ↔ |1,+1⟩ is stationary at 119.446 G with f₀ = 1,207,495,853.5 Hz against the 119.45 G and 1,207,495,843 Hz of Langer et al. 2005 (A = −625.008837048 MHz, μ_I = −1.177432 μ_N, g_J = 2.00226; the 10 Hz residual is the precision of the constants used, which fixes the test tolerance and the citation requirement); 25Mg+ |3,+1⟩ ↔ |2,+1⟩ is stationary at 212.78 G with f₀ = 1.686462 GHz against the 212.8 G and 1.686 GHz of Srinivas et al. (Section 4.4.5); and the 171Yb+ clock transition's quadratic shift is 310.9 Hz/G² both numerically and from the closed form (g_J − g_I)²μ_B²/(2h²A) with A = 12.642812118 GHz in Hz (the second revision printed 2hA, one factor of h short; 2026-09-04 theorist critique), against the 310.8 Hz/G² of Section 6.3. The coefficient moves by 0.03% across the three g_J values the second revision carried in different rows (2.00225664, which is 43Ca+'s; 2.00254; 2.00292, giving 310.76, 310.85 and 310.97 Hz/G²), so the plan adopts one cited value for the 171Yb+ ground state, g_J = 2.002615(70) (Han et al., arXiv:2501.09973, 2025, a multiconfiguration Dirac-Hartree-Fock and multireference configuration-interaction determination whose own second-order coefficient is 31.0869(22) mHz/µT² = 310.869 Hz/G²; the only other value in the literature is a spectroscopic 1.998 of larger uncertainty), which with g_I from μ_I = +0.49367 μ_N gives 310.87 Hz/G² and sets the test tolerance at ±0.02 Hz/G² from the g_J uncertainty, with the quoted 310.8 inside it (`check_critique_v3.py`) **[verified]**, **[corrected: critique, 2026-09-04]**, **[recomputed here]**. The negative A of 43Ca+ is what places F = 3 above F = 4 (Section 8.4); the module derives that ordering rather than storing it.

Two consequences for the rest of the plan. First, the field-sensitivity numbers of Section 6.3 (1.4 MHz/G per unit g_F m_F at low field, 2.8 MHz/G for a Δm_J = 1 Zeeman qubit; 310.8 Hz/G² as the coefficient of B² for 171Yb+, and for 43Ca+ at 146 G Harty's d²ν/dB² = 2.4 mHz/mG², i.e. 1.21 mHz/mG² as a coefficient of (δB)², the two conventions of the Section 13 curvature row) are now derived: the low-field slope is g_F μ_B m_F/h with g_F = g_J[F(F+1) + J(J+1) − I(I+1)]/(2F(F+1)) + g_I[F(F+1) − J(J+1) + I(I+1)]/(2F(F+1)), and the quadratic coefficients come from the diagonalization. Second, the operating field is a `Field` object with magnitude, direction and noise spectrum: its direction is the quantization axis of Section 4.5.3, and a field offset sampled per shot (Section 6.3) is converted into per-ion, per-level frequency offsets through the computed sensitivities, never through a hand-entered MHz/G.

#### 4.5.2 Dipole matrix elements, lifetimes, branching and saturation

The decay rate of an upper fine-structure level J′ to a lower level J fixes the reduced dipole matrix element, in Steck's normalization,

Γ_{J′→J} = ω³/(3πε₀ħc³) · (2J+1)/(2J′+1) · |⟨J‖d‖J′⟩|²,

and when J′ decays to several lower levels (P₁/₂ → S₁/₂ and D₃/₂ in Yb+, Ca+, Ba+ and Sr+) the level's total Γ is the sum of the partial rates and the fine-structure branching ratio is their ratio, a radial-integral quantity that the species module stores with its citation (0.501(15)% for 171Yb+ P₁/₂ → D₃/₂, Section 8.1). Everything below the fine-structure level is angular algebra and is computed. The hyperfine-resolved element factorizes by the Wigner-Eckart theorem (Steck's convention; the Rb87 notes' Eqs. 35-37),

⟨F m_F|d_q|F′ m_F′⟩ = ⟨J‖d‖J′⟩ (−1)^{F′+J+1+I} √((2F′+1)(2J+1)) {J J′ 1; F′ F I} · (−1)^{F′−1+m_F} √(2F+1) (F′ 1 F; m_F′ q −m_F),

with a 6j symbol in braces and a 3j symbol in parentheses, nonzero only for m_F = m_F′ + q. Two sum rules that the ingest test checks numerically, both printed by Steck (Rb87 data Eqs. 39-40) **[verified]**: Σ_{m′,q}|⟨J m|d_q|J′ m′⟩|² = |⟨J‖d‖J′⟩|² (the normalization convention itself) and Σ_{q,F}|⟨F (m_F′+q)|d_q|F′ m_F′⟩|² = (2J+1)/(2J′+1) |⟨J‖d‖J′⟩|², so the total decay rate out of any upper sublevel |F′ m_F′⟩ equals Γ_{J′→J} whatever its hyperfine quantum numbers, the relative strength S = (2F′+1)(2F+1)(2J′+1) (3j)² (6j)² is the branching ratio of that decay channel and sums to one over the lower manifold, and the total absorption strength out of any lower sublevel, summed over upper states and polarizations, equals |⟨J‖d‖J′⟩|². Steck's alternative reduced element (J‖d‖J′) = √(2J+1)⟨J‖d‖J′⟩ is the conversion the ingest test applies to sources that use it. Recomputed (Section 9.13): the 171Yb+ P₁/₂ F′ = 1 level decays 1/3 to S₁/₂ F = 0 and 2/3 to F = 1, and F′ = 0 decays only to F = 1, which is the branching that Section 8.1's leakage rate R_d and Section 4.2.6's optical pumping (1/3 per excitation into |0⟩) assumed; for I = 1/2 the stretched ground state's absorption strength to P₃/₂, summed over polarizations, lies half in the cycling |2,2⟩ component and entirely there for σ⁺ light, which is why that transition is closed.

At intermediate fields the |F m_F⟩ states of Section 4.5.1 are mixtures, and the module evaluates the element between the actual eigenvectors, Σ c^{(a)*}_{F m_F} c^{(b)}_{F′ m_F′} ⟨F m_F|d_q|F′ m_F′⟩, which is what makes the 43Ca+ shelving probabilities of Section 8.1 (ground states other than the stretch state shelving with up to about 30% probability at 146 G) computable rather than tabulated.

**Intensity, saturation and the Rabi frequency.** A beam of intensity I has field amplitude E₀ = √(2I/(ε₀c)) and polarization components ε_q in the spherical basis of Section 4.5.3; the Rabi frequency of the transition |a⟩ → |b⟩ that it drives, in the plan's convention H = (ħΩ/2)σ₊ + h.c. (Section 4.3.1), is

Ω_{ab} = E₀ Σ_q ε_q ⟨b|d_q|a⟩ / ħ.

The saturation intensity I_sat = πhcΓ/(3λ³) (Section 13) is a property of a two-level transition whose dipole element squared is 3πε₀ħc³Γ/ω³; for such a transition, and only for such a transition, Ω = Γ√(I/(2I_sat)) and the saturation parameter s = I/I_sat equals 2Ω²/Γ². Recomputed (Section 9.13): 171Yb+ 369.5 nm has |⟨J‖d‖J′⟩| = 1.751 e a₀ and I_sat = 50.77 mW/cm² (the 50.8 mW/cm² of Section 9.12); 40Ca+ 397 nm 2.045 e a₀ and 45.11 mW/cm²; 40Ca+ 393 nm 2.972 e a₀ and 50.25 mW/cm². Which transitions qualify is decided by the angular algebra, and the two readout schemes of Section 8.1 fall on opposite sides of the line. The stretched-state σ⁺ cycling transition |I+½, I+½⟩ → |I+3/2, I+3/2⟩ through P₃/₂ has relative strength exactly (2J+1)/(2J′+1) = 1/2 of |⟨J‖d‖J′⟩|², the same factor that appears in Γ_{P₃/₂→S₁/₂}, so Ω = Γ√(I/(2I_sat)) holds and Acton's s = I/I_sat is exact. The 171Yb+ S₁/₂ F = 1 → P₁/₂ F′ = 0 detection line has J = J′ and relative strength 1/3 per Zeeman component, so with the full-line Rabi frequency Ω = Γ√(I/(2I_sat)) each ground sublevel |1, m⟩ couples to |0, 0⟩ through one polarization component with |⟨0,0|d_q|1,m⟩|² = |⟨J‖d‖J′⟩|²/3, so its Rabi frequency is Ω_m² = |ε_q|²Ω²/3, and since Σ_q|ε_q|² = 1 for any single beam the three sublevels share Σ_m Ω_m² = Ω²/3 however the beam is polarized, the magic-angle choice |ε_q|² = 1/3 giving each Ω_m² = Ω²/9; in a rate model with the three ground states equally populated the excited fraction is Σ_m (1/3)(Ω_m²/Γ²)[1 + (2Δ/Γ)²]⁻¹ = (Ω²/9Γ²)[1 + (2Δ/Γ)²]⁻¹ = s_∘/18 at low intensity with s_∘ = 2Ω²/Γ² for the full-line Ω, which is the Γ/18 prefactor of Section 8.1 (the second revision wrote Ω/√3 per sublevel and s_∘/6, counting the hyperfine factor but not the polarization share; derivation audit of 2026-09-04), and the saturation ceiling of Γ/4 follows from four equally populated levels. Noek's prefactor is therefore derived, not fitted, his s = 2Ω²/Γ² in the (Γ/6, 2/3) form being s_∘/3, and the factor between Crain's quoted I/I_sat and the saturation inferred from his measured rate (Section 8.8) must be intensity calibration, detuning or polarization, all of which the level-C Bloch solve of Section 8.1 takes as inputs **[corrected: derivation audit, 2026-09-04]**. The code never accepts "I/I_sat" for a transition it has not classified as closed two-level.

#### 4.5.3 Polarization in the atomic frame

The quantization axis is the direction B̂ of the static field (Section 4.5.1). A beam with wavevector k̂ and complex unit polarization vector ε̂ in the laboratory frame is decomposed as ε̂ = Σ_q ε_q ê_q with ê_{±1} = ∓(x̂′ ± iŷ′)/√2 and ê₀ = ẑ′ = B̂, where x̂′ and ŷ′ complete a right-handed frame with B̂; ε_q = ê_q*·ε̂, and q = +1, 0, −1 drive σ⁺, π and σ⁻ absorption |F m_F⟩ → |F′ m_F + q⟩; the rotation of the laboratory Jones vector and k̂ into the B̂ frame is the module's own construction (no source in Run 4 supplies it, though every sign in the layer depends on it) and it asserts |ε₋₁|² + |ε₀|² + |ε₊₁|² = 1 at beam construction **[background]**. This q is the photon-helicity label. In Steck's matrix-element formulas (Section 13) the index on the operator component is q_op = m_F − m_F′ = m_lower − m_upper, because the lower state is the bra, so a σ⁺ absorption appears in his Eqs. 34-37, in his sublevel Rabi frequencies Ω(m_e, m_g) = ⟨F_g m_g|F_e m_e; 1 −(m_e − m_g)⟩Ω_{−(m_e−m_g)} and in his tables under q = −1: the two labels are opposite because d·ε̂ = Σ_q (−1)^q d_q ε_{−q}, so the field component ε_{+1} couples through d_{−1}. The code stores the helicity label, converts at the one place the Wigner-Eckart routine is called, and a test asserts that a pure ε_{+1} beam drives only m → m + 1 (the 2026-09-04 verifiers found the extracted Steck items with the two labels interchanged; Steck's Rb87 Eq. 57 for the polarization-resolved scattering branching also omits Γ as printed and returns a population rather than a rate) **[corrected]**. Since ε̂ ⊥ k̂, a beam along B̂ carries no π component, a linearly polarized beam perpendicular to B̂ with ε̂ ⊥ B̂ carries equal σ⁺ and σ⁻ components (|ε_{±1}|² = ½), and one with ε̂ ∥ B̂ is pure π. Every beam in `light/beams.py` stores k̂ and ε̂ in the laboratory frame and the atomic module returns its q components at each ion, so that the polarization purity on which Harty's ac Zeeman shift (Section 4.3.3), the optical-pumping efficiency (Section 4.2.6), the coherent-population-trapping factor of Section 8.1 and the polarization-gradient gate of Section 4.4.4 all depend is a computed number with a documented laboratory-frame origin, not a per-transition fudge factor. Polarization impurity is a device parameter (an ellipticity and a misalignment angle), sampled quasi-statically when a spectrum is given.

#### 4.5.4 Raman couplings and light shifts from the level structure

For two beams j = 1, 2 with frequencies ω_j, field amplitudes E_j and polarizations ε̂_j, and the set of intermediate levels |e⟩ (all hyperfine and Zeeman sublevels of the P₁/₂ and P₃/₂ manifolds at their field-dependent energies), each far detuned, Δ_e^{(j)} = ω_j − ω_{e,g} with |Δ_e| ≫ Ω, Γ_e, adiabatic elimination of the excited manifold gives, in the plan's convention (Section 13: Ω = −Ω₁Ω₂/(2Δ_R)), the effective two-photon Rabi frequency between ground states g₁ and g₂ and the light shift of each ground state:

Ω_{g₁g₂} = Σ_e Ω^{(2)*}_{e g₂} Ω^{(1)}_{e g₁} / (2Δ_e^{(1)}),  δ_g = Σ_j Σ_e |Ω^{(j)}_{e g}|² / (4Δ_e^{(j)}),

with Ω^{(j)}_{eg} = E_j ⟨e|d·ε̂_j|g⟩/ħ the single-photon Rabi frequencies of Section 4.5.2 and Δ_e = ω_L − ω_eg the plan's detuning sign (Section 13), so that red-detuned light lowers a ground level and the Raman coupling is the coherent sum over all intermediate sublevels with their signs; the second-order derivation gives these signs directly (E_g^{(2)} = |ħΩ/2|²/(E_g − E_e) = ħ|Ω|²/(4Δ)), and the minus sign in Wineland 2003's Eq. 2.3 and in the Section 13 row comes from that paper's definition Ω ≡ −E₀⟨↑|d·ε̂|↓⟩/(2ħ), a phase convention that the drive phase φ absorbs. Both are evaluated by explicit summation over the level structure of Section 4.5.1, and the closed forms of Sections 4.3.2 and 4.4.4 are their tests: Ozeri's clock-qubit Ω_R = (g_b g_r/3)(b₋r₋ − b₊r₊) ω_f/[Δ(Δ − ω_f)] is what the sum reduces to when the excited hyperfine splittings are neglected against Δ and the P₁/₂ and P₃/₂ paths are combined with their 6j weights (Ozeri's g_b and g_r are each beam's peak field times the fully m_F-resolved stretched cycling element |⟨P₃/₂, F = I + 3/2, m = F|d·σ̂₊|S₁/₂, F = I + ½, m = F⟩| over 2ħ, not a reduced matrix element, from which they differ by a Clebsch-Gordan factor of order 30% in Ω_R; and his τ_π = π/(2Ω_R) is a half-Rabi convention converted on ingest **[corrected]**) (the two paths enter with opposite signs, which is the origin of the ω_f/[Δ(Δ − ω_f)] structure and of the 1/Δ² fall-off); the 9Be+ eight-term differential shift of Wineland 2003 Eq. 2.11 and its polarization null; the vanishing differential vector shift of clock states that Baldwin's gate exploits. The decomposition into scalar, vector and tensor polarizabilities is an equivalent representation offered for reporting **[background]**, not a separate computation.

Two approximations enter here by default and are the only ones added to the optical rotating-wave approximation of Section 1.2, both reported with their size: the adiabatic elimination itself, whose residual excited-state population Σ_e |Ω^{(j)}_{eg}|²/(4Δ_e²) is exactly the quantity the scattering channels of Section 4.5.5 use, so the two are consistent by construction; and the counter-rotating two-photon pairing (beam 2 exciting g₁ and beam 1 de-exciting to g₂), which oscillates at about twice the qubit splitting and would need picosecond steps to integrate, and is instead replaced by its second-order frequency shift of order |Ω_{g₁g₂}|²/(2ω₀) (about 2π × 40 Hz for a 1 MHz Raman Rabi frequency on 171Yb+), which the calibration layer absorbs like any other static shift. A frequency-comb drive is the same computation over the set of tooth pairs, with the single-photon couplings replaced by the comb amplitudes g_k = g₀√(πν_rep τ) sech(2πk ν_rep τ) obeying Σ_k g_k² = g₀² and the two-photon Rabi frequency becoming the tooth-pair sum Ω^{(l)} = Σ_k g_k g_{k+l}/(2Δ) at beat-note separation l; the residual excited-state population is then the same sum over teeth, so the scattering channels of Section 4.5.5 stay consistent with the comb drive by construction (Section 4.3.7).

#### 4.5.5 Scattering amplitudes: Raman, Rayleigh and differential Rayleigh decoherence

Off-resonant light also scatters, through the same intermediate sum. The amplitude for scattering a photon of beam j from ground state a to ground state b with emitted polarization q′ is the Kramers-Heisenberg sum A^{(j)}_{a→b,q′} ∝ Σ_e ⟨b|d_{q′}|e⟩⟨e|d·ε̂_j|a⟩/Δ_e^{(j)}, and the rate is Γ_e times the far-detuned excited population times the branching, combined coherently over e: Γ^{(j)}_{a→b} = Σ_{q′} |Σ_e √Γ_e c_{e→b,q′} Ω^{(j)}_{ea}/(2Δ_e^{(j)})|², with c_{e→b,q′} the normalized decay amplitude of Section 4.5.2 (Σ_{b,q′}|c_{e→b,q′}|² = 1) and √Γ_e inside the coherent sum, one factor per intermediate path (strictly √(Γ_e(ω_j/ω_e)³), a 10⁻⁴ fine-structure correction), because the emission element ⟨b|d_{q′}|e⟩ = √(3πε₀ħc³Γ_e/ω_e³) c_{e→b,q′} carries it; the second revision placed Γ_e outside the sum, which is exact only when every intermediate level has the same total rate, true for 9Be+ under one radial integral and false for the species whose P₃/₂ branches to D levels, by up to 11× per channel (derivation audit of 2026-09-04) **[corrected: derivation audit, 2026-09-04]**. This is the general form of Ozeri 2005's Γ_{i,f} = g²γ|a^{(1/2)}_{i→f}/Δ + a^{(3/2)}_{i→f}/(Δ − Δ_f)|² (Section 4.3.2), and the tabulated 9Be+ amplitudes of Section 9.10 (Raman a^{(1/2)} = −√2/3, a^{(3/2)} = +√2/3; Rayleigh 2/3 and 1/3 for σ⁺ on m = −1/2, and so on) together with Ozeri 2007's P_total, ε_S and ε_D are its tests; the module produces the corresponding amplitudes for 43Ca+, 171Yb+, 133Ba+ and 88Sr+ from their level structures, which closes the "worked for 9Be+ only" gap of Section 12. Four things Run 4 fixed in Ozeri's paper as a source: the sentence after his Eq. 18 defines λ_{3/2} = c/ω_{3/2}, contradicting his Eq. 18, Table I and every Table II value, and taken literally inflates the required laser power by (2π)² = 39.5, so λ = 2πc/ω is used (his Table II reproduces to two figures for eight ions with it; the Yb+ row does not: it prints 0.2 × 10⁻⁴ and 2 mW where the paper's own Eq. 17 gives 0.29 × 10⁻⁴ and 2.9 mW, and it is excluded; the first revision of this plan had the printed and recomputed pairs transposed, caught by the errata check of 2026-09-04); his P_total has two equally deep minima, 2√2 πγ/ω_f at Δ = (√2 − 1)ω_f and at −(√2 + 1)ω_f, with P_∞ = 3πγ/ω_f, and P_min is not a gate-error floor because P_Raman falls as 1/Δ²; the Raman scattering rate falls as 1/Δ⁴ and the per-π-pulse probability as 1/Δ², two statements his text leaves implicit; his 5/12 recoil average is isotropic, the dipole-weighted value for fixed polarization being 9/20 (8% more, 27/25; the second revision wrote 16%), his D(β) = exp(βa − β*a†) is the conjugate of QuTiP's convention (harmless only because his β is imaginary), his ε_S is an upper bound (Eq. 11 discards a positive term) and his P_Rayleigh silently includes the D-level Raman channel for Ca+, Sr+, Ba+ and Yb+, overstating the elastic rate by fP_total **[corrected]**.

Three kinds of channel follow, all Lindblad operators on the joint space (Section 5.7):

- **Raman scattering** (b ≠ a): √Γ_{a→b} |b⟩⟨a| ⊗ R, with R the recoil operator of Section 4.2.8. When b is outside the qubit pair this is **leakage**, the channel that Section 12 recorded as absent from every source: for a hyperfine qubit the leaked population sits in the other Zeeman sublevels of the same or the other F manifold, and its fate at readout (bright or dark) follows from the manifold (Section 8.1; for 171Yb+ the F = 1 sublevels are bright and F = 0 is dark); for an optical qubit it sits in other D₅/₂ or S₁/₂ sublevels with their own readout polarity and lifetimes. Leakage is therefore simulated, not estimated, whenever d > 2, and its rate is the sum of the out-of-pair Raman rates otherwise.
- **Rayleigh scattering** (b = a) with **equal** amplitudes for the two qubit states, r_↑ = r_↓ for every emitted mode: the collapse operator is proportional to the identity on the spin and acts only on the motion through R, so it heats but does not decohere; this is the regime of Section 4.3.2's statement that Rayleigh scattering at large detuning leaves a hyperfine (clock) qubit's coherence intact.
- **Differential Rayleigh scattering**, r_↑ ≠ r_↓ (Uys et al. 2010): the operator r_↑|↑⟩⟨↑| + r_↓|↓⟩⟨↓| = ½(r_↑ + r_↓)𝟙 + ½(r_↑ − r_↓)σ_z has a σ_z part that dephases the qubit at a rate proportional to Σ_modes |r_↑ − r_↓|², an error absent from Ozeri 2007's Raman-only estimate. It vanishes for clock states far from the fine-structure levels, where the two amplitudes coincide by symmetry, and does not vanish for Zeeman qubits or at detunings comparable with the fine-structure splitting. The module computes r_↑ and r_↓ from the same amplitude sum, so the channel comes for free once the amplitudes exist. Uys et al. 2010 give the rate **[verified]** (Eqs. 4, 5 and 7 transcribed by Run 4 and re-read from the e-print by a three-verifier panel in Run 5, which confirmed them and sharpened four conventions; see the closing paragraph of this subsection): with Kramers-Heisenberg amplitudes A^{i→j}_{J,λ} = b_λ⟨j|d·ε̂*_{λ+(i−j)}|J, λ+i⟩⟨J, λ+i|d·ε̂_λ|i⟩/(δ_{iJλ} μ²) normalized to the largest dipole element μ of the manifold, so that the scattering rates are Γ_{ij} = Ω_R²γ Σ_λ (Σ_J A^{i→j}_{J,λ})² with Ω_R = μE₀/(2ħ) (a coherent sum over the intermediate fine-structure levels J inside the square, an incoherent sum over the driving polarization components λ outside it), the elastic decoherence rate is

Γ_el = Ω_R² γ Σ_λ (Σ_J A^{d→d}_{J,λ} − Σ_{J′} A^{u→u}_{J′,λ})²,

the square of the difference of the two states' elastic amplitudes, and it enters the master equation as 𝓛_el ρ = −(Γ_el/4)[ρ − σ_z ρ σ_z], so that the qubit coherence decays at (Γ_Ram + Γ_el)/2 while populations relax at the Raman rates alone **[verified]**. **The exact reduction to a driven, parametrically modulated oscillator.** For an ion of charge Q and mass m on the axis in a potential φ(q, t) whose minimum follows a prescribed transport function q₀(t), set φ_res = φ − φ_local(q − q₀) − φ₀(t) and a_res = −(Q/m) ∂φ_res/∂q. In u = q − q₀(t) the equation ü + ω₀²u = −q̈₀ + a_res(u + q₀) is exact; expanding a_res about q₀(t) and truncating at first order (the source assumes a_res^{(n)} = 0 for n ≥ 2 throughout) gives **[verified]** (Reichle et al. 2006, Eqs. 2-7)

ℋ(t) = p²/2m + (m ω²(t)/2) u² − m f(t) u,  ω²(t) = ω₀²(1 − a′_res[q₀(t)]/ω₀²),  f(t) = −q̈₀(t) + a_res[q₀(t)],

with p = mu̇ the *moving-frame* momentum and f an acceleration. Eq. (7) follows from the lab Lagrangian only after discarding d/dt(m q̇₀ u), which converts the inertial cross term into −m q̈₀ u, so expectation values of p are moving-frame and the coherent amplitude is built in that gauge. Second-quantizing with u = √(ħ/2mω₀)(a + a†) at fixed ω₀ gives H/ħ = ω₀(a†a + ½) + [(ω²(t) − ω₀²)/(4ω₀)](a + a†)² − f(t)√(m/(2ħω₀))(a + a†): the (a + a†)² term is the squeezing channel, vanishing identically only at constant curvature, and the linear term alone makes the propagator a pure displacement **[verified]**. Adiabaticity is χ = d̈_CP/(d_CP ω_CP²), χ < 1 adiabatic, with d̈_CP = (4/25)(4/(β⁷κ³))^{1/5} α̇_CP² for the relative coordinate; the printed 2/25 is short by a factor 2, and 4/25 reproduces the printed χ to one part in 10¹⁵ **[corrected]**, **[recomputed here]**. The incoherent floor is the Section 4.1.5 rate Γ_h(ω) = e² S_E(ω)/(4mħω) integrated as Δn̄_th = ∫₀^T Γ_h(ω(t)) dt (Kaufmann Eq. 32, identical to Blakestad's Eq. 18, so one S_E model drives the splitting, transport and junction channels); because both ramps depend only on t/T at fixed endpoints, ω(t) is a function of t/T alone and the integral is *strictly proportional to T*, so the source's clause that "the anomalous heating contribution is scaling as 1/T" contradicts its own equation and figure and must not be implemented **[corrected]**. **Junctions: the pseudopotential barrier and the sum rule that must be switched off.** The only junction in the source set is one two-wafer three-dimensional X design with rf bridges, so this layer is single-apparatus. The total potential is Φ(r) = φ_ps(r) + Σ_n V_n φ̃_n(r) with φ_ps = q(V_rf ∇φ̃_rf)²/(4mΩ_rf²), *also a potential in volts*, the ion's energy being qΦ and the denominator 4 rather than 2 the *peak* V_rf convention **[verified]** (Blakestad et al. 2011, Eqs. 1-2). φ_ps ≥ 0 identically and is *not* a Laplace solution, so the Hessian of the total Φ is not traceless - only the φ̃_n basis is - while linearity in V_n makes the constrained least-squares waveform solve possible with φ_ps a fixed additive term **[verified]**. The four barriers, one per entrance, are about 0.3 eV for 9Be+ at V_rf ≈ 200 V peak and Ω_rf ≈ 2π × 83 MHz, which fixes |∇φ̃_rf| = 873 m⁻¹, and rescale as 1/m at fixed charge, giving 68 meV for 40Ca+ and 16 meV for 171Yb+; the paper's own boundary-element pseudopotential needed a 1.4× scaling to match its measured rf-noise heating, so that factor is an explicit fit parameter **[verified]**, **[recomputed here]**. Everywhere ω_i² = ω̃_i² + ω_rf,i² with Σ_i ω̃_i² = 0, ω̃_i the control contribution (imaginary means anti-trapping) **[verified]** (Eq. 13); but ω_x² + ω_y² + ω_z² = 2ω_rf² holds only where ω_rf,z = 0, that is in the straight channels, because inside the junction the pseudopotential has axial curvature - *anti*-confining at the apex, confining at the centre - so the solver asserts that sum rule outside and disables it within **[corrected]**. At the junction centre all control voltages are zero and the pseudopotential alone traps; channel equivalence h_x = h_z plus a traceless rf Hessian forces h_y = −2h_x, and with ω_rf,i ∝ |h_i| this predicts ω_y = 2ω_x, 11.4 MHz against the reported 11.3 MHz at ω_x/2π = 5.7 MHz **[background]**, **[recomputed here]**. That x-z degeneracy leaves the principal axes ill-defined and can rotate a two-ion crystal uncontrollably; the reported data lifted it with a shim giving |ω′_x − ω′_z| > 2π × 400 kHz, so reproducing exact degeneracy will not match the measured excitation, the alternative being a dwell tuned to a full mode-mixing cycle **[verified]**.

**Junctions: residual micromotion and the two technical excitation channels.** Residual axial rf field gives axial micromotion z₁^peak = qE₀/(mΩ_rf²) = 2√(qφ_ps/(mΩ_rf²)) with z₁^rms = z₁^peak/√2. The first technical channel is rf-drive sideband noise on a pseudopotential slope, ṅ_z = q⁴/(16 m³ Ω_rf⁴ ħ ω_z)[∂(E₀²)/∂z]²[S_{V_N}(Ω_rf + ω_z) + S_{V_N}(Ω_rf − ω_z)]/V_rf², one-sided S_{V_N} in the same convention as Eq. (18), both sidebands summed separately and the normalization fractional **[verified]** (Eqs. 15-16). The rate goes as the *square* of the pseudopotential slope - the prose says "proportional to the slope" while the equation has it squared - and it is one of two mechanisms, neither dominant nor intrinsic: the only apportionment puts rf noise at 0.1 to 0.5 quanta per barrier pass, 3 to 30% of the observed excitation, the remainder attributed primarily to DAC update noise, while anomalous surface heating, the genuinely intrinsic channel, is insignificant here **[corrected]**. **Schedule, wall clock and the circuit-level error model.** A QCCD schedule charges transport as 1.10 Σ_p N_p τ_p, the factor being the stated interpolation-and-electronics overhead, with per-primitive durations 57.8 µs (intrazone shift), 282.6 µs (interzone shift), 128.2 µs (split/combine) and 200.0 µs (swap) **[verified]** (Pino et al. 2021, Tables II-III). The tabulated per-primitive "axial heat" and "radial heat" columns are *temperatures expressed as frequencies*, k_B T/h in MHz, not quanta; convert with the full Bose-Einstein expression n̄ = [exp(ν/ν_T) − 1]⁻¹, so the ν_T = 2 MHz swap row gives n̄ = 1.6021 at ν = 0.97 MHz (T = 96.0 µK), while dividing the tabulated value by the mode frequency is the Rayleigh-Jeans shortcut and returns approximately n̄ + ½ because k_B T/(hν) is only 1.4 to 2.1 here **[corrected]**, **[recomputed here]**. **Readout forward models: how the sources measure the excitation this module predicts.** The resolved-sideband lineshape is r_m(Δ_m) = Σ_{n≥m} ρ_nn [4|g_nm|²/Ω_nm²] sin²(Ω_nm t_m/2) with Ω_nm = √(4|g_nm|² + Δ_m²), built from g_m = (−iη)^m g₀/m! and g_nm = g_m √(n!/(n − m)!), so g_n1 = −iηg₀√n and g_n2 = −½η²g₀√(n(n−1)) **[verified]** (Sterk et al. 2022, Eq. 2 and Methods). g₀ is *not* the carrier Rabi frequency: in this sin²(Ωt/2) convention g₀ = Ω_carrier/2 and the sideband π-time is π/(2|g_nm|), so reading g₀ as a Rabi frequency is a factor 2 in every probe time, and the sum starting at n = m makes r_m vanish identically for a pure ground state **[corrected]**. Because r_m depends only on the post-transport Fock populations it is blind to motional coherences and hence to motional phase, but *not* to the diagonal's shape: the claim that it cannot separate coherent from thermal excitation at equal mean quanta is wrong, since the couplings are n-dependent and the second-order sideband weights ⟨n(n−1)⟩ = n̄² (Poissonian) against 2n̄² (Bose-Einstein) **[corrected]**. Two pseudo-energy metrics serve as optimizer objectives, neither in quanta and neither monotonic in mean occupation. The closed-loop loss ℒ(X) = max_h [α₁ ∫_{B₁} (dΔ₁/2π) r₁ + (α₂ ∫_{B₂} (dΔ₂/2π) r₂)²] has α₁ = α₂ = 2 kHz⁻¹ with the second weight *inside* the square, which keeps the term dimensionless and weights the second moment more heavily; it is a worst case over hold offsets, never a mean, and is not invertible above its fold (10² to 10³ quanta at short probe times, 10 to 10² at long ones), because once |g_nm| t_m exceeds π the sin² factor stops growing - "monotonically" is an extraction artefact **[corrected]**.

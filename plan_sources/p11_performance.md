### 11.2 Cost model

Wall time is the number of right-hand-side evaluations times the cost of one application of H(t) to the state:

- **Evaluations.** Counted directly in v4 through the coefficient calls: 1.9 × 10⁴ (dimension 48), 2.1 × 10⁴ (256), 2.4 × 10⁴ (864) and 3.5 to 3.8 × 10⁴ (2048) right-hand-side evaluations per 20 µs with `dop853`, 1.2× more with `vern9`, that is 27 to 52 steps per period of the 3 MHz mode at 12 evaluations per step, growing with the cap as Section 5.2's 14 to 45 anticipated; a 100 µs pulse needs 1 to 2 × 10⁵ evaluations and a 10 µs single-qubit pulse 1 to 2 × 10⁴ (the second revision's 2 × 10⁴ per 20 µs came from a one-ion `solve_ivp` run in `bench_numerics.py` and was 1.8× low at 2048; 2026-09-04 numerics critique) **[corrected: critique, 2026-09-04]**.
- **Cost per evaluation.** Dominated by the drive operator: σ₊^i ⊗ Π_m D_m has 2^{N−1} Π_m d_m² non-zeros per ion (Section 5.1.1), the merged operator Σ_i(σ₊^i D_i + h.c.) has N 2^N Π_m d_m², a fraction N/2^N of the (2^N Π_m d_m)² matrix (one half for two ions, 3/8 for three, 1/4 for four), and the tones share it through the coefficient. Measured constants (v4): a CSR product costs 0.5 to 0.6 ns per non-zero at every size from 256 to 2048, memory-bound at about 35 GB/s (20 bytes per non-zero), so the 2.1 × 10⁶ non-zeros at dimension 2048 cost 1.2 ms per evaluation; the dense product costs 0.16 ns per element at 2048 (67 MB, beyond cache, about 100 GB/s through the vendor BLAS) and 0.07 to 0.09 ns while the matrix fits in cache, so the same operator costs 0.65 ms per evaluation dense, and four unmerged dense terms 3.0 ms, which is where v3's 114 s came from. A 100 µs gate at dimension 2048 is therefore about 1.2 × 10⁵ × 0.65 ms ≈ 80 s merged dense (2.1 min measured), 2.5 min merged CSR and 10 min four-term dense; the second revision's '3 to 4 ns per non-zero' divided a four-term dense time by a CSR non-zero count and an evaluation count from another script (2026-09-04 numerics critique) **[corrected: critique, 2026-09-04]**.
- **Scaling.** The cost is evaluations × elements touched per evaluation, dense D² = 4^N Π_m d_m² or CSR N 2^N Π_m d_m², so doubling d_m on three modes multiplies either by 64 and the evaluation count by about 1.4 (2.4 → 3.5 × 10⁴ from d_m = 6 to 8), adding a fourth mode at d_m = 8 multiplies by 64, and adding an ion multiplies the dense cost by 4 and the CSR cost by 2(N + 1)/N. Fitted to the four v4 rows, cost = a + b × (elements per evaluation) × evaluations with a ≈ 0.02 s and b = 0.16 ns (dense, beyond cache) or 0.58 ns (CSR) predicts every measured row within about 20% once the in-cache rows use their faster constant (the second revision's single-point rule mispredicted adjacent rows by up to 22×, the small rows being coefficient-call bound, four Python calls per evaluation being most of the 0.03 s at dimension 48). Four ions with three modes at d_m = 8 (dimension 8192; dense 6.7 × 10⁷ elements, 1.1 GB; CSR 1.7 × 10⁷ non-zeros, not the 3 × 10⁷ of the second revision) cost about 10 ms per evaluation either way and about 25 to 30 minutes per 100 µs gate at 1.5 × 10⁵ evaluations, the factorized kernel of Section 11.3 (24 against 512 amplitudes per mode product) being what brings that back to minutes **[corrected: critique, 2026-09-04]**; and eight ions with eight axial modes at N_exc = 6 have a drive operator of 1.15 × 10⁹ non-zeros (18.5 GB per ion, measured), which is why the reductions below are not optional and why the JOINT_EXACT range of Section 5.4 is stated as a dimension budget.

### 11.3 Reduction techniques, in the order they are applied

1. **ENR truncation** (option) for a group of cold, undriven modes carried dynamically: C(M + N_exc, N_exc) states instead of Π_m d_m, with the exponential of the ENR sum generator as the operator (Section 5.1.1); an ENR group is not a product space, so item 4 does not apply to it and the two are never composed on the same modes.
2. **Mode selection per pulse**: a mode leaves a pulse's resolved set when its closed-form residual displacement and entangling-angle contribution from the Section 4.4.3 integrals, evaluated with the pulse's actual detuning schedule, are both negligible (defaults |α_m(τ)|²(2n̄_m + 1) < 10⁻⁶ and |χ_m(τ)| < 10⁻⁴), not on η alone, because a mode with η = 10⁻³ two kilohertz from a tone contributes more than one with η = 0.05 a megahertz away; a mode above this pair but with |χ_m| below the freeze tolerance of Section 5.2 (default 0.05 rad) is frozen rather than dropped, the third class; dropped modes evolve freely (their heating still applies) and the summed dropped contribution is reported.
3. **Frozen spectators** (Section 5.2): modes far from every tone are removed from the joint space and enter through per-shot Debye-Waller factors, with the off-resonant excitation bound reported.
4. **Mode-factorized application of the drive operator** (product spaces only). Because Π_m D_m is a tensor product, applying it to a state costs Σ_m d_m per amplitude when done mode by mode (three reshaped dense matrix products) instead of Π_m d_m when done as one assembled matrix: 24 against 512 for three modes at d_m = 8, a twentyfold reduction at the largest measured case and more beyond it. This needs a matrix-free right-hand side, either scipy's `solve_ivp` on a `LinearOperator` or a custom QuTiP `Data` type, and is the main engineering item of milestone M9; the table of Section 11.1 is its acceptance test.
5. **Cached operators**: the σ₊ ⊗ D_i matrices (or their factors) per (ion, Δk, space) are built once; pulse propagators are reused only on internal-state-only spaces (Section 5.3), never on the joint space. On such a space the cached propagator is the step's channel and the GATE_LOCAL tomography reads its Kraus operator off it (`JointExactEngine.propagator`, one call per motional branch); on a joint space the tomography propagates the prod_i d_i internal basis columns of the step (4800 × 4 for a pair at dimension 4800), never the D × D propagator, so the memory reason behind this item is respected (performance pass 2026-09-09, Section 5.4). One engine serves a whole GATE_LOCAL walk, so its propagator cache outlives the step that filled it, and the one-ion idle channels are cached by (ion, duration, engine setup, sample, options), the absolute window joining the key only when the sample carries a fast trajectory.
6. **Interaction frame for slow pulses** (optional): for microwave or co-propagating-Raman single-qubit gates with negligible motional coupling, the motion factors out and the pulse is evolved on the internal space alone with the exact Debye-Waller reduction folded into Ω.
7. **Sideband-truncated interaction picture** (Section 5.2), when the cap is large and few sidebands matter.
8. **GATE_LOCAL** (Section 5.4) above the joint-exact range.
9. **Trajectory count adaptivity**: `mcsolve` `target_tol` on the observables of interest, so easy circuits use fewer trajectories; trajectories and quasi-static samples are spread over the 18 cores with the parallel `map`, which requires every `QobjEvo` coefficient to be a module-level named function, a `Coefficient` or an array (a lambda or closure raises `PicklingError` under `map="parallel"` on 5.3.1, and only `map="loky"`, an optional dependency, survives it through cloudpickle), so the builder never emits closures and a CI test runs `mcsolve` with `map="parallel"` on a `QobjEvo` built by the real builder (2026-09-04 numerics critique) **[corrected: critique, 2026-09-04]**.

10. **Exact rotating frame** (performance pass 2026-09-09; `dynamics/rotating.py`, `SolverOptions.rotating_frame`): every ket segment integrates i φ' = Θ†V(t)Θ φ with Θ = e^{−iH₀t} the diagonal H_mot + H_int (+ a constant Stark shift), the phases applied to the state around the unchanged drive operators of items 4 and 5, collapse operators that are eigenoperators of ad_{H₀} carrying e^{iλt}, expectation values, stored states and final states phased back; a segment with a collapse operator that is not an eigenoperator (a recoil kick, the intensity-noise channel) keeps the Schrödinger picture, because `mcsolve` forms c†c from the operators it is given and a rotated non-eigenoperator would make every such product time dependent. It is not a frame of Section 5.2's builder option (nothing is expanded or truncated; the builder's H(t) is unchanged) but a change of integration variable the engine makes and undoes, and the results equal the Schrödinger-picture integration to the solver tolerance (1.4 × 10⁻⁷ to 5.8 × 10⁻⁷ in norm on the four Section 11.1 rows, the dop853-vern9 figure of Section 11.1) with 3.2, 4.1, 5.0 and 7.7 times fewer right-hand-side evaluations on those rows (the state moves at the drive and detuning frequencies, not at the Fock energies) and 6.7 instead of 43 steps per period of the 3 MHz mode on the real 100 µs pulse, which runs in 1.0 s instead of 5.1 s at dimension 540 (`bench_rotating.py`) **[recomputed here]**. `mesolve` segments form the Liouvillian from the Schrödinger-picture matrices as before, and the step-density band of Section 5.3 is a Schrödinger-picture statement checked with the frame off.

### 11.4 Interactive budgets for the application

The zoom ladder of Section 14 needs three responses to feel continuous, and the measured table fixes what is achievable: re-simulating one trajectory of a zoomed 100 µs entangling pulse with its recorded noise sample in under a second is possible at joint dimensions up to about 256 (two modes at d_m = 8: 0.6 s merged dense) and in a few seconds up to about 864 (three modes at d_m = 6: 6 s); above that (2 minutes at 2048), and for anything that needs an ensemble (a representative jump record, a process matrix from 16 tomography inputs), the record's stored traces are shown at once and the computation runs as a background job with progress, its view filling in when it finishes. Recalibration by simulated experiment (Section 7.5) is hours of pulse integrations at these rates and is a user-initiated background job that invalidates the calibration table and leaves a stale badge until it completes; only the analytic re-derivation (modes, η, pulse-solver solutions and closed-form channel estimates) is immediate. App-side channel-replay runs of 20-qubit, 100-gate circuits take seconds, and the re-derivation of modes and Lamb-Dicke parameters after a device-parameter change is immediate, with pulse re-solves of at most about 10 s per pair in the background behind stale badges. These budgets are tested in M11 alongside the physics budgets above, and the factorized kernel of Section 11.3 is what would move the one-second boundary to three modes. With the exact rotating frame of item 10 (2026-09-09) the measured 100 µs pulse costs 0.7, 0.8, 1.2 and 1.9 s at dimensions 48, 256, 864 and 2048 (five times the 20 µs integrations of `bench_rotating.py`), so the one-second boundary now sits between the two-mode and the three-mode rows and the 2048 row that took two minutes takes two seconds **[recomputed here]**.

### 11.5 Memory

State vectors are dense complex128; at dimension 10⁶ a state is 16 MB and a trajectory ensemble is stored only as final states or sampled observables. An assembled drive operator at dimension 2048 with 2.1 × 10⁶ non-zeros is about 40 MB in CSR form (20 bytes per non-zero) and 67 MB dense, and grows as Π_m n_max,m²; with the factorized kernel only the per-mode D_m (n_max,m² each) are stored. The monitor refuses to build joint spaces above a configurable dimension (default 4096, from the measured cost model of Section 11.2) or above a configurable estimate of the drive operator's non-zeros (default 2 × 10⁷, from Π_m d_m² and the ion count), and directs the run to GATE_LOCAL; the second revision's default of 4 × 10⁶ was 2000 times the budget Section 5.4 states and could not have been honoured (2026-09-04 architect critique) **[corrected: critique, 2026-09-04]**.

## Appendix D. How the physics in this plan was checked

Three multi-agent research runs on 2026-09-03 produced the provenance tags used throughout.

**Run 1** (wf_55100f76-ac4, 111 agents): five web-search angles, 29 primary sources fetched, 145 falsifiable claims extracted with quotes, the top 25 put to a three-voter adversarial panel that fetched the primary PDFs (a claim died on two refuting votes). Result: 15 claims survived, 10 were refuted, mostly on single details (a wrong isotope, an inverted inequality, a dropped digit, a mislabelled mechanism, a superseded record). Those 15 are the **[verified]** facts in Sections 1, 4.1 and 8 that carry vote counts in the companion report "Paul Trap to Fault Tolerance".

**Run 2A** (wf_4eccb03e-072, 295 agents, 23.5 M subagent tokens, 6.9 h) and **Run 2B** (wf_5d918595-430, 398 agents, 32.2 M subagent tokens, 7.5 h): 59 source readings across six topics (trap and motion 8, cooling 8, readout 9, Hamiltonian and single-qubit control 9, two-qubit gates 15, system level 10), chosen by the author rather than found by search, read through ar5iv LaTeX renderings or the PDFs so that equations were transcribed rather than paraphrased. Each source yielded up to six items (equation, definition, parameter, procedure, result or check) with a verbatim quote and location. Every non-tangential item, 354 in total, went to a fidelity verifier instructed to refute on any transcription, convention, condition or numerical error; a refutation or a low-confidence pass escalated to two more verifiers and a majority decision. Outcome by topic at the time of writing:

| Topic | Items | Passed (with flags) | Refuted as stated, corrected | No verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trap, crystal, modes, micromotion, heating | 48 | 34 | 14 | 0 (13 re-run in Run 3) |
| Laser cooling and state preparation | 48 | 32 | 16 | 0 |
| Readout and SPAM | 54 | 27 | 27 | 0 |
| Spin-motion Hamiltonian and single-qubit control | 54 | 40 | 14 | 0 |

**How the tags map.** **[verified]** in Part II means the item passed its fidelity check (one confident pass or a majority of three) and the verifier's flags were folded into the text. **[corrected]** means a majority refuted the item as extracted and the plan states the corrected form; in roughly two thirds of these cases the error was the source's own (a printed typo, a dropped factor, an inconsistent equation), which is precisely why the plan quotes corrected forms with the correction named. **[extracted]** means the item has a verbatim quote but no agent verdict. The 27 items whose verifiers stalled in the original runs were re-run in Run 3: 19 passed with flags and 8 were refuted on a gloss or symbol definition while their equations stood, so no tag now rests on a stalled verification; the tag remains only on a handful of Run 1 statements that were extracted but never put to the vote (a waveguide-crosstalk figure, one budget line from Pino et al., optical-pumping details, a 32-ion mode-frequency pair). The user had confirmed the 27 items independently before Run 3; the agent verdicts agree with that confirmation on every equation and add corrections to seven surrounding statements. **[background]** is the author's textbook physics, added for coherence and never load-bearing without a test.

**What the checking cannot give.** The verifiers established fidelity to the sources and internal consistency, and in many cases recomputed numbers, but they did not re-derive the physics from scratch; a source that is consistently wrong would pass. The convention table of Section 13 is the author's reconciliation across sources, informed by the verifiers' flags but not itself agent-verified. Run 2A's per-topic synthesis stage produced the readout brief but not the trap or cooling briefs: those two synthesis agents exceeded the 64,000-token output cap and were lost, so Run 2A's consolidation ran on the readout brief alone (its findings are folded into Sections 8 and 12). Run 2B produced the Hamiltonian and system-level briefs; its two-qubit brief was lost the same way. Its consolidation, run on those two briefs, listed 47 cross-source inconsistencies and independently recomputed the eight that change a unitary or an error estimate; those results are folded into Sections 6, 7, 9, 12 and 13 with the tag [corrected]. Run 3 recovered the three missing briefs.

**Run 3** (wf_489f7283-103, 65 agents, 6.5 M subagent tokens, 8.2 h, 2026-09-04) recovered what the output cap and the stalled verifiers had cost. The transcripts held no partial output from the three brief agents, only the API error, but they held every input: the 27 stalled checks were re-run from their original prompts with the same escalation (all 8 refutations were unanimous three-vote decisions, each on a gloss or a symbol definition while the transcribed equations stood: the R′ relation, Brownnutt's misprinted Eq. 14 and the Eq. 24 rate, a misattributed α example, Landa's two separate pseudopotential-recovery conditions, Baldwin's clock-qubit encoding in three items and the meaning of Δ_hf), and the three lost briefs were synthesized from the recorded item-plus-verdict inputs in 5, 5 and 8 parts of at most eleven items each, with one assembly agent per topic (trap: 70 model entries, 86 parameters, 97 checks; cooling: 68, 109, 90; two-qubit: 109, 188, 166). One consolidation agent then read all six briefs and both earlier consolidations and produced 20 cross-topic inconsistencies, 17 glossary decisions, 14 components, 22 tests and 20 open issues, folded into Sections 4, 6, 8, 9, 12 and 13. The recovered briefs and the consolidation are stored beside the plan as brief_recovered_<topic>.json and consolidated_C.json. The numerical checks marked "recomputed here" in Sections 7 and 13 were run by the author in the QuTiP environment on 2026-09-03 (Run 2B's unitary checks) and 2026-09-04 (everything else), not by workflow agents.

**Refutation rate as a design input.** Across all three runs about a third of carefully extracted, quoted claims failed a fidelity check, and a substantial fraction of those failures were errors in the published sources themselves. That is the empirical case for Section 9: a simulator built by transcribing formulas would inherit those errors, so the validation suite pins every implemented formula to a checked number.

**Local numerical checks (2026-09-04, this machine, QuTiP 5.3.1, NumPy 2.5.2, SciPy 1.18.1, run under both Python 3.14 and the pinned 3.13.14 with `uv`, with identical outputs and wall times within 10%).** Six scripts, committed to the repository under `validation/scripts/` as the oracle for every number tagged **[recomputed here]**, produced those numbers (`check_recoil2.py` recomputes the Run 4R checks: quadrature identities, the single-kick expectation, the α → 0 floor, the Doppler optimum, Cirac's Fig. 8 minima, the Franck-Condon index, the 40Ca+ Lamb-Dicke parameters and the unit regressions): `bench_numerics.py` (displacement-operator constructions and their unitarity and interior accuracy, ENR construction, right-hand-side evaluation counts in the Schrödinger and interaction pictures; Sections 5.1.1 and 5.2), `bench_ms_timing.py` (wall time of a two-ion Mølmer-Sørensen pulse at joint dimensions 48 to 2048; Section 11.1), `check_atomic.py` (Breit-Rabi against numerical diagonalization, clock points of 43Ca+, 9Be+ and 25Mg+, the 171Yb+ quadratic Zeeman coefficient, Wigner-Eckart branching ratios, Γ to reduced element to I_sat; Sections 4.5 and 9.13) `check_surface_mixed.py` (Laplace residual and boundary values of the gapless-plane electrode potential, the five-wire null height, the mixed-species two-ion mode formula; Sections 4.1.6 and 4.1.7) and `check_recoil.py` (the recoil angular factors 1/5, 2/5, 2/5, 3/10 and 1/3 by quadrature over the dipole emission patterns, and the 171Yb+ recoil-per-photon numbers; Section 4.2.8). The QuTiP interface facts of Section 9.13 were introspected in the same environment. These checks establish internal consistency and agreement with published anchors that earlier runs had verified; they do not replace the source checks, which Run 4 supplies for the transcribed formulas. The third revision (2026-09-04, afternoon and evening) added nine scripts to the same directory, each described in its README and each with its output committed under `outputs/`: `check_ms_closure.py` and `check_c0_floquet.py` (the two derivation-audit corrections the author re-derived independently, Sections 4.4.1 and 4.1.1), `bench_ms_timing_v3.py` (Section 11.1 at the corrected closure), `check_sr_lifetime_quad.py` and `check_sr_lifetime_bbr.py` (Section 4.5.7), and the four topic scripts of Run 5S, `check_comb.py`, `check_composite.py`, `check_constants.py` and `check_transport.py` (Sections 4.3.7, 4.3.5 and 6.9, 8.1 and 4.2.4, 4.6), whose numbers fill Section 9.15; every **[recomputed here]** number added in this revision names its script.

## 2. The physical process the simulator reproduces

This section is the narrative version of the pipeline; Part II gives the equations. Read it as the sequence of physical states the simulated device passes through for one shot of one circuit.

**Before the circuit.** N ions of one species sit in a linear Paul trap. Their equilibrium positions are set by the balance of the axial confining potential and mutual Coulomb repulsion; their small oscillations about those positions decompose into 3N normal modes, N along each axis, each a harmonic oscillator with its own frequency and pattern of ion participation. The modes are the bus every entangling gate uses, so the simulator computes them from the trap parameters rather than taking them as free inputs, and it records how far each ion participates in each mode (that participation, times the photon-recoil scale of the drive, is the ion's Lamb-Dicke parameter for that mode).

**Cooling.** Doppler cooling on the cycling transition brings every mode to a thermal state whose mean phonon number is set by the transition linewidth and the mode frequency. Resolved-sideband or EIT cooling then brings the modes used by gates close to the ground state; the simulator produces per-mode thermal states with mean occupations derived from the cooling model and parameters, and, where requested, simulates the cooling dynamics themselves as a check. Optical pumping puts every ion in the qubit's |0⟩ with a small, species-specific residual population elsewhere. This is the initial state of the run: internal states nearly pure, motional modes thermal, no correlations.

**Compilation and calibration.** The user's circuit is rewritten into the native gate set: single-qubit rotations about equatorial axes (GPi, GPi2 with a phase), the two-qubit entangling gate (MS with two phases and an angle, or ZZ), and Z rotations that cost nothing because they are absorbed into the phases of later pulses. Each native gate becomes a pulse whose parameters (Rabi frequency envelope, detuning, phase, duration, which modes it closes loops on) are not typed in by the user but come from calibration tables the simulator built by running the same experiments a laboratory runs on the simulated device: Rabi flopping to find the π time, sideband spectroscopy to find mode frequencies, gate scans to find the amplitude and detuning that produce maximal entanglement, and phase scans to align the entangling axis with the single-qubit frame.

**Gates.** Each pulse illuminates chosen ions (with a small, unavoidable fraction of light on their neighbours) and adds a drive term to the Hamiltonian: the ion's internal transition coupled to the motion through the exact displacement operator. A single-qubit pulse on resonance with the carrier rotates the spin while the motion, because the beams are chosen co-propagating or the drive is a microwave, barely notices. A Mølmer-Sørensen pulse drives both sidebands slightly off resonance; the resulting spin-dependent force pushes the driven mode around a closed loop in phase space so that, when the loop closes, the motion is back where it started and the two spins have acquired a joint geometric phase. Throughout, the mode heats at a rate set by electric-field noise, spins dephase from magnetic-field and laser noise, and the drive scatters photons off resonance at a rate set by the detuning and power. The simulator evolves the joint state under all of it, pulse by pulse, including the idle intervals between pulses.

**Readout.** A detection beam on the cycling transition makes an ion in the bright state scatter photons at a rate set by saturation and detuning, and an ion in the dark state scatter almost none, except that off-resonant excitation slowly pumps dark to bright and bright to dark. A fraction of the photons reach a detector; some background arrives too; some of one ion's light lands on its neighbour's detector. The simulator samples the count record for each ion from this model, applies the same discriminator the device would, and returns bits. For optical qubits and shelved hyperfine qubits the dark state is a metastable level whose decay during the window is another error channel.

**Results.** One shot is one sample of the whole chain. Shots aggregate into counts and probabilities. The results object also carries the diagnostics that make the run auditable: truncation-boundary populations, which modes were included, the fidelity level, the calibration values used, and, when asked, the final density matrix or trajectory states.

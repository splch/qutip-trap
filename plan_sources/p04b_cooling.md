### 4.2 Laser cooling and state preparation

The preparation module produces the initial motional and internal states of a run. It offers three levels of description for every cooling stage, all sharing one parameter set: (A) closed-form rate equations that return a steady-state mean phonon number and cooling rate per mode, used by default because they are what the sources validate against, each mode's rate carrying the participation weight W_k = Σ_{j illuminated} η_{j,k}² of Section 4.1.7 and the beam projection cos²θ_L of Section 4.2.8 rather than a single-ion value, and raising rather than returning a steady state when a mode's projection on every cooling beam is below threshold, because such a mode is not cooled at all (added after the 2026-09-04 experimentalist critique); (B) the Lamb-Dicke birth-death rate equation on Fock populations, used when the time dependence or non-thermal distributions matter; (C) the full master equation of the internal levels plus one mode, solved with `mesolve` or `mcsolve`, used to validate (A) and (B) and to reproduce the multi-level effects the closed forms miss. The output of every stage is a per-mode thermal density matrix (or the Fock distribution from level B) and an internal state with a preparation error, handed to the next stage.

#### 4.2.1 Doppler cooling

Semiclassical picture (RMP 2003 Eqs. 96-107) **[verified]**: an ion in the pseudopotential ½mν²x² with velocity v(t) = v₀cos(νt) feels the average radiation force F_a = ħkΓρ_ee with the saturated Lorentzian excited-state population ρ_ee = (s/2)/[1 + s + (2δ_eff/Γ)²], δ_eff = Δ − k·v. Linearizing in v gives F_a ≈ F₀(1 + κv): a constant push F₀ = ħkΓ(s/2)/[1 + s + (2Δ/Γ)²] that displaces the ion, and a friction term that cools only for Δ < 0. The balance of friction against recoil heating gives the Doppler limit, minimized at Δ = −(Γ/2)√(1 + s) **[verified]** (in the review's Eq. 105 the factor (1 + s) sits inside the bracket multiplying only Γ/(2Δ), and in Eq. 106 the radical covers 1 + s alone; both were verified symbol by symbol). The RMP's Eq. 106 with its own angular factor ξ = 2/5 (the recoil-pattern average of Section 4.2.8) gives k_B T_min = 0.35ħΓ, a factor 1.43 below the textbook ħΓ/2 that Berkeland uses; at Γ/2π = 20 MHz that is 0.336 mK against 0.480 mK, and the Bose occupation of a 1 MHz mode 6.5 against 9.5, so the two limits are not interchangeable and neither closed form is used as the Doppler stage **[corrected]**. The force model itself is valid only for ν ≪ Γ, because it linearizes in the velocity and treats the Doppler shift as quasi-static; the 9Be+ anchor below sits at ν/Γ = 0.58, 0.94 and 1.54, in the resolved-sideband regime where the floor scales as (Γ/2ν)² rather than Γ/2ν and the optimum detuning moves from −Γ/2 toward −ν, which is why Monroe's best detuning was −30 MHz, 1.55Γ, and the measured n̄ lie near half the unresolved-limit values. Level A's Doppler stage therefore uses the rate framework of Section 4.2.2, A_± = W(Δ ∓ ν) + (η̃²/η²)W(Δ) with W from the multi-level Bloch steady state, valid at any ν/Γ, and optimizes Δ numerically, once per beam over the whole mode set: a Doppler beam has one detuning, one saturation parameter and one direction for all 3N modes, so Δ, s and k̂ are beam parameters chosen to minimize the participation-weighted mean of the per-mode steady-state n̄ (weights b_{i,m}² of the gate modes the schedule uses next, uniform when none is scheduled), and the per-mode residual n̄ is reported, never optimized mode by mode, since a per-mode optimum is a state no single beam can prepare (the optimum Δ for a 0.5 MHz axial and a 4 MHz radial mode differ by more than Γ); the acceptance test is Monroe's three modes from his single Δ = −30 MHz beam, whose three measured n̄ one shared detuning has to reproduce together (2026-09-04 experimentalist critique) **[corrected: critique, 2026-09-04]**; the force model is the ν ≪ Γ cross-check, invoked only when ν/Γ < 0.1 (the first version of this plan made the force model the default; caught by the 2026-09-04 experimentalist critique) **[corrected]**. The textbook limit k_B T_D = ħΓ/2 at low saturation and the corresponding n̄ ≈ Γ/(2ν) up to angular factors are **[background]**; valid in the unresolved-sideband regime ν ≪ Γ **[verified]**.

Anchors the simulator must reproduce: NIST 9Be+ (Γ/2π = 19.4 MHz, best detuning about −30 MHz) reached n̄ = (0.47, 0.30, 0.18) on modes at (11.2, 18.2, 29.8) MHz, consistent with the theoretical 0.484 for x **[verified]** (Monroe et al. 1995). For 40Ca+ on the 397 nm line at −20 MHz with an 866 nm repumper, the two-level estimate (0.5 mK, n̄_z ≈ 3, n̄_x,y ≈ 6) fails: the measured starting points were n̄_z = 6.5(1.0) and n̄_y = 16(2), because the multi-level structure invalidates the two-level assumption **[verified]** (Roos et al. 2000). For 171Yb+ at 0.5-0.8 MHz trap frequencies Doppler cooling leaves n̄ ≈ 20 **[verified]** (Ejtemaee and Haljan 2017). Level A therefore uses the two-level closed form only for near-two-level species and otherwise takes n̄_D from a level-C solve of the actual level scheme (S₁/₂, P₁/₂, D₃/₂ with repumper for Ca+; the F = 0, 1 hyperfine manifolds for Yb+), which is the reason the species modules carry full branching ratios.

#### 4.2.2 Resolved-sideband cooling

**Single-photon scheme.** With the cooling laser on the first red sideband, δ = −ν, each cycle removes ħν and the ground state is dark. Rate-equation treatment (RMP 2003 Eqs. 110-112, 116) **[verified]**: the rate out of |n⟩ is R_n = Γ̃ P_e(n) = Γ̃ (η√n Ω)²/[2(η√n Ω)² + Γ̃²], with Γ̃ the effective linewidth (possibly repumper-broadened); the ground-state population obeys

ṗ₀ = p₁ (ηΩ)²/Γ̃ − p₀ [ (Ω/2ν)² η̃² Γ̃ + (ηΩ/4ν)² Γ̃ ],

where the two loss terms are off-resonant carrier excitation followed by a sideband decay (η̃ is the Lamb-Dicke parameter of the spontaneously emitted photon, η̃² = α(k_em x₀)² with the angular factor α of Section 4.2.8 and k_em the emitted photon's wavenumber, which is the drive's only for a closed two-level cycle: in a quenched or Raman scheme the recoil photon is the fast level's decay, 393 nm for 40Ca+ cooled on the 729 nm quadrupole line and quenched at 854 nm, so (η̃/η)² = α(k_em/k_L)²/cos²θ_L = 0.4 × 1.85² = 1.37 there and the bracket below is 1.62, not 0.65 **[verified]**) and off-resonant blue-sideband excitation. The steady-state limit is ⟨n⟩_min ≈ (Γ̃/2ν)²[(η̃/η)² + 1/4] in the review's form and ⟨n⟩_min ≈ (γ/2ω_z)² ≪ 1 in Wineland 1998 **[verified]**, valid for γ ≪ ω_z, ω_z ≫ ω_R, with the recoil condition ω_R/ω_z = η² **[verified]**. Two printed errors in the review must not be copied: Eq. 109 as printed is non-Hermitian (i η(σ₊a + σ₋a†) must read i η(σ₊a − σ₋a†)) and would give a growing norm in any solver **[corrected]**; Eq. 113 lacks a 1/(n̄+1) normalization and a factor 1/2 in its sine arguments **[corrected]**. The general Lamb-Dicke framework for any scheme with known scattering rate W(Δ) at rest **[verified]** (RMP Eqs. 117-121):

R_{n→n+1} = (n+1) η² A₊,  R_{n→n−1} = n η² A₋,  A_± = W(Δ∓ν) + (η̃²/η²) W(Δ),  η̃²/η² = α k_em²/(Δk·ê_m)²,

with the intermediate state |e, n±1⟩ paired with detuning Δ∓ν (anti-correlated) **[corrected]**; this is level B's engine, with W(Δ) = Γρ_ee(Δ) from the Bloch steady state, α the recoil angular factor of Section 4.2.8 and k_em the emitted photon's wavenumber, so that the carrier term carries the emitted photon's Lamb-Dicke parameter η̃ while the sideband terms carry the drive's η, the two coinciding only for one resonant beam along the mode axis, where the weight reduces to α (the participation b_{i,m} multiplies both η̃ and η and cancels from the ratio, so the sideband-cooling floor of a given mode is independent of N, which is the regression; the second revision carried b_{i,m} in the denominator, inflating the floor by 1/b² for every multi-ion mode while every single-ion test passed at b = 1; 2026-09-04 experimentalist critique) **[corrected: critique, 2026-09-04]** and the expression to Stenholm's A_± = P(Δ ∓ ν) + αP(Δ) (Stenholm 1986 Eqs. 5.49-5.54); the first two revisions of this plan wrote the weight as α in general while computing (η̃/η)² = 1.376 for quenched 40Ca+ a few lines above, an inconsistency the derivation audit of 2026-09-04 caught **[corrected: derivation audit, 2026-09-04]**. Two conventions are fixed here, after a correction found by the 2026-09-04 critique: A_± are the bare coefficients with units of rate and the Fock rate equation carries η², where the RMP's Eqs. 117-119 place η² inside A_± and the EIT sources (Morigi, Roos, Lechner) outside; and the carrier term W(Δ) is kept, weighted by η̃²/η², because it is the recoil heating that accompanies carrier scattering and produces the (η̃/η)² piece of the floor above (equal to α only in the single-beam, on-axis case). The EIT papers omit it because at the dark resonance W(Δ) = 0, a property of that scheme and not a convention. The carrier term cancels in the cooling rate A₋ − A₊ = W(Δ+ν) − W(Δ−ν) but not in the steady state ⟨n⟩ = A₊/(A₋ − A₊) = [W(Δ−ν) + (η̃²/η²)W(Δ)]/[W(Δ+ν) − W(Δ−ν)], the opposite of what the first version of this plan stated **[corrected]**. W(Δ) is the same object as the readout scattering rate R_∘ of Section 8.1 and comes from one shared multi-level optical-Bloch steady-state solver, because the two-level Lorentzian saturating at Γ/2 over-predicts the 171Yb+ detection-beam rate by 2× against the three-ground-plus-one-excited ceiling of Γ/4 **[corrected]**.

**Stimulated-Raman scheme for hyperfine qubits** (Monroe et al. 1995; Wineland 1998) **[verified]**. Two beams detuned Δ from the P level with difference frequency ω₀ − ω_ν drive |↓,n⟩ → |↑,n−1⟩ with two-photon Rabi frequency Ω = g₁g₂/Δ, g₁ and g₂ the single-photon couplings in the half convention g = Ω_single/2 (in Monroe's convention P = sin²(Ωτ), so a π pulse is Ωτ = π/2, half the usual sin²(Ωt/2) convention, and in plan units the same quantity is Ω₁Ω₂/(2Δ), Section 13's two-photon row and its g-symbol row **[verified flag]**); a resonant repump |↑⟩ → |3⟩ ⇝ |↓⟩ returns the spin with Δn ≈ 0 when ω_R ≪ ω_z. A failure mode the simulator must reproduce: because the red-sideband Rabi frequency Ω_{n−1,n} depends on n, a fixed pulse area traps population in any |↓,n⟩ for which Ω_{n−1,n}t = 2mπ in this plan's Rabi convention (transfer probability sin²(Ω_{n−1,n}t/2)), which reads Ω_{n−1,n}t = mπ in Wineland's and Monroe's half-Rabi convention; laboratory sequences vary the pulse duration between cycles for this reason **[verified]**, and a missed conversion places the stranded population at a different n **[corrected]**. Cooling of higher sidebands first, as in the Home 2009 sequence (Doppler to n̄ ≈ 15, then second-sideband cycles, then first-sideband cycles, reaching about 0.94 ground-state fidelity and ⟨n⟩ ≈ 0.06 per mode in a four-ion mixed crystal) **[verified]**, is supported as a schedule option.

#### 4.2.3 EIT cooling

In a Λ system a strong blue-detuned coupling beam (Rabi frequency Ω_r, detuning Δ_r > 0) dresses the excited state and produces a Fano absorption profile for a weak cooling beam (Ω_g ≪ Ω_r): an exact zero at the two-photon resonance Δ_g = Δ_r that cancels carrier scattering, and a narrow bright resonance displaced by the light shift δ = (√(Δ_r² + Ω_r²) − |Δ_r|)/2 ≈ Ω_r²/(4Δ_r) **[verified]** (Morigi, Eschner, Keitel 2000, Eq. 1; the narrow dressed state shifts by +δ and the broad one by −Δ_r − δ **[corrected]**, and δ is the light shift of the ground-like dressed state **[corrected]**). The tuning rule is Δ_g = Δ_r and δ ≃ ν, which puts the red sideband on the narrow peak **[verified]**. With Δ_r = Δ_g = Δ the rate coefficients are

A_± = (Ω_g²/γ) · γ²ν² / { γ²ν² + 4[Ω_r²/4 − ν(ν ∓ Δ)]² },

the upper sign (heating) pairing with ν − Δ and the lower (cooling) with ν + Δ **[verified]**, and the Fock populations obey Ṗ(n) = η²[A₋((n+1)P(n+1) − nP(n)) + A₊(nP(n−1) − (n+1)P(n))] with η defined against the two-photon wavevector difference **[verified]**. The mean phonon number relaxes exponentially at rate η²(A₋ − A₊) to

⟨n⟩_S = A₊/(A₋ − A₊) = [γ²ν² + 4(Ω_r²/4 − ν(ν+Δ))²] / [4Δν(Ω_r² − 4ν²)],

which equals (γ/4Δ)² exactly at δ = ν **[verified]** (δ is not a free parameter once Ω_r and Δ are chosen; the calibration direction is Δ and target ν → Ω_r = 2√(ν(ν + |Δ|)) **[corrected]**), has poles at Δ = 0 and Ω_r = 2ν where cooling vanishes, and requires Δ > 0 **[verified]**; the RMP's (Γ/4Δ_r)² is approximate, the exact value at the RMP's own approximate tuning Ω_r² = 4νΔ_r being (Γ² + 4ν²)/(16Δ_r(Δ_r − ν)) **[corrected; the earlier flag printed (Δ_r + ν), a sign error caught by the 2026-09-04 critique: at γ = 1, ν = 0.3, Δ = 5 the formula gives 0.00361702, the (Δ − ν) form 0.00361702 and the (Δ + ν) form 0.00320755]**. The bandwidth advantage comes from the dressed-state linewidth Γ′ ≃ Γ(2δ/Ω_σ)² ≃ Γν/Δ_σ **[verified]**, wide enough to cool modes 1.7 MHz apart at one setting. Validation anchors: Roos et al. 2000 on 40Ca+ with Δ_σ = Δ_π = 2π × 70 MHz, Ω_σ = 2π × 21.4 MHz, Ω_π = 2π × 3 MHz, δ/2π = 1.6 MHz, reaching n̄_y = 0.18 and, at δ/2π = 3.3 MHz, n̄_z = 0.1 in about 250 µs, insensitive to the π-beam intensity only while I_π ≪ I_σ (their ratio about 100) **[corrected]**; Lechner et al. 2016 with Ω_σ = 2π × 30 MHz, Ω_π = 2π × 6.2 MHz, light shift 2.2-2.3 MHz, cooling an 18-ion 40Ca+ string's radial modes to n̄ ≈ 0.01-0.02 at R = (19 ± 3) × 10³ s⁻¹, with a fitted heating term of (65 ± 7) s⁻¹ on a nine-ion COM mode and per-mode rates from 5 to 17 × 10³ s⁻¹ across 1.13-3.84 MHz in a two-ion crystal **[verified]**. Level C for EIT is a four-level (Zeeman-resolved) master equation, not the idealized three-level one, because the experiments use the S₁/₂ and P₁/₂ Zeeman manifolds **[corrected]**.

#### 4.2.4 Polarization-gradient (Sisyphus) cooling

Two counter-propagating, cross-polarized beams blue of the 171Yb+ S₁/₂(F=1) → P₁/₂(F=0) line (Δ/2π = +310 MHz, s₀ = I/(51 mW/cm²)) in a 5.9 G transverse field cool all three axes from one beam pair because each beam projects onto the axes with factors {1/2, 1/2, 1/√2}, giving η = (0.052, 0.053, 0.090) at (0.790, 0.766, 0.525) MHz **[verified]** (Ejtemaee and Haljan 2017). In the Lamb-Dicke regime without Zeeman coherences the cooling rate scales as s₀² (measured exponents 1.98(6) axial, 1.91(3) transverse, over about a factor 7 in intensity) **[corrected]**; single-ion steady states reach n̄ ≈ 1.5-2 only at the optimum intensity and rise on both sides, all twelve modes of a four-ion string reached n̄ ≤ 3, and the cooling must be pulsed with an optical-pumping-plus-microwave reset because 15-20% of the time the ion is pumped into the dark |0,0⟩ state **[verified]**. The paper's quantum model (1D Monte Carlo wavefunction with hyperfine structure and Zeeman coherences, 20 or 200 Fock levels, 40-80 trajectories) is the template for level C here, including its documented truncation sensitivity **[corrected]**. Blue detuning is required for this F → F′ = F − 1 scheme with a transverse field; it is not a universal Sisyphus rule, though the paragraphs below show it is required for the same reason on any inverted j_e ≤ j_g line, where the most strongly coupled sublevel is the optical-pumping source rather than the sink **[corrected]**.

**The analytic polarization-gradient model, and why it is a second model rather than a refinement of the first** (Joshi et al. 2020, verified in Run 5) **[verified]**, **[corrected]**. The position dependence is printed inside Eq. (1) already, so the substitution 2φ → 2kz + 2φ belongs to the **pumping rates only**; applying it to Eq. **The Lindblad layer and the namespacing.** The full model is a four-level atom (the S₁/₂ and P₁/₂ Zeeman manifolds, no D branch) tensored with one truncated mode: two circular standing waves offset by ∓π/4 about the common phase, so their **relative** phase is π/2, a λ/8 displacement and not π/4; a coupling amplitude √(1/3) = √(2/3) × 1/√2 that is a Clebsch-Gordan ratio times each linear beam's projection onto one circular component and **not** a bare Clebsch-Gordan factor, since √(2/3) doubles the optical potential; and kẑ = η(a + a†) kept as the full exponential when η√(⟨n⟩ + ½) is not ≪ 1 **[corrected]**. The two layers are therefore separate models with no shared coefficients until a source for the second is transcribed, and a simulator must not validate one against the other's steady-state number: 0.87 quanta analytic phase-averaged, about 1 as the 171Yb+ expectation, 1.5-2 measured there, 7.7(1.1) outside the Lamb-Dicke regime in the analytic model's own experiment **[contested]**. #### 4.2.5 Sympathetic cooling

A second species (or, in the omg scheme, a metastable manifold) is cooled with light the qubit species does not see; Coulomb coupling shares the energy through the normal modes. #### 4.2.7 Thermometry and hand-off

After every stage the simulator can run the same diagnostics the laboratory runs and compare them with the state it actually prepared: (i) sideband-asymmetry thermometry, P_e^{rsb}/P_e^{bsb} = [n̄/(n̄ + 1)]^k for k-th-order sidebands, exact in η, in pulse duration and for any coupling matrix element because the same Ω_{m+k,m} appears on both sides (Turchette 2000 Eqs. 8-11; verified to 6 × 10⁻¹⁵ at η = 1.5, n̄ = 8), inverted as n̄ = R_k^{1/k}/(1 − R_k^{1/k}); the constancy of the ratio under varying pulse time is itself the test of thermality, since constancy forces a geometric distribution **[verified]**; (ii) blue-sideband Rabi flopping, P_↓(τ) = ½(1 + Σ_n P_n e^{−γ_n τ} cos(2Ω_{n+1,n}τ)), inverted by cosine transform for the Fock distribution P_n **[verified]** (Wineland 1998 Eq. 42, with Ω defined by ħΩ = −μB₁/4 so that the flopping frequency is 2Ω); and (iii) the thermal Debye-Waller reduction of a carrier Rabi frequency by spectator modes, Ω̄ = Ω^{(0)} Π_p Σ_{n_p} P_{n_p} e^{−η_p²/2} L_{n_p}(η_p²) ≈ Ω^{(0)} Π_p exp[−η_p²(n̄_p + 1/2)] with P_n = n̄^n/(1 + n̄)^{n+1} **[verified]** (Wineland 1998 Eqs. 122-124), which is what the carrier-Rabi calibration of Section 7.5 fits.

A rotating frame that removes the optical frequencies exists when each fine-structure manifold can be assigned one frame frequency such that every beam's detuning from every transition it drives is small: the builder assigns frame frequencies manifold by manifold, checks each beam against the manifolds it connects, moves the residual detunings onto the diagonal, and flags any beam whose residual is inconsistent with an already assigned manifold (two beams on one transition at different frequencies, or a closed loop of beams whose frequencies do not sum consistently), in which case the Hamiltonian is explicitly time periodic and is integrated as such or handled with `FloquetBasis`. The steady state of this system (`steadystate`) is the single scattering-rate object of Section 13 that supplies W(Δ) for cooling (Section 4.2.2), R_∘, R_d and R_b for readout (Section 8.1) and the dark-state physics of coherent population trapping; its time evolution from a given initial state gives the optical-pumping fidelity and duration of Section 4.2.6 from first principles, which fills the gap Run 3 named from both ends of the stack; and with one motional mode and the recoil operators above it is the level-C cooling simulation of Sections 4.2.1 to 4.2.4. The effective two-level descriptions of those sections (repumper-broadened linewidth Γ̃, the three-level EIT Fano profile, the two-level Doppler force) are recovered from it by adiabatic elimination under the conditions Marzoli et al. 1994 state, which Run 4 supplies; the simulator tests each closed form against the level-C solve in its stated regime.

**Pulsed sideband cooling with exact matrix elements** (Che et al. 2017; Rasmusson et al. 2021) **[verified]**, **[corrected]**. The rate picture of Section 4.2.2 is completed for pulsed schemes with the exact Ω_{n,n−k} of Section 4.3.1: one cycle, a k-th-order red-sideband pulse of duration t (a free parameter, not a π time; Rasmusson's worked example is t = 1.016 × 2π/Ω) followed by fast optical pumping, is a column-stochastic transfer matrix W_k(t) on the Fock populations, one matrix per sideband order with diagonal a_n = cos²(Ω_{n,n−k}t/2) and k-th upper diagonal b_n = sin²(Ω_{n,n−k}t/2), coherences being discarded between pulses because the pumping erases them; N pulses compose as an ordered matrix product, and the mean occupation after a schedule is Σ_n n[W(t_{N−1})⋯W(t₀) p_th]_n.

## 12. Risks, open physics and how the plan handles them

- **Anomalous heating has no first-principles model.** The heating rate follows from the electric-field noise spectral density S_E(ω) exactly [verified], but S_E itself is empirical: measured values span ten orders of magnitude, the distance exponent is disputed (2.6 to 4.0), and no microscopic mechanism is established [verified]. The simulator therefore takes S_E(ω) as a device input (constant, power-law, or tabulated) and derives everything downstream; it does not pretend to predict heating from electrode materials. Presets carry documented literature values with their conditions.
- **Convention drift.** Mathieu parameters, the Lamb-Dicke parameter, single- versus double-sided spectral densities, the sign of detuning, and the phase of σ_φ differ between sources; the research runs found factor-of-2 differences even between canonical papers. Section 13 fixes one convention per quantity, cites the source it follows, and every module's docstring restates the convention it uses. Tests pin the conversions.
- **The application could drift into a cartoon.** A user interface invites drawings that are merely suggestive, and a suggestive drawing next to an exact number misleads. Section 14 forbids it by construction: every level is a view over one run record, every drawn element is computed from the device model or the run, every displayed quantity must resolve to a provenance chip (a static test, Section 9.11), and coarse-graining identities between levels are tested numerically. The residual risk is stale views after a parameter change, handled by explicit stale badges rather than by hiding the dependency.
- **Exactness versus size.** Joint-exact simulation ends at a joint dimension of about 2 × 10³ to 4 × 10³, two or three ions with two or three dynamically resolved modes (Section 5.4; the second revision's 'near 8 ions' rested on the withdrawn ENR estimate, and the guard of Section 11.5 refuses above 4096). GATE_LOCAL is an approximation; the plan makes it measurable (residual displacement, comparison tests) and visible in results rather than hiding it.
- **Photon scattering and leakage need extra levels.** Modelling scattering as pure dephasing is wrong for hyperfine qubits (Rayleigh scattering at large detuning does not decohere them [corrected]); correct treatment needs the branching structure and, for leakage, extra levels per ion. The species modules carry the branching ratios and the Hilbert-space builder supports d > 2; the default d = 2 run reports scattering as an estimated rate rather than simulating it, and says so.
- **Readout is species-specific.** The off-resonant pumping channels that limit hyperfine-qubit readout differ from the shelf-decay channel that limits optical-qubit readout; the readout module is parameterized by the species' level structure and the plan validates each archetype separately.
- **Calibration realism.** Real devices calibrate against drifting parameters. The first release calibrates once per device configuration; drift enters through quasi-static noise sampling. Recalibration-in-the-loop is a planned extension.
- **Micromotion.** Default runs use the pseudopotential and the secular-frame Hamiltonian, with the Wronskian-normalized micromotion factor C₀ = 1 + 3q²/16 + O(q⁴) applied to effective Lamb-Dicke parameters (the (1 + q/2)⁻¹ of the earlier revisions was an rf-clock artifact) [corrected: derivation audit, 2026-09-04]. Explicit rf-sideband (Floquet) dynamics is an option for validation, not the default, because it multiplies the time-step budget by Ω_rf/ω_m.
- **Mixed-species dynamics** are out of scope for the first release; the crystal module already computes the mixed-species mode structure (Section 4.1.7) and works from a per-ion mass list, so sympathetic cooling at the master-equation level and gates on mixed crystals are local extensions.
- **Speed-fidelity trade-offs of real gates** (fast gates, Rydberg gates) are not targets; the simulator reproduces the gate families in Section 4 and their documented error mechanisms.

**Gaps named by the Run 2B consolidation.** Beyond the risks above, the cross-source consolidation of the Hamiltonian and system-level briefs listed what no source in the set supplies. The plan's answer to each is stated here so that nothing is assumed silently.

- **No published mode-participation matrix** exists for any chain whose fidelities are quoted (Debnath's 5, Wright's 11, Egan's 15, Landsman's 17, Chen's 30), and no published AM or FM segment amplitudes for any experiment. Published fidelities therefore cannot be reproduced from first principles without recomputing b_{im} from an assumed potential and re-solving the pulses; Section 9 treats such reproductions as consistency checks with stated assumptions, not as exact targets.
- **The ZZ native gate has no published physical model**; that it is an MS gate dressed by two SK1 rotations is an inference from the 220 µs duration difference. The simulator implements exactly that construction and labels it as inferred.
- **SK1, the detuning-compensating composite families and the general-θ_t construction for PD6** were outside the source set until Run 5 read Brown-Harrow-Chuang 2004, Cummins-Llewellyn-Jones 2003, Merrill-Brown 2014 and Low-Yoder-Chuang 2014 for them; Section 4.3.5 now carries the verified library with the printed defects it repairs, and the residual gaps are narrower: PD6's phase vector at arbitrary γ still needs the numerical analytic continuation rather than a printed formula (and the continuation ranges are stated inconsistently between that paper's body text and its Fig. 2 caption at n = 7), Low-Yoder-Chuang's AP3 and NS2 closed forms and its Table III κ prefactors were not transcribed, so a scheduler can rank same-order sequences by order but not by prefactor, and no source gives a rule for choosing a family from a measured (ε_A, ε_D, ε_N) triple against a target infidelity. Run 5 read only arXiv preprints and performed no errata search, so its ~20 repairs are brief-derived rather than published errata until the journal versions are checked.
- **No leakage channel, and no crosstalk, appear in the fault-tolerance error model**, so its pseudothresholds cannot assess a crosstalk-limited or leakage-limited architecture; the simulator's Section 6.8 export includes both so that a client can evaluate the two halves together for the first time.
- **Electric-field noise is available only as an exponent** (α_S = 0.8(1)) with an apparatus-specific offset, no spatial correlation length and no mechanism for the measured 1.2× (15 ions) and 1.35× (25 ions) excess over the uniform-field model; the 8(1) ms motional coherence quoted by Fang is unsupported within its own paper, whose cited section documents only a 494(18) ms laser value. Heating and motional dephasing therefore remain user-supplied rates with provenance.
- **Rayleigh recoil kicks and the Rayleigh-rate-imbalance channel** are named mechanisms without a validated operator form or matrix elements; Clebsch-Gordan tables and a^{(J)} amplitudes are worked only for 9Be+. The species database computes the angular algebra for 43Ca+ and 171Yb+ itself and tests it against the 9Be+ example.
- **Residual off-resonant carrier excitation** is a pulse-design target in two sources but appears in no error budget, so a carrier-suppression implementation has nothing published to calibrate against; the simulator reports it from the exact dynamics.
- **Micromotion beyond first order in q**, the rf phase relative to the pulse envelope, beam-intensity and pointing spectra, and the laser linewidth as a spectrum are all absent; the plan's Floquet treatment (Section 4.1) covers the first, the drive coefficients accept the rest as user-supplied spectra.
- **The composite-pulse detuning error δ̂ has no stated normalization** (reproducing the published curves requires guessing δ̂ = 2πf/Ω); the DRB error-rate prefactor is undecidable from the published data (both (15/16)(1 − p) and 1 − p fall inside the reported spread). Both are recorded as open conventions with the simulator's choice stated.
- **Cetina's quasi-static amplitude model is validated only for n̄ ≫ 1**; sideband-cooled axial modes should show revivals at t_rev = 2πn̄/(Ω₀κ) that no source characterizes, and the quadratic beam expansion is only marginally valid in the reported data (407 nm rms spread against an 870 nm waist). The exact position-operator implementation of Section 6.2 covers both regimes and is tested against the closed forms only where they apply.
- **The factor of about 2 between the depolarizing model and application data** on the 30-qubit machine is attributed to mechanical spin-phase noise via optical path length, with no spectral density or correlation time; the hypothesis cannot be implemented or falsified from the source and is left as a user-supplied phase-noise spectrum.
- **No data exist on how gate fidelity depends on qubit-to-ion mapping**, although heating and dephasing scale with pair-dependent gate time while depolarizing and over-rotation do not; the simulator exposes the mapping as an input and reports per-pair budgets so the dependence can be computed.
- **Fock-space truncation for the exact exponential Hamiltonian at large n̄ has no sourced criterion**; Section 5.5's convergence policy is standard practice and is enforced by the paired-cutoff test rather than by a citation.
- **Transport, splitting, merging and recooling dynamics** were outside every source of Runs 1 to 4 and remain outside this release (Section 1.3). Run 5 closed the sourcing gap for transport, splitting and junctions at the equation level (Section 4.6, milestone M12), and its own residue is the nine gaps listed under the Run 5 open issues below: no merge model, no zero-excitation diabatic protocol, no quantum dynamics through the splitting critical point, no electrode basis potentials, no bridge from residual quanta to gate error, no mixed-species transport, no micromotion in either splitting framework, an undefined adiabatic-suppression constant, and no prescription for the inter-segment hand-off of the invariant state. Recooling dynamics after a transport remain unsourced entirely.

**Gaps named by the recovery consolidation across all six briefs, and their status after the revision of 2026-09-04.** Surface-electrode trap geometry appeared in no source; Section 4.1.6 now supplies the gapless-plane analytic layer with Run 4 checking it against House and Wesenberg, and the trap module still accepts field curvatures from outside for gapped or three-dimensional geometries. The quantized multi-ion Floquet frame did not exist in the set; Run 4 reads Landa's quantum companion paper and Kaufmann's measured deviations (Section 4.1.7), and until that is folded in every N > 1 result is pseudopotential-frame with the residual error reported as unquantified. Optical pumping had no first-principles rate model; Section 4.2.8 builds it from the multi-level Bloch equations with the matrix elements of Section 4.5. The spontaneous-emission recoil kernel and second-sideband cooling rates are specified in Section 4.2.8 (recoil operators on a direction quadrature; exact matrix elements for pulsed schemes), with Run 4 checking the angular factors and the pulsed-cooling models; the Sisyphus Liouvillian that Run 4 recorded as absent is supplied by Run 5 (Section 4.2.4) as the four-level lin-perp-lin model with a twelve-operator recoil-resolved dissipator, whose analytic prefactors are specific to a j_g = ½ ↔ j_e = ½ line and are asserted by nobody to carry to the 171Yb+ F = 1 → F′ = 0 scheme, so the two polarization-gradient layers stay separate models. Mixed-species modes are specified in Section 4.1.7 from the mass-weighted eigenproblem, with the two-ion closed forms as tests and Run 4 checking the sources. Leakage, the Rayleigh-imbalance channel and the angular algebra for species other than 9Be+ are supplied by Section 4.5, which derives every hyperfine-resolved quantity from cited constants. The quadrupole run (Section 4.5.7) left two named gaps, one of which closed on 2026-09-04 when the 88Sr+ D₅/₂ lifetime was pinned at 390.8(1.6) ms with a negligible blackbody correction (Section 4.5.7; the 674 nm Rabi frequencies fell 6.06% against the superseded 1987 value); the remaining one is that the ac Stark shift of a driven Zeeman component by the nine off-resonant components within the 30 MHz span has no published number to validate the second-order computation against. No paper states whether its Rabi frequency was calibrated at n = 0, at n̄ or against a nominal pulse area, which is exactly what selects among the three η⁴ thermal coefficients. The cubic Coulomb anharmonicity has never been measured at gate time scales. Anomalous heating cannot be extrapolated across traps (distance exponents from d⁻² to d⁻¹² within the set), so every heating number is apparatus-local. Correlated SPAM across a register and multi-ion detector crosstalk are unmeasured. And the Lamb-Dicke parameter carries a √N base ambiguity between sources worth a factor 5 to 17 in every η² rate, while the Rabi-frequency convention table now has seven entries; both are enforced by executable ingest tests rather than documentation (Section 13).

**Compute budget and calibration cost (added 2026-09-04).** The measured cost model of Section 11.1 bounds what JOINT_EXACT can validate: two ions with two or three resolved modes, three ions with two, four ions with two at low caps; a four-ion gate whose closure needs both transverse families (eight modes) is beyond exact integration and is verified through the closed-form integrals of Section 4.4.3. Calibration by simulated experiment does not fit the budget beyond two ions, so the default is the closed-form surrogate with exact spot checks of Section 7.5, and the full path is an opt-in audit; the risk is a surrogate that misses a physical effect the exact path would have shown, which is why the audit exists and why every calibrated entry reports the surrogate's error against its spot check.

**Open issues named by the Run 4 consolidation (2026-09-04).** Five things the four briefs could not source, recorded so they are not mistaken for settled: no source treats a mixed-species crystal in the Floquet frame, so the per-species time-dependent η of Section 4.1.7 is the plan's derivation validated against the static limit; no source prints the time-periodic Coulomb Hessian that Kaufmann's zigzag shift needs, so the module's construction from the closed-form Coulomb Hessian along the micromotion trajectory has one measured anchor; the exact origin of the 171Yb+ detection factor 1/3 was closed by Run 5, which extracted and verified Berkeland and Boshier 2002 and identified it as the equal-population saturation ceiling n_e/(n_e + n_g) of the Λ repump cycle, the 1/4 of the same section being that ceiling for the four-state F = 1 → F′ = 0 manifold (Section 8.1); Uys's Eq. 5 with its definitions and Wineland 2003's Eqs. 2.13-2.18 were re-read from the e-prints by a three-verifier panel in Run 5, which confirmed them and fixed four conventions (signed amplitudes, λ as the incident rather than the scattered polarization index, the Γ_el/4 dissipator prefactor that must not be "corrected" to Γ_el/2, and the detuning optimum being of the ratio R_SE/|Ω| and not of R_SE alone), so the M0a check is now a regression rather than a first verification (Section 4.5.5); and the errata check of 2026-09-04 (Appendix D) found no published erratum, corrigendum or publisher's note for any of the twenty-five printed source errors it tested, so every one of them stands on this plan's recomputation: corroborated where a preprint exists (Wineland 1998's 2aρa† is printed correctly in both arXiv versions), and downgraded in two cases where the error turned out to be the preprint's alone while the journal version is correct (Turchette 2000, Lee 2005), with the Brownnutt Eq. 24 and ladder-operator sub-claims still resting on a single preprint because the published review was unreadable; APS abstract pages refused automated access, so the publishers' own erratum panels were not read and the negatives rest on Crossref, INSPIRE, OpenAlex, PubMed and the QIC index, each of which surfaced real errata for other papers in the same check. Three more the plan fills by construction and tags as its own: the laboratory-to-atomic-frame polarization rotation (Section 4.5.3), the N-level multi-laser optical Bloch builder with its rotating-frame existence test (Section 4.2.8), and the general-J hyperfine diagonalizer with the B_hfs term (Section 4.5.1); each carries a **[background]** tag and its tests in Section 9 are the only evidence for it.

**Open issues named by the Run 5 briefs (2026-09-04).** The four source briefs of Run 5S (Appendix D) and their consolidation named what they could not source, listed here by topic so that none is mistaken for settled; each is tagged where it arises in Sections 4.2.4, 4.3.5, 4.3.7, 4.5.5, 4.5.7, 4.6, 6.9 and 8.1.

- **The comb layer has no spontaneous-emission rate at all, and that is its largest gap.** Every Hamiltonian in all six Run 5S comb sources is written after adiabatic elimination of the P manifold and carries no Lindblad term; one of them never even defines γ except inside Ω₀ = sγ²/(2Δ). Missing: per-gate Raman and Rayleigh scattering probabilities (which scale differently in Δ than Ω does), branching ratios, and decay of the P manifold during the pulse. The plan's answer is to compute the scattering channels of Section 4.5.5 from the *same* tooth sums that build the drive, the residual excited population being Σ_k |g_k|²/(4Δ²), so drive and dissipator cannot drift apart; until that is validated against an atomic-data source the simulator can bound but not compute the real fidelity ceiling of a 355 nm comb drive.
- **No multi-ion, multi-mode comb construction exists in the source set.** Every comb equation is single-ion and single-mode, so mode frequencies, participation factors b_{im}, per-mode η_{im}, the spectator-mode sum that limits Mølmer-Sørensen fidelity, phase-space closure for a comb-driven gate, and the crosstalk of the comb's other beat notes onto spectator modes all come from Sections 4.1.3 and 4.4.3 rather than from these papers. The reported two-ion fidelities (0.86 twice, from two different apparatus) are therefore consistency checks with stated assumptions, not reproduction targets, and no source states how the Dirichlet grating factor resolves closely spaced modes.
- **Beat-note phase stability across space does not close.** What exists: relative arm lengths must overlap the pulses at the ion but need not be stable at optical-wavelength scales; splitting one source into two arms cancels common-mode optical-frequency fluctuations; ΔL ≪ cτ ≈ 3.0 mm. What is missing: a path-length-mismatch tolerance in units of τ or of the overlap window, the spatial phase gradient of the beat note across a chain, the relation between arm-length drift and the spin-dependent-force phase, and any interferometer path-length noise spectrum. The one printed wave-front tolerance does not close arithmetically (0.02° over a 30 µm chain gives 15.0° end-to-end against a stated "< 10°"; 0.0133° is needed and 0.05° was achieved), the tighter shuttling calibration "was not implemented", and the claim that no active stabilization is needed is asserted for single-shot operation with no spectrum behind it.
- **No noise spectra anywhere, only single numbers.** Repetition rate: a 1 Hz/min counter drift and a ~10⁻⁷ fractional excursion over minutes, with no power spectral density, no Allan deviation and no correlation time - and since 2.617 Hz/s accumulates only 0.008 Hz over the measured 3 ms unlocked Ramsey time, the fast jitter must be *fitted* to that 3 ms (about 53 Hz rms on the beat note, 0.34 Hz rms on ν_rep) and labelled as fitted, not sourced. Intensity noise: one flat α, with no relative-intensity-noise spectrum and no measurement above the phase-locked-loop corner where the profile takes on the reference oscillator's character. Master-oscillator and local-oscillator phase noise, which set the residual after feed-forward, are never quantified. Pulse-to-pulse amplitude and timing jitter of the train are excluded by construction (identical envelopes assumed), and the pulse-picker jitter blamed for the observed revival decay is never measured.
- **The Chain I / Chain II factor of 2 remains formally contested.** Chain I (Ω = g²/2Δ) is what the plan's own Section 13 two-photon-Rabi row selects, and Chain II (Ω = g²/Δ) is what reproduces the source's own printed ~12 nJ π-pulse energy. Adopting Chain I therefore asserts that printed number is 2× too small (27.122 against 13.561 nJ). That conclusion is derived from the plan's convention, not verified against the paper, and it is exactly the kind of factor earlier runs found overturned; the constant is exposed as a flag and the doubled E_π is never presented as a source value.
- **The fourth-order Stark perturbation series is marginal at full power and nobody bounds it.** The source never states |Ω_{n,a}| ≪ |δ_{n,a}|, yet at its own quoted operating point Ω₀/2π = 45.7 MHz exceeds δ_{00,10}/2π = 42.8 MHz, so the expansion parameter is order unity precisely where the paper advertises the shift as three orders above the second-order one. A non-perturbative check - direct diagonalization of the tooth-pair-coupled ground manifold, or a Floquet or Magnus treatment - is needed before the closed form is trusted at full power, and the simulator does not claim it there.
- **The published absolute fourth-order shifts are not reproducible from the stated inputs.** Independent chains give 17.8 and 9.07 MHz against the printed 23 and 12 MHz; a single Ω₀/2π = 45.68 MHz reproduces the first and predicts the second to 2.5%, so the equation's *structure* and the comb factors validate while the absolute scale does not, the ~30% gap being absorbed entirely by an unstated beam waist ("less than 3 µm"). Compounding it, the 23 MHz branch requires pure σ_± light, which that apparatus cannot deliver (its field is orthogonal to all viewports), and the demonstrated shift was about 10 MHz. Regression targets are the polarization ratio and the comb factors, never the absolute megahertz.
- **The same source's pulse duration and bandwidth are mutually inconsistent, and it propagates.** τ ≈ 14 ps implies a 20.0 GHz intensity FWHM through the paper's own tooth formula, while its quoted "~70 GHz" is 1/τ read as a width and implies τ ≈ 4.0 ps. Unresolvable from the paper, and it matters: C_{00,10} moves from 0.9438 to 0.7174 across the ambiguity, a 24% swing straight into δω^{(4)}. τ is therefore an explicit input, and the polarization ratio (stable to ±1% over 4 to 20 ps) is the regression target.
- **No fourth-order (intensity-squared) Stark shift exists for a resonant carrier drive.** One source has no fourth-order term at all, the single-comb form excludes the resonant beat note by construction, and the third builds its machinery for a deliberately off-resonant Z shift with δ_{n,a} ≠ 0. Missing: the intensity-squared correction to the qubit frequency *while* a Raman rotation is being driven - the case a gate simulator cares about - its interplay with the sideband operator in a counterpropagating spin-dependent-force geometry, whether the Stark-nulling comb offset is even compatible with the sideband resonance condition and the excluded offsets, and the fourth-order shift of the motional modes and of the Zeeman states as a dephasing channel during a gate.
- **The crossover between the ultrafast-kick and resolved-sideband pictures is unbounded.** Two sources operate at large per-pulse area, four at small; the selector is train duration against the trap period plus the Lamb-Dicke condition, but nothing quantifies at what per-pulse area the continuous-tone replacement begins to fail, nor how to interpolate. The runtime guard therefore refuses to interpolate and routes to the exact pulse-by-pulse propagator, which is a safe default rather than a model of the crossover.
- **No numerical Lamb-Dicke parameter is given by any comb source** (grep-confirmed absent from three of them; one gives 0.1 only as a worked-case figure, and its own notation contradicts it - the value closes only if the printed bare k is read as the two-photon Δk). Both the geometry and the species mass come from outside, and one source's Δk = √2 k itself rests on an inferred 90° beam crossing, so η stays a fitted parameter against a measured sideband or collapse width.
- **The comb-tooth linewidth is never quantified.** The tooth-narrowness requirement is stated only as N ≫ 1, with no linewidth value and no relation between N, the tooth width and the resolved-sideband requirement; the "tooth width below the AOM offset" condition is an inequality with no number, and the one reshaped train that is characterized satisfies it only marginally (~370 MHz teeth against a 489 MHz offset). Nothing says how to model the resulting partial wrong-direction kick beyond adding an opposite-sign displacement branch by hand.
- **Comb species coverage is one isotope at one wavelength.** Everything is 171Yb+ at 355 nm with a 12.64 GHz splitting: no I_sat, γ, fine-structure splitting, sweet-spot wavelength or (ν_rep, tooth-order) working point for any other hyperfine qubit, and the D1/D2 line-strength weights (1/12, 1/6, the −2 and the +1) are species- and manifold-specific. The saturation intensity in particular is a paper-specific lumped convention that cannot be transferred. No mixed-species content and no D- or F-level leakage channel appears at all.
- **Several of these repairs could not be checked against the version of record.** All verification was against arXiv preprints, since the publishers' landing pages were access-walled during the run; one source's equation numbering is disputed between verification passes, so its expressions are cited by content rather than by number. Given that roughly a dozen printed defects were repaired in this topic alone, the journal versions should be checked before any of them is presented as published errata rather than as brief-derived.
- **Multi-axis dynamical decoupling is absent from every Run 5 source.** XY4, XY8, KDD, concatenated CDD, PDD and QDD all alternate the pulse axis, and every closed form the run verified assumes each pulse is a π rotation about the same axis, which is exactly what collapses the toggling matrix Λ^{(l)} to the scalar (−1)^l. That scalar bookkeeping is **invalid for alternating axes**, so no (−1)^j filter-function sum, no CP or UDD order result and none of the finite-pulse asymptotics of Section 6.9 carries over, and no source supplies the transverse rows of the filter matrix, the resulting filter functions, or a CPMG-against-XY4 comparison. The module implements the full 3 × 3 machinery instead and tags the multi-axis results **[background]**, validated only against the single-axis closed forms; the primary sources to read are Maudsley, J. Magn. Reson. 69, 488 (1986) and Gullion, Baker and Conradi, J. Magn. Reson. 89, 479 (1990). This matters directly, because the plan's own (XY)^N anchor (Egan's T₂ = 2.84(16) s) is a multi-axis sequence.
- **The dynamically corrected gate is not implementable from any source in the set.** Green 2013's Table I caption gives only amplitudes and timings, with no segment phases and no amplitude-time normalization, and its Eqs. 47-48 were not transcribed; the reconstruction in which the angles sum to π gives a sequence **worse** than a primitive pulse (detuning slope 2 rather than 4). Yet the DCG is the cited remedy for restoring Carr-Purcell's 18 dB/octave under finite pulses, so it must come from Khodjasteh and Viola, PRL 102, 080501 (2009), directly.
- **No closed-form detuning filter function.** The exact segment sum with its A_l and B_l coefficients exists only for the amplitude quadrature. The intra-segment control vector ρ_d^{(l)}(s) = ẑ cos Ω_l s + ρ̂(φ_l + π/2) sin Ω_l s is derived and verified here, and its Fourier integral is elementary (three terms at ω and ω ± Ω_l), but it is written down by no source, so the detuning quadrature is either derived in the module or evaluated on a fine grid, and either way tagged **[background]**.
- **No validated multiplicative amplitude-noise filter function.** The filter-function formalism is strictly additive (H₀ = β·σ independent of the control), whereas a Rabi-frequency fluctuation Ω → Ω(1 + ε(t)) has noise term ε(t)h_x(t)σ_x, which is control-proportional and, under pulsed control, not wide-sense stationary, so the spectral-overlap formulas do not cover it as written; Green lists control-field noise only as future work. This is exactly the error class SK1 and BB1 exist to cancel, so the gap sits at the weakest joint between the static and spectral halves of the topic. The module treats a multiplicative spectrum as additive with β_a = (Ω_l/Ω_max)β and reports that it has done so.
- **No second-order filter function**, hence no quantitative error bar on the first-order path beyond |n-point term| ≤ O(ξ^n); F_zz^{(2)} is used by Green Eq. 34 but defined only in Green, Uys and Biercuk, PRL 109, 020501 (2012). Relatedly there is no closed second Magnus term for an arbitrary sequence (only SK1's), so every ĉ_{m+1} and the cross-term ĉ_{1,1} is fitted numerically.
- **Two published dc floors do not reproduce.** With the corrected Ω^{−2(m+1)} normalization, only SK1's floor matches the figure it is read from (5.8736 × 10⁻⁶ against 5.86 × 10⁻⁶). BB1 recomputes to 3.5368 × 10⁻⁹ against a quoted 3.9 × 10⁻⁹ (10% low) and CORPSE to 1.6725 × 10⁻⁹ against a quoted 3.0 × 10⁻⁹ (a factor 1.8), and a back-solve from the figure would need ĉ₂ ≈ 0.012 against two independent high-precision computations agreeing on 0.006500751892 to nine digits. The simulator carries the computed coefficients and records the discrepancy rather than tuning to the figure.
- **No infrared regulator is supplied by any source**, yet on the ω⁻⁴ spectra a real trap shows, χ genuinely diverges for free induction, the Hahn echo and every odd-n sequence. ω_min is therefore the implementer's choice, materially changing absolute numbers; the plan ties it to the total experiment duration, records it as a tagged assumption and prints it beside every χ. Biercuk's own unexplained odd-n excess error (≈10% against ≈1% at even n, persisting to n = 10) is a known mismatch between this model class and that data set: a faithful finite-τ_π implementation **does** contain a large odd-n penalty, but whether it explains the data is open and the simulator is not tuned to its magnitude.
- **No trapped-ion parameters or measured spectra anywhere in this topic.** Green's Gaussian test spectrum, Kabytayev's hand-normalized 1/f benchmark at Ω = 1.5 × 10⁶ rad/s and Biercuk's un-normalized ambient spectrum are all synthetic or uncalibrated, and Biercuk's ambient case additionally drifts in real time including spur positions. Every absolute infidelity from this layer is therefore conditional on the device's own S_B, S_Ω and (ε_A, ε_D, ε_N), which are user inputs with provenance.
- **No scheduler objective.** Nothing converts a sequence's total rotation angle into a spontaneous-scattering probability, motional heating or laser-power cost, and nothing gives a rule for choosing a composite family from a measured error triple against a target infidelity. The closest published aids are Low-Yoder-Chuang's κ (valid only for same-order, same-γ comparisons, and its Table III values were not transcribed) and the saturation condition |δ| ∼ |ε|^n. Every dc coefficient, filter function and crossover in the sources is quoted at θ = π while the sequences are defined for arbitrary θ, so the θ dependence of the prefactors is unmapped.
- **Nothing beyond square pulses, and nothing about a DD train of composite pulses.** Every derivation in the topic is piecewise-constant; Green states explicitly that continuous modulation can be evaluated only approximately. There is no filter function for a decoupling sequence whose π pulses are themselves composite (a BB1 or a DCG inside a CPMG or UDD train), which is precisely the construction the finite-pulse degradation result points to as the fix, and the plan's own chip-trap practice (Section 7.8) uses Gaussian-enveloped SK1.
- **Nothing non-Gaussian, non-stationary or quantum-bath, and no phase-noise quadrature.** Every usable result needs zero-mean Gaussian wide-sense-stationary classical noise, which is also what licenses W = e^{−χ} and the moment closure. Telegraph noise, two-level fluctuators, spin baths, slow drift and laser phase noise as a spectrum are outside the model, and the cross-spectrum S_ad is assumed exactly zero.
- **No errata search was performed and only arXiv preprints were read.** Roughly twenty printed defects were repaired across five papers in this topic, which is itself a reason to check the journal versions (PRA 70 052318; PRA 67 042308; Adv. Chem. Phys. 154 241; PRA 89 022341; PRA 90 012316; NJP 15 095004; PRA 79 062324) before shipping any of them. Until then every **[corrected]** tag here is brief-derived, not a published erratum.
- **One brief-level check did not reproduce and one is a typo in the Run 5S brief.** The Run 5S brief's assertion that Wimperis's W1 = R(π, φ)R(2π, 3φ)R(π, φ) equals −I is wrong in the plan's own generator convention: it is **+I** (two π pulses give −I, the 2π filler gives −I). And the Run 5S brief's "2.9 × 10²⁰" for the SK1 dc floor with Ω⁻⁴ omitted recomputes as 2.97 × 10¹⁹; the physics of the trap it illustrates is unaffected. Both are recorded so that a future reader does not "fix" the tests back to the Run 5S brief.
- **No hyperfine (F-basis) version of the coherent-population-trapping Liouvillian.** The excited-to-ground coherence-transfer structure is given in J and m_J only. For 171Yb+ the branching among F′ levels and the corresponding 6j coherence-transfer weights are needed and absent, and the dark-state coefficient vectors are transcribed only for the 1 ↔ 0 and 1 ↔ 1 entries of the source's table (each needing the σ-index repair), so the general J_i ↔ J_i and J_i ↔ J_i − 1 vectors that a large-J or D₅/₂-P₃/₂ case would need are unavailable. Section 8.1's ceiling assertion is therefore the only guard the detection module has against a wrong dark-state basis.
- **The species-specific dark-resonance map exists for one species.** The S-P-D Raman-resonance zeros and the optimum destabilizing field are transcribed only for 88Sr+ (P_f vanishing at δ_B = (5/22)γ and (5/6)γ with the optimum at δ_B ≈ 0.05γ). The Ca+ and Ba+ values, and the general recipe for locating those zeros from g-factors and detunings, are missing, so a per-species destabilization optimizer cannot be guarded away from them; and the ac Stark shift of the cooling line by a strong repumper is only remarked on qualitatively, so line-centre pulling by the destabilizing beam cannot be predicted.
- **The corrected Λ-arm repumper window is a Run 5 repair with no published erratum.** What follows from the rate-equation steady state is (1 − α)R_d > αR_i, i.e. √α Ω_SP < Ω_DP < γ/2; the printed condition is too strong by 1/α² (about 200× for Ca+ and Sr+) and leaves no valid operating point. The repair is cross-checked only against the source's own figure, and it consumes the 40Ca+ P₁/₂ branching fraction, which is itself uncertain at the 30% level (1:12 in one source, 1:16 in another for the same quantity, with later work nearer 1:17.6 and no dedicated measurement verified in any run). Section 4.2's repumper design therefore carries a 30% uncertainty that no test can remove.
- **The collision bound is not reproducible from its own coefficients.** Recomputing R = Σ_s Γ_s p_s/(k_BT) at 300 K with 10⁻¹¹ mbar of each partner gives 4.36 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ against the printed "< 3 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹"; the source states no temperature and no conversion rule, and its 50/50 N₂/H₂ composition is imported from a different apparatus. The neglect conclusion survives either way, but 3 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ must not be presented as derived, and the coefficients themselves are quoted rather than measured by the reporting paper, so error bars and level assignments need the 1995 primary source. Because collisional decay is indistinguishable in shape from natural decay, any higher-pressure comparison must be measured at several pressures and extrapolated.
- **Blackbody physics for the Ca+ D levels stops at the D-D M1 channel.** There is no BBR-driven D → P depopulation estimate for Ca+, no temperature-dependent correction to the measured 1168 and 1176 ms, and no ambient-temperature formula, so "what is the 40Ca+ D₅/₂ lifetime at 4 K or 350 K" cannot be answered from the source set - unlike 88Sr+, whose D₅/₂ → P₃/₂ channel Section 4.5.7 now quantifies. Also absent: the 729 and 854 nm laser-induced deshelving rates that dominate real experiments, which the reshelving rate equation parameterizes as a single R without predicting it.
- **The Raman and Rayleigh dissipators still carry no recoil operator.** They are internal-state only, with no photon-recoil kick, no scattering-induced heating rate and no Lamb-Dicke dressing - only the source's remark that the per-π-pulse probability rises by 1/η for sidebands, with no operator form. The twelve-operator polarization-gradient kernel of Section 4.2.4 is the only recoil model any Run 5 source supplies, and its transfer to the scattering channels is untested. Related and still open: no criterion for the detuning at which Rayleigh suppression sets in, and no unified formula spanning the clock-state 1/Δ⁴ and the general 1/Δ² regimes, which would need the Ozeri 2005 and 2007 papers transcribed properly rather than through the rejected rate-difference form.
- **Wineland 2003 Eqs. 2.5-2.9 and 2.11-2.12 remain untranscribed.** The most consequential is the net 9Be+ Stark shift, which pairs all eight coefficient sets of the general scattering rate with the same detunings at first power and is what a simulator needs in order to null a differential shift - and is the counter-example that makes the clock-qubit shift's non-nullability meaningful. Also missing: the Rabi-flopping equations that define the half-angle π-pulse convention, the displacement operator and sideband Rabi frequencies, and the Debye-Waller factors the paper names as necessary outside the Lamb-Dicke limit but never gives.
- **The two polarization-gradient layers have no transfer path between them.** The analytic model's 1/3, 1/9, 16/9, 2/9 and 8ξ² prefactors are specific to j_g = ½ ↔ j_e = ½; the sources explicitly warn they fail for j_e = 3/2, and whether they carry to the 171Yb+ F = 1 → F′ = 0 scheme is asserted by nobody, that source printing no display equations at all. The only bridge is the derived, unverified s_Joshi = (3/2)s₀(Γ/2)²/((Γ/2)² + Δ²), so no closed-form light-shift depth, pumping rate, cooling rate prefactor or steady state exists for the 171Yb+ scheme - only the measured s₀² exponent - and the criterion "light-shift depth ≈ ħω/2 gives one quantum" cannot be turned into a numeric test. There is also no j_g = ½ ↔ j_e = 3/2 model at all, which is the relevant case for D2-line cooling.
- **Polarization-gradient cooling has no B-field dependence, no D branch, no 3D form and no model outside the Lamb-Dicke regime.** The analytic model omits Zeeman shifts entirely, so it cannot predict how cooling degrades as the Zeeman splitting approaches the detuning or as the quantization axis tilts off the beams; it omits the P₁/₂ → D₃/₂ branch the experiment handles with an 866 nm repumper, so the three-manifold Liouvillian and the repumper rate needed to keep the four-level reduction valid are both absent; everything analytic is 1D along one axis, with no multi-beam geometry, no planar-crystal theory and no 3D Doppler closed form; and outside η√(⟨n⟩ + ½) ≪ 1 there is only the observation that the limit rises and the optimum shifts to larger ξ. Multi-ion use additionally needs per-mode Lamb-Dicke parameters that neither source supplies, and one source's trap-frequency triple is unlabelled by axis in a way a normal-mode recomputation contradicts.
- **Two published corrections behind the Run 5 numbers are unobtainable.** The intensity-dependent analytic correction for ²P₁/₂ F = 1 scattering that the 171Yb+ theory curves carry comes from an unpublished companion reference, so a from-scratch simulation cannot overlay those panels; and the derivation of the three-point recoil weights is credited to a 1993 paper that does not show it, so generalizing them to a different quantization-axis geometry is not possible from the source set.
- **Provenance ceiling.** Every Run 5 source was verified against an arXiv e-print, all published versions being paywalled or bot-walled, and the errata check of 2026-09-04 found no published erratum for any of them. So the repairs this topic makes on its own authority - the reversed dark-state pairing, the "≈ 63°" misprint, the reciprocal θ_BE mapping, the 2π/Φ harmonic count, the repumper inequality, the omitted −i/ħ, the Γ-versus-√Γ jump scaling and the coherent recoil sum - stand on this plan's recomputation and are tagged brief-derived rather than published errata. One preprint's title differs from the published title under the same DOI, and the 171Yb+ Letter appeared about 17 months after its only preprint version, so its numbers could have been revised in proof.
- **No merge model exists in any source.** Every splitting formula in the Run 5 set is one-directional. Nothing states whether the time reverse of a split waveform is optimal, how the relative motional phase of the two wells at the moment of merging enters, how merge excitation scales with duration, or what a merge costs; one source recombines ions only for state detection and reports no merge budget, and the other reports a single combined split-and-combine duration without saying whether it covers one operation or the pair. There is also no data on repeated split-merge cycling or cumulative excitation over a circuit. Section 4.6 therefore specifies merging as the plan's own construction - the time reverse of the split with an explicit relative-phase parameter - tagged **[background]**, and milestone M12's exit criteria cannot test it against anything.
- **Diabatic transport with zero final excitation is a criterion, not a protocol.** The Fourier condition on the well acceleration says when the excitation vanishes but no source supplies a solved family: no bang-bang or two-well-hop construction, no integer-secular-period timing condition beyond the idealized impulsive case, and no invariant-based inverse-engineered trajectory. The only anchor is the published Sterk statement that one shortcut-to-adiabaticity trajectory is the lowest-order member of its Bézier family, near the boundary of the feasible space. Closing this needs Torrontegui et al. 2011, Fürst et al. NJP 16, 075007 (2014) and Kaushal et al. (2020), none of which was read.
- **Quantum dynamics through the splitting critical point are absent.** Every splitting treatment in the set is classical, with phonon numbers obtained post hoc as ΔE/(ħω_f) - an explicit ceiling, not a state. Missing: the second-quantized two-ion Hamiltonian through the critical point, the mode structure where the external well is purely quartic (the harmonic normal-mode picture fails there), the squeezing generated by the frequency collapse to ω_CP, and any wavefunction-level criterion for when the classical treatment fails, beyond the empirical remark that the Lamb-Dicke regime is regained above roughly 40 µs. Section 4.6's two-ion Hessian at the critical point (COM = ω_CP exactly, stretch stiffer by √(5/3)) is the plan's own derivation, corroborated only by another trap's simulated 880/700 kHz ratio of 1.257, and is tagged **[background]** pending an independent re-check.
- **The splitting adiabatic-suppression constant is undefined in its source.** The exponent constant of δE′ = δE exp[c²(1 − 1/χ)] occurs exactly once in the whole e-print, inside that formula, and cannot be evaluated from the paper; it is an O(1) fit parameter, with c ≈ 0.93 the value that reproduces the source's own statement that single-phonon splitting needs several hundred microseconds. Relatedly, the printed impulsive chain and the printed closed form differ by exactly 4, so the printed π²/8 must be used as a pair with the fitted ξ² = 0.1 and never re-derived.
- **The published error-function transport quanta figures do not reproduce.** The factor 2 in the erf suppression amplitude was independently re-derived and is correct as printed, yet with it the source's own sub-quantum claims fail (γ = 3.259 at y = 12 for 400 µm, γ = 4.322 at y = 13 for 2.2 mm). Both come out right only if the 2 is dropped, so the paper's numerics almost certainly lost it. The equations are the regression targets and the two quanta figures are recorded as disagreements; the design conclusion (a smooth ramp beats a sine ramp by roughly an order of magnitude in duration) survives, since the defect is only about 3% in the control parameter.
- **No electrode basis potentials exist in any Run 5 source.** No published basis set, no objective function or regularization for the constrained voltage solve, no derivation of the residual acceleration from geometry, and no coefficient rows for the alternative trap geometries a regression suite would need. One source's boundary-element model is unpublished and accurate only to 10% in axial frequency; another simulated exactly one trap. The dominant real-world excitation channel - residual force and curvature from finite electrode structure - therefore has no quantitative model, and the practically dominant failure mode, a 3 mV offset near the critical point driving n̄ above 15, cannot be predicted. Section 4.1.6's gapless-plane layer is the plan's own substitute and carries its own **[background]** tag.
- **No bridge from residual quanta to a circuit-level error model.** Nothing maps a residual n̄ or a residual coherent displacement onto Mølmer-Sørensen or light-shift gate infidelity, onto spin dephasing accumulated during a shuttle, or onto a Kraus channel; one source cites the claim that single quanta of coherent excitation cost fidelity and gives no formula. There is no composition rule for the hundreds of transports in a schedule, and the inter-segment motional-phase bookkeeping (which needs the unimodular phases the closed forms drop) is unspecified. The one published two-qubit budget carries no transport row and does not close against its own measurement (an upper bound of <7.2 × 10⁻³ against a measured zone average of 7.9(4) × 10⁻³, with rows summing to 7.3 × 10⁻³), so the absence of a transport row is not evidence that transport heating is negligible; the plan carries the residue as a per-gate modelling uncertainty of order 10⁻³ rather than resolving it.
- **Mixed-species transport and splitting have no model.** The only statement in the set is that a different sympathetic-cooling species "will require special consideration since all modes will be excited during both diabatic transport and separation". There are no mass-ratio mode-participation expressions, no mixed-species normal-mode spectra for the crystals actually used (needed both to turn one fitted temperature into per-mode occupations and to model sympathetic cooling rates, which scale with the coolant eigenvector amplitude), no species-dependent well positions, and no differential pseudopotential barrier at a junction. Palmero et al. PRA 90, 053408 (2014) is cited but was not read.
- **Micromotion is absent from both splitting frameworks and unquantified at a junction apex.** Both splitting treatments assume the ions sit on the rf node with tight radial confinement persisting throughout, so neither contains a pseudopotential term or any micromotion at all: no Mathieu treatment, no radial-axial coupling during transport or splitting, no tilt-induced mode rotation and no radial-mode excitation, even though scaling the axial frequency necessarily moves the radials. At a junction apex, where the axial rf field and hence the micromotion are maximal, the only quoted amplitude is a model estimate at a deliberately low-gradient zone 880 µm away, and that number is not self-consistent in one peak-or-rms convention.
- **The inter-segment state hand-off is unspecified.** The closed-form amplitude solution holds only at constant frequency and the squeezing parameters depend on the past evolution, but no source prescribes how to propagate the Ermakov state (ρ, ρ̇, μ), the accumulated squeezing (δ, θ) and the coherent phase across a transport, a split, a junction and a merge - which is exactly what accumulating squeezing over a multi-segment schedule requires. Section 4.6 specifies that carry-across as the plan's own construction.
- **Junction coverage is single-apparatus and dated.** One source family supplies the rf pseudopotential barriers, the apex axial anti-confinement, the frequency schedule and two technical noise channels, and its junction is a two-wafer three-dimensional design with rf bridges rather than a modern surface-electrode junction. Missing: surface-trap junction barrier structure, an explicit-Ω_rf or Floquet treatment of micromotion at the apex, the excitation cost of crossing a large-micromotion region, and two-dimensional transport with rotating principal axes. The DAC-comb artefact that dominated the measured excitation is 2011 hardware and is specified as an optional term.
- **The splitting voltage recipe is not geometry-general, and its counterexample is a surface trap.** Its source assumes that "any reasonable segmented trap geometry will exhibit similar characteristics", yet its own tabulated surface trap with a segmented ground plane has the centre and splitting quartic coefficients sign-flipped relative to every three-dimensional design, and the authors warn that the quartic Taylor truncation itself breaks down there. This bears directly on Section 4.1.6, which is the plan's surface-electrode layer. Relatedly, the quartic expansion is used at or past its own stated validity edge: it holds while the well separation is small against the segment width, but the critical-point separation is already a sizeable fraction of the segment spacing and the final separation exceeds it, with no error estimate supplied.
- **Splitting excitation has no external validation and N > 2 is unquantified.** The measured budgets present are 1.4 to 2.1 quanta per two-ion split with partial de-excitability and a simulated floor near one phonon at tens of microseconds; a nine-ion partitioning result reports reliability across all combinations but no excitation numbers, and one two-ion transport result is qualitative only. Ruster et al. 2014, Kaufmann et al. 2017, Kaushal et al. 2020 and the Quantinuum QCCD reports would be the external checks and were not read.
- **Transport-induced decoherence and recooling are unmodelled.** Every Run 5 transport entry is unitary apart from an ambient-heating rate: no dephasing rate during transport, no spin-motion entanglement from the waveform, no magnetic-field-gradient phase along the path, and no coherence budget per shuttle beyond one spin-echo 1/e time and one qualitative Ramsey verification. No cooling-time budget as a function of the excitation to be removed is given, and no source reports a post-cooling occupation for any cooling stage, so the residual n̄ entering a gate is a free parameter.
- **Transport failure and reordering rates are unquantified, with a known undetected path.** The sources describe such events only as "rare" and "automatically detected and scrubbed", and the crystal-mode-structure probe used to catch them is explicitly blind to events that swap the qubit ions and to multiple reorderings that restore the correct order before the probe. The heralded-erasure channel of Section 4.6 therefore has no rate and a known undetected-error path with no bound.
- **None of the Run 5 transport corrections could be checked against a version of record.** Every verification was against arXiv preprints, publisher pages having been unreachable during the run, and the splitting source exists as arXiv v1 only. About ten printed defects are corrected across Section 4.6; each should be re-checked against the journal text before it enters a code comment, and each is brief-derived rather than a published erratum.

**What the derivation audit does not establish (2026-09-04).** The audit re-derived equations from the plan's stated conventions and confirmed or corrected them numerically; it did not check citations against the source PDFs, so the provenance claims about printed errors (Wineland's Eq. 62, Brownnutt's Eq. 14, Turchette's Eq. 2) rest on the errata check and on internal consistency; it left every empirical species input unverified (ε_sys presets, background rates, measured branching ratios, reduced-element values are ingest data, not derivable quantities, and both derivers of the readout rows said so); it audited equations, not the code that will implement them; four rows rest on one blind derivation and one on one adjudicator, and about fifteen setups were judged insufficient, so a handful of confirmations rest on a guessed convention that happened to match. Five items it left open are recorded here so that they are not mistaken for settled: which triangle of the displacement matrix ⟨n′|D|n⟩ a plan sentence calls 'above' the diagonal (now stated explicitly in Section 5.1.1, but the modulus is transpose-symmetric and nothing computable depends on it); the 171Yb+ P₁/₂ → D₃/₂ reduced element, whose value hinges on using the 2.438 µm channel wavelength rather than the 935 nm repump (Section 4.5.6 now pins it); the number of digits at which the 87Rb reference elements are quoted, which follow the stored lifetime (Section 4.5.6); the sign branch of the entangling angle, which Section 4.4.3 now states as |χ| = π/4 modulo π/2 with the compiler's sign; and the apparatus quantities of Section 8 (detection efficiencies, background rates, the D₃/₂ branching), which no derivation can supply. The two equations that lost their comparer to the output caps, the transverse Hessian of Section 4.1.3 and the emission angular factors of Section 4.2.8, keep their earlier tags and the committed check scripts behind them (`check_recoil.py`, `check_surface_mixed.py`), and are the first candidates for a second audit pass.

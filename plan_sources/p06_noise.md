## 6. Noise and error models

### 6.1 Principles

Every error channel in the simulator is a physical process with a rate or spectrum derived from device parameters, entering the dynamics in one of four ways: (a) as a term in the Hamiltonian (crosstalk light, Stark shifts, beam-curvature coupling, coherent over-rotation); (b) as a Lindblad collapse operator (heating, fast dephasing, photon scattering, shelf decay); (c) as a quasi-static parameter sampled once per dynamical sample (slow magnetic-field drift, mode-frequency drift, Rabi-frequency drift, beam-phase drift); (d) as a stochastic time series in a drive or Hamiltonian coefficient (laser phase and intensity noise with a given spectrum, and the qubit-frequency and mode-frequency trajectories of Sections 6.2 and 6.3, including deterministic periodic components such as mains harmonics with a per-shot line-trigger phase); (e) as a discrete event process, the background-gas collisions of Section 6.7 that heat, reorder or lose ions and are reported as heralded or discarded shots. The choice among (b), (c) and (d) for a given physical noise is set by its correlation time relative to the gate: white noise is Lindblad, noise slow compared with a shot is quasi-static, anything in between is a sampled process (the band from 1 Hz to 10 kHz, where every laboratory's magnetic dephasing and every decoupling sequence live, is (d), not (c)). Phenomenological circuit-level channels (depolarizing, Pauli-twirled over-rotation) are not used inside the simulator; they are what the simulator produces when asked to summarize its own behaviour for a circuit-level client (Section 6.8). Every rate carries its provenance (apparatus, species, chain length), because the published numbers come from different machines: Fang's 614 quanta/s centre-of-mass heating and 8(1) ms motional coherence on a five-ion chain, Cetina's 88(6) quanta/s with S_E ∝ ω^{−0.8(1)} and no absolute normalization on a 15-ion chain, Trout's simulated 25 quanta/s. Fed into Sørensen-Mølmer's or Ballance's heating formula, Lechner's 65 quanta/s gives an error of 8 × 10⁻³ for a 250 µs two-ion gate, ten to seventy times the fidelities quoted alongside it, which is what mixing apparatus does. An N-ion error budget assembled from them is stitched from at least three apparatus, and the report says so **[extracted]**.

### 6.2 Motional channels

**Heating.** Collapse operators √(Γ(N̄+1)) a_m and √(ΓN̄) a_m† with Γ_h = ΓN̄ from S_E(ω_m) (Section 4.1.5) **[verified]**. The two closed forms the tests reproduce: ε_h = ṅ t_g/(2K) for a K-loop geometric-phase gate coupled to an infinite-temperature bath (Ballance 2016 supplement; adopted by Trout 2018 as an XX Kraus channel with p_h = r_heat t_MS) **[verified]**, and ΔF = Γ_h t_g/2 for the single-loop Mølmer-Sørensen gate (Kirchmair 2009 after Sørensen-Mølmer 2000) **[verified]**; Sørensen-Mølmer's own treatment gives the displaced jump operators and the resulting J_y-basis dephasing rate ∝ (M_y − M_y′)²Γ(1 + 2n_th)(G² + F²)/4 **[verified]**. Ballance states the closed form only for ε_h ≪ 0.1, a caveat Trout drops, and Trout's K is quoted inconsistently between sections (1 and 2 in one, 2 and 4 in another), so the simulator carries K per pulse solution and refuses the closed form outside its stated range **[corrected]**. Multi-ion correlation of the noise (uniform versus uncorrelated fields) selects which modes heat (Section 4.1.5); Cetina et al. show that under uniform-field heating the lowest axial mode dominates, with the chain's beam-curvature dephasing (below) growing as t_w²N^{4+2α} **[verified]**.

**Motional dephasing.** Two representations. Lindblad: L = a†a√(2/τ), which makes the coherence of |n⟩ + |n′⟩ decay at (n − n′)²/τ and gives a gate error ε_d = α_K t_g/τ with α_K = (8K + 3)/(16K²) = {0.6875, 0.2969, 0.1367} for K = {1, 2, 4} (Ballance 2016 prints 0.686, 0.297, 0.137; Section 4.4.4) **[verified]**, **[corrected: derivation audit, 2026-09-04]**; the simulator uses this normalization and calibrates τ from a motional Ramsey or, as Srinivas et al. did, from squeezing analysis **[corrected minor]**. Quasi-static: per-sample mode-frequency offsets drawn from the drift distribution, which is what Hughes et al. model as Ĥ_e = ħε(t)a†a with the exact two-term infidelity, thermal spin-motion (2n̄ + 1)|α_t|²λ² plus a temperature-insensitive gate-angle term **[verified]**, and what FM/AMFM stabilization is designed against **[verified]**. Srinivas's anchor: motional coherence time 64 ms costing 5.8 × 10⁻⁴ on a 740 µs gate; frequency feedforward residual σ ≈ 50 Hz costing 3 × 10⁻⁵ **[corrected minor]**.

**Spectator modes and Debye-Waller factors** need no separate channel in JOINT_EXACT mode: they are in the Hamiltonian. The closed forms they must reproduce are Bermudez et al.'s ε_m (Section 4.4.7), Landsman's δF = (4/5)Σ_{j,m}|α_{j,m}|²(2n̄_m + 1) **[verified]**, Sørensen-Mølmer's F = 1 − π²N(N−1)η⁴Var(n)/8 **[verified]**, and Ballance's ε_n̄ = (1/4)π²η⁴n̄(2n̄ + 1) **[verified]** (n̄(2n̄ + 1) is ⟨n²⟩ of the thermal state, not its variance). In GATE_LOCAL mode the dropped modes' contribution is estimated from these forms and added to the report. The cubic Coulomb coupling of Section 4.1 is a further coherent motional channel, present in no published budget, whose leading off-resonant effect is a second-order dispersive shift of order g²/Δ_res, of order 10⁻² rad or less over published gate times (Section 4.1.4; the first version of this plan reported a first-order phase g·t of 0.1 to 0.9 rad, corrected on 2026-09-04); it is an opt-in Hamiltonian term whose second-order estimate and resonance distance are reported by default **[corrected]**. Mode-frequency drift has two components with different correlation structure, and the distinction is the whole point of the FM and AM-FM robustness the plan reproduces: a common-mode fractional drift of every rf-derived mode of a family from rf-amplitude drift (ω_sec ∝ q ∝ V_rf, so 10⁻⁴ in V_rf moves every radial mode by 300 Hz at 3 MHz, the common δ against which ∂α_k/∂δ is nulled), and a per-mode differential term from dc drift; each has a correlation time and is sampled as a trajectory or a quasi-static value by the rule of Section 6.1, never as M independent offsets, because independent sampling makes closure errors average as 1/√M instead of adding and lets robustness tests pass for the wrong reason. rf amplitude and phase noise spectra S_V(ω) and S_φ(ω) are device parameters that drive the Ω_rf ± ω_t sideband heating of Section 4.1.5 and a parametric-heating term at 2ω_sec (added after the 2026-09-04 experimentalist critique) **[corrected]**.

**Beam-curvature dephasing** (Cetina et al. 2022) **[verified]**: an ion oscillating along the chain axis through a tightly focused addressing beam samples the beam's field-amplitude curvature, Ω̄_i = Ω_{i,0} + ½Ω″_{i,0}Σ_m b_{im}²E_m/(Mω_m²); with thermal mode energies this gives the decay parameter θ_{im} = −b_{im}²ξ_m²(Ω″_{i,0}/Ω_{i,0})n̄_m, a carrier Rabi curve p₁(t) = [1 − C cos(Ω_{i,0}t − φ)]/2 with C = Π_m(1 + θ_{im}²Ω²t²)^{−1/2} and φ = Σ_m arctan(θ_{im}Ωt) (a phase lag; the source's Eq. 2 prints the wrong sign **[corrected]**), and a two-qubit bound F_ij = ½ + ½Π_m[1 + (N_gπ/2)²(θ_{im} + θ_{jm})²]^{−1/2} after N_g gates **[verified]**. For a Gaussian beam Ω″/Ω = −(2/w²)(1 − 2x²/w²) **[verified]**. This is not a Debye-Waller effect (the beams are perpendicular to the motion) **[corrected]**; the simulator implements it exactly by making Ω depend on the axial position operator, Ω(x̂) = Ω₀(1 + (Ω″/2Ω₀)x̂²), which couples the drive to a†a of the axial modes, and reproduces the closed forms as tests. This mechanism and the spectator-mode Debye-Waller factor of Section 4.3 are distinct sources of shot-to-shot Rabi-frequency inhomogeneity: the Debye-Waller factor involves the modes along Δk with exponent η²(n̄ + ½) and a thermal Fock distribution; the curvature term involves the modes perpendicular to the beam with exponent κ = b²ξ²(Ω″/Ω)n̄, about 120 times smaller than a k-vector reading would give, and exponentially distributed classical mode energies with an algebraic contrast envelope. Neither is Gaussian or Markovian. They get two separate per-shot samplers on disjoint mode sets, both feeding the same frozen-Ω propagation, and neither may stand in for the other **[corrected]**.

### 6.3 Qubit dephasing

Magnetic-field noise shifts a hyperfine level by g_F μ_B m_F/h, 1.4 MHz/G per unit m_F for g_F = 1 (the F = 1 manifold of 171Yb+), so that a Δm_F = ±1 transition from the clock state shifts by 1.4 MHz/G and a Zeeman qubit between m_J = ±½ levels by 2.8 MHz/G (the first version of this plan wrote 2.8 MHz/G per unit m_F; caught by the 2026-09-04 critique), and clock qubits only quadratically: written as δν = c₂(δB)², c₂ = 310.8 Hz/G² for 171Yb+ at zero field and 1.21 mHz/mG² for the 43Ca+ |4,0⟩ ↔ |3,+1⟩ transition at 146.094 G, whose second derivative d²ν/dB² = 2c₂ is Harty's printed 2.4 mHz/mG² (the second revision paired the two numbers as if they shared a convention, which the derivation audit of 2026-09-04 caught; the Section 13 'curvature naming' row is the rule) **[corrected: derivation audit, 2026-09-04]**; both are outputs of the hyperfine-Zeeman diagonalization of Section 4.5.1 (310.85 Hz/G² and c₂ = 1.2077 mHz/mG², d²ν/dB² = 2.415 mHz/mG², recomputed), so a field sample is converted to per-level frequency offsets through computed sensitivities. Slow noise is quasi-static: per-sample frequency offsets drawn from the measured or configured distribution, correlated across ions with a correlation length ξ_c so that the gate error goes as Nt_g/(2T₂) (uncorrelated) or N²t_g/(4T₂) (global), with T₂ the single-ion 1/e coherence time (Bermudez 2017 prints 2t_gN/T₂ and 2t_gN²/T₂, 4 and 8 times high; Section 4.4.7) **[corrected: derivation audit, 2026-09-04]**; fast noise is a Lindblad σ_z term; and between them the qubit frequency has a sampled trajectory Δ_i(t) on the array-coefficient machinery of Section 6.1's option (d), built from S_B(ω) through the computed sensitivities plus a deterministic periodic component (amplitude per mains harmonic at 50 or 60 Hz and its multiples) with a per-shot line-trigger phase, uniform when the sequence is not line-synchronized and fixed when it is, because that band dominates magnetic dephasing in every laboratory and is where every decoupling sequence operates, so that Ramsey revivals, the sequence-length dependence of echo and the T₂ of (XY)^N decoupling are reproducible; the validation is a filter-function calculation of T₂ under (XY)^N for the configured spectrum, on the machinery and the χ = (2/π)∫₀^∞(dω/ω²)S_b F normalization of Section 6.9 (Section 9.7), and the Egan and Harty anchors below are compared only against a spectrum that contains their mains lines (the first version of this plan had no representation of this band; caught by the 2026-09-04 experimentalist critique) **[corrected]**. Anchors: T₂* = 610(120) ms from an un-echoed Raman Ramsey on the centre ion of a 15-ion chain, attributed to control noise; T₂ = 2.84(16) s with Gaussian decay under microwave (XY)^N decoupling (Egan 2021) **[verified]**; 50(10) s for a 43Ca+ clock qubit without shielding (Harty 2014) **[verified]**; > 0.5 s for Debnath's 5-ion chain **[verified]**; and Crain's Gaussian spectator dephasing during readout (Section 8.5). Microwave gates at the intrinsic-dynamical-decoupling point J₀(4Ω_μ/δ) = 0 suppress low-frequency dephasing by construction **[verified]**. Laser phase noise matters for phase-sensitive gates only (Section 4.4.2); it is represented as an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck realization of φ(t) in the drive coefficient with the configured linewidth, and its effect is checked against the Kirchmair anchor of 2 × 10⁻³ per gate from incoherent carrier excitation **[corrected minor]** and the Baldwin filter-function form ε = ∫S_φ(ω)F(ω)dω scaled by (4g²/Δ²)² **[extracted]**.

### 6.4 Amplitude and control errors

Slow Rabi-frequency variation is a per-sample scale factor (δΩ/Ω ≈ 1.4 × 10⁻² in Kirchmair 2009 **[verified]**; ≲ 5 × 10⁻⁴ in Harty 2014 **[verified]**); fast intensity noise with zero-frequency density Γ_I gives ε_I ≈ Γ_I t_g η²(n̄ + ½) + Γ_I t_g η² · 3(N − 1)/(8K) (Bermudez 2017 prints (N − 1)/4 for the second term; Section 4.4.7) **[corrected: derivation audit, 2026-09-04]**. Static pulse-area and detuning errors are the composite-pulse model R = exp[−(i/2)θ(1 + ε)(σ_φ + δZ)] (Mount 2015) **[verified]**, which is Section 4.3.5's primitive M(θ, φ; ε_a, ε_d) with ε = ε_a and δ = ε_d, and whose frequency-resolved counterpart, together with the quasi-static-versus-sampled decision of Section 6.1, is Section 6.9; the frequency-comb beat notes at δ′ and 2δ′ with weights 1 : 2 : 1 are included for comb-driven devices **[verified]**. Coherent over-rotation of any gate, ε_G(ρ) = e^{−iθG}ρe^{iθG}, is the model Egan et al. used to reproduce their logical-qubit data **[verified]**; the simulator produces it naturally from a miscalibrated pulse area, and the Pauli-twirled equivalent p_xx = sin²α (Trout 2018) **[verified]** is a reporting option, not a simulation primitive. Timing: Harty's 14 µs dead times between pulses and the +4.5 Hz deliberate detuning are the kind of scheduler detail the error model must carry to reproduce randomized benchmarking at the 10⁻⁶ level **[verified]**.

### 6.5 Photon scattering and leakage

Rates from Section 4.3.2 (Ozeri 2005, 2007) **[verified]**, computed for every species from the amplitude sums of Section 4.5.5: Raman scattering as spin-flip Lindblad operators between qubit levels and into the other hyperfine or Zeeman levels (leakage, requires d > 2, with the leaked levels' readout polarity taken from the manifold), Rayleigh scattering as a motional recoil kick (the recoil operator of Section 4.2.8, a displacement averaged over the emission pattern) with no internal-state effect when the two qubit states' elastic amplitudes coincide, as they do for clock qubits far from the fine-structure levels **[verified]**, the differential-Rayleigh dephasing channel of Section 4.5.5 when they do not (Uys et al. 2010, Run 4), and D-level scattering with branching f as leakage to a shelved level that the readout model then treats as dark or bright according to the species **[verified]**. The error per π pulse is P_total = (πγ/ω_f)(2Δ² + (Δ − ω_f)²)/|Δ(Δ − ω_f)| with ε_S = P_Raman **[verified]**. The photon-scattering line was the largest term in Ballance's budget (0.4 × 10⁻³) **[verified]** and second in Pino's (1.5 × 10⁻³) **[extracted]**.

### 6.6 Addressing crosstalk

Individually addressed beams leak onto neighbours. The crosstalk ratio is a Rabi-frequency ratio ε_ij = Ω_j/Ω_i measured by driving single-qubit gates on ion i (1-4% in Fang et al.'s 5-ion 171Yb+ chain; below 0.5% two sites away), and the leaked drives add as vectors in the spin plane so the spectator's effective Rabi frequency and phase are Ω_jσ_{φ_j}^{(j)} = ε_{1j}Ω₁σ_{φ₁}^{(j)} + ε_{2j}Ω₂σ_{φ₂}^{(j)} with φ₁ = 0, φ₂ = φ_beam **[verified]** (the index convention is beam i onto ion j **[corrected]**). During an MS gate the leading-order effect is U_xtalk = XX(θ)·exp[−iΣ_{j≠1,2}(θ_{1j}X^{(1)}σ_{φ_j}^{(j)} + θ_{2j}X^{(2)}σ_{φ_j}^{(j)})] **[verified]**; the printed closed forms for the Bell fidelity and the spectator excitation are off by a factor 2 inside the cosines relative to the paper's own Eq. 1. The exact forms are F = cos²θ₁₃ cos²θ₂₃ and P_ion3 = [1 − cos(2θ₁₃)cos(2θ₂₃)]/2, which give 0.900790 and 0.097217 at (θ₁₃, θ₂₃) = (0.1644, −0.2763), independent of φ_beam, against the printed 0.974422 and 0.025450; at θ = π/2 the exact form gives P_ion3 = 0 where the printed one gives 0.5. The small-angle infidelity coefficient is therefore θ², not θ²/4, a factor 4 in every crosstalk budget built on the printed forms **[corrected]**. Because φ_beam drifts on hundreds of milliseconds **[corrected]**, it is a quasi-static sample. Suppression schemes are scheduler options: neighbour suppression (split the gate, Z(π) on affected spectators) and local suppression (split the gate, Y(π) or Z(π) on the two targets), both exact to first order **[verified]**. The simulator implements crosstalk exactly by adding the leaked drive terms to the Hamiltonian; Wright et al.'s minimal model (3% bit flips into |0⟩ applied twice for Bernstein-Vazirani; 1% either way applied five times for hidden shift; 0.2% detection misidentification) is a test of what emerges **[corrected]**. Parallel gates on two pairs incur an additional commuting unitary with ½‖ℰ‖◇ ≤ Σ|Θ_{rs}| over the four inter-pair phases, which fall as 1/n³ with separation (Landsman 2019) **[corrected: the ½ is load-bearing]**; the bound is vacuous for closely spaced pairs because ‖ℰ‖◇ ≤ 2 always, so the simulator reports the exact simulated channel rather than the bound whenever the bound exceeds 1 **[corrected]**. Beam-curvature dephasing (Section 6.2) and readout crosstalk (Section 8.5) complete the addressing picture; the intensity crosstalk of integrated waveguides (0.26-0.36% at 200 µm) is the corresponding number for photonic delivery **[extracted]**.

### 6.7 Idle time, memory and preparation

Between pulses the state evolves under H₀ with heating, motional dephasing and qubit dephasing active; identity gates in benchmarking are delays (Harty) **[verified]**. Preparation error comes from the optical-pumping model (Section 4.2.6) and is reported as part of SPAM; Egan's single-ion budget (bright-state error 0.71(4)% dominated by bright-to-dark pumping 0.55% in a 100 µs window; dark-state error 0.22(2)% from dark-to-bright pumping 0.13% and background 0.07%) is a validation target for the readout model of Section 8 **[verified]**. Two SPAM figures coexist in the same papers: Egan and Wright each report about 99.3% from randomized-benchmarking fits and 99.80% from a one-sided microwave measurement, and their algorithm budgets use the former; mixing them changes a ten-qubit budget by about 5%, so every SPAM number in the results object is labelled with the definition it follows **[corrected]**.

**Background-gas collisions and crystal events** (added after the 2026-09-04 experimentalist critique) **[background]**, **[recomputed here]**. None of the channels above lets a shot fail the way real shots fail. A Langevin collision with background gas occurs at the rate Γ_L = n_bg k_L per ion with k_L = (e/(2ε₀))√(α_p/μ_r) (α_p the neutral's SI polarizability, μ_r the reduced mass); for H₂ (polarizability volume 0.80 Å³) on a heavy ion k_L = 1.5 × 10⁻⁹ cm³ s⁻¹, so at 10⁻¹¹ torr (n_bg = 3.2 × 10⁵ cm⁻³ at 300 K) Γ_L = 4.8 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ per ion, one event per 35 minutes per ion and one per minute for a 30-ion chain (`check_collisions.py`). The device carries a pressure, a gas composition and outcome probabilities, and the collision channel is a Poisson process (Section 6.1's option (e)) with three outcomes: a heating kick drawn from a configured energy distribution whose scale is the neutral's thermal energy times the mass ratio, tens to thousands of quanta, which melts the crystal and is followed by recooling within the sequence's cooling stages; a reorder, sampled from a configured permutation distribution when the kick exceeds the ordering barrier, which permutes the ion labels and with them every b_{i,m}, every η_{i,m} and the pair sign s of Section 7.7, so that a crystal that reorders mid-sequence runs the rest of the sequence with the wrong mode structure until the next calibration detects it; and a loss or dark-ion event, molecule formation or, for 171Yb+, population of the F₇/₂ level (cleared at 638.6 nm, Section 4.5), which leaves the ion dark until repumped or reloaded. Results carry per-shot heralds (collision event, all-dark detection, count anomaly) and a discarded-shot count; a transport failure or an undetected ion swap under milestone M12 (Section 4.6) is the same channel with an unknown rate, since the published QCCD data removed such shots rather than modelling them **[corrected]**, so that the shot distribution has the outlier tail a laboratory sees; the melt and recrystallization dynamics themselves are a non-goal (Section 1.3), the event being instantaneous with its outcome distribution.

### 6.8 Reporting to circuit-level clients

The simulator can summarize any gate's simulated channel as (i) an average gate infidelity from process tomography of the simulated pulse, (ii) a Pauli-twirled channel, and (iii) a depolarizing rate ε with the normalization Λ_ε(ρ) = (1 − ε)ρ + ε/(4ⁿ − 1)Σ_{P≠I}PρP, in which ε equals the entanglement infidelity (Chen et al. 2023) **[verified]**. Bermudez et al.'s three-variant mapping of ε onto Pauli channels for fault-tolerance studies is provided as well **[verified]**. Reference points for such summaries: Trout et al.'s five-channel model at its fault-tolerance operating point (single-qubit over-rotation 10⁻⁴, two-qubit 10⁻³, heating 25 quanta/s as ṅ/2K, background depolarizing 8 × 10⁻⁴, dephasing 15 s⁻¹, gate time t_g = 10 + 38d µs with d the ion separation) **[verified]**; Chen et al.'s Forte medians (single-qubit 2.0 × 10⁻⁴, two-qubit 46.4 × 10⁻⁴ by direct RB, worst pair 885 × 10⁻⁴, no measurable dependence on ion separation) **[verified]**; and the error budgets tabulated in Section 4.4.7.

### 6.9 Dynamical decoupling and the filter-function description of dephasing and amplitude noise

Sections 6.2 to 6.4 give each channel a rate or a spectrum and Section 6.1 routes it into the dynamics. This subsection adds how a **control sequence** shapes its own susceptibility to a given spectrum, so that a decoupling sequence, a composite pulse or an idle is scored analytically against S_B(ω) or an amplitude spectrum before any trajectory is propagated. Run 5 read Green, Sastrawan, Uys and Biercuk 2013, Kabytayev et al. 2014 and Biercuk et al. 2009 for this layer and repaired six printed defects; the normalization is the most dangerous thing in the topic, so the pieces below travel together and are asserted against each other by a test.

**The toggling frame and the first-order overlap.** Split H(t) = H_c(t) + H₀(t) with H₀(t) = β(t)·σ a classical, zero-mean, wide-sense-stationary c-number field carrying **no** factor ½ on the Pauli vector, and factor the propagator exactly as U(t) = U_c(t)Ũ(t) with U_c the noise-free control propagator. Then H̃₀(t) = β(t)R(t)·σ with **R_ij(t) = ½Tr[U_c†(t)σ_i U_c(t)σ_j]**, a real element of SO(3) - strictly the adjoint of U_c†, not of U_c, so it is implemented from the trace formula and never from prose; the check script confirms R R^T = I with det = +1 and that for U_c = exp(−iΩtσ_x/2) the third row is (0, sin Ωt, cos Ωt) **[verified]**, **[recomputed here]**. Under piecewise-constant control R(t) = Σ_l G^{(l)}(t)R^{P_l}(t − t_{l−1})Λ^{(l−1)}, with G^{(l)} the segment boxcar, R^{P_l} the per-pulse matrix relative to the pulse start and Λ^{(l−1)}_ij = ½Tr[Q_{l−1}†σ_i Q_{l−1}σ_j] the adjoint of the propagator accumulated **before** segment l; Λ right-multiplies R^{P_l}, and substituting Q_l for Q_{l−1} or writing Λ·v for v·Λ each mis-computes the first Magnus term by about 3 × 10⁻³, the size of the noise itself **[verified]**. The frequency-domain matrix carries an **explicit −iω**: R_ij(ω) = −iω∫₀^τ dt R_ij(t)e^{+iωt}. Truncating at the first Magnus term and averaging then gives **1 − F_av ≃ (1/2π)∫_{−∞}^{∞}(dω/ω²)Σ_{ijk}S_ij(ω)R_jk(ω)R*_ik(ω)** (Green Eq. 23), the conjugate on R_ik and **not** on R_jk, with S_ij the two-sided cross-spectrum from ⟨β_i(t₁)β_j(t₂)⟩ = (1/2π)∫dω S_ij e^{iω(t₂−t₁)} and F_av ≡ ¼⟨|Tr Ũ(τ)|²⟩ the operational fidelity, which is Section 4.3.5's F_K and floors at ½ rather than 0 **[verified]**. The 1/ω² weight and the −iω are a **matched pair**: R_jk R*_ik scales as ω², the weight cancels it, and the net weighting on the bare Fourier transform of R(t) is ω⁰, so the control factor is finite at ω = 0; dropping the explicit 1/ω² in the belief that it is already inside R is an ω² error, and a 1/ω⁴ weight appears nowhere in this literature. Everything is angular frequency with exactly one 1/(2π), so the one-sided fold carries **1/π**, never 1/(2π). For pure dephasing β = (0, 0, b(t)) and F_z^{(1)}(ω) = Σ_{i=x,y,z}|R_zi(ω)|² (the two-term |R_zy|² + |R_zz|² is specific to the source's own xy-axis pulses), whence ⟨a₁²⟩ = (1/π)∫₀^∞(dω/ω²)S_b F_z^{(1)} and 1 − F_av = ⟨a₁²⟩ + O(ξ⁴); the two quadratures add incoherently only because ⟨β_aβ_d⟩ = 0 for jointly Gaussian processes. Validity is ξ² ≪ 1 with ξ² = (τ²/π)Σ_i∫₀^{ω_ci}S_i dω plus Magnus convergence **[verified]**. Green's per-pulse closed form for the dephasing row (Eq. 40) needs an explicit ½ on its Tr(σ_{φ_l}σ_zσ_j) term that the paper drops, so the printed version overshoots both transverse components by exactly 2.0000 while R_zz is right; the corrected components match direct quadrature to 3 × 10⁻⁶¹ and reproduce the paper's own primitive-π_X forms, which alone discriminate **[corrected]**, **[recomputed here]**. Its poles at ω = ±Ω_l are removable (guard on ‖ω| − |Ω_l‖), and free evolution and a pure z rotation are the one degenerate case θ_l = 0 **and** Ω_l = 0 together, whose angle still propagates through Q_{l−1} into Λ^{(l−1)} (Section 13).

**S_b is the PSD of the σ_z coefficient, and that is a factor 4.** Fix once for the whole simulator: H_deph(t) = b(t)σ_z with **no** ½, so b(t) is **half** the qubit-splitting fluctuation and the noise-accumulated relative phase is φ(t) = 2∫₀^t b dt′; S_b is the two-sided PSD of b under the 1/(2π) inverse-transform convention.

### 4.3 Spin-motion interaction Hamiltonian and single-qubit control

This is the core of the simulator: one Hamiltonian builder that every pulse goes through. The conventions below are the simulator's; Section 13 lists the alternatives the sources use.

#### 4.3.1 The Hamiltonian the builder implements

For N ions with internal transition frequency ω₀, modes m with frequencies ω_m and ladder operators a_m, and a running-wave drive on ion i with Rabi frequency Ω_i, effective wavevector projection δk along the motional axis, frequency ω_L and phase φ_i, the lab-frame Hamiltonian is (Monroe 2020 Eqs. 6-7; RMP 2003 Eqs. 60-63; Wineland 2003 Eq. 2.1) **[verified]**

H_lab = (ω₀/2) Σ_i σ_z^i + Σ_m ω_m a_m†a_m − Σ_i d̂_i · E(X̂_i),  E_i = E_{i0} cos(k X̂_i − ω_L t − φ_i),

with ħ = 1 and X̂_i = X̄_i + Σ_m b_{im} ξ_m^{(0)}(a_m + a_m†), ξ_m^{(0)} = √(ħ/2Mω_m). Moving to the frame of the qubit and applying the optical rotating-wave approximation (dropping e^{±i(ω_L+ω₀)t}, valid for ω_L, ω₀ ≫ Ω) leaves the exact spin-motion interaction (Monroe Eq. 8, in the interaction picture for the motion) **[verified]**

H = Σ_i (Ω_i/2) σ₊^i e^{i(δk X̄_i − μ_i t − δφ_i)} Π_m exp[iη_{im}(a_m† e^{iω_m t} + a_m e^{−iω_m t})] + h.c.,

where μ_i = ω_L − ω₀ is the detuning from the carrier and η_{im} = b_{im} δk ξ_m^{(0)} the Lamb-Dicke parameter of ion i on mode m (Section 4.1.3). The simulator keeps the motion in the Schrödinger picture instead (Section 5.2), so the operator Π_m exp[iη_{im}(a_m + a_m†)] = Π_m D_m(iη_{im}) is time independent and the time dependence sits in the scalar coefficient (Ω_i(t)/2) e^{−i(μ_i t − φ_i(t))}. Resonances occur when μ_i = Σ_m ℓ_m ω_m for integers ℓ_m, the sideband of total order p = Σ|ℓ_m| carrying a prefactor of order η^p/√(p!), since ⟨p|D(iη)|0⟩ = e^{−η²/2}(iη)^p/√(p!) **[corrected; the earlier η^p/p! understates the third and fourth sidebands by 2.4 and 4.9 times at η = 0.1, and the truncation and mode-dropping heuristics use the exact element rather than the scaling]**. The RMP 2003 form of the same expansion carries the micromotion sidebands through the Floquet function u(t); as printed, its Eqs. 67-68 and the sentence "β_x ω_rf ≈ ν" drop a factor 1/2 relative to the paper's own Eq. 37 (ν = β_x ω_rf/2), which would double the secular sideband spacing in the micromotion comb **[corrected]**.

**Exact matrix elements.** For a single mode the coupling between |↓, n⟩ and |↑, n′⟩ is (Wineland 1998 Eq. 18; RMP 2003 Eq. 70) **[verified]**

⟨n′| e^{iη(a+a†)} |n⟩ = ⟨n′|D(iη)|n⟩ = e^{−η²/2} (iη)^{|n′−n|} (n_<!/n_>!)^{1/2} L^{|n′−n|}_{n_<}(η²),  Ω_{n′,n} = Ω |⟨n′|D(iη)|n⟩| = Ω e^{−η²/2} (n_<!/n_>!)^{1/2} |η|^{|n′−n|} |L^{|n′−n|}_{n_<}(η²)|,

exact in η for one running-wave drive and one mode, the signed element being what the builder uses and the modulus what a Rabi frequency means (the first two revisions equated the modulus to the signed Laguerre expression, which is negative for odd |n′ − n| at negative η and beyond the polynomial's first zero, already at n = 6 to 8 for η = 0.5; caught by the derivation audit of 2026-09-04) **[corrected: derivation audit, 2026-09-04]**. In the Lamb-Dicke regime, η√⟨(a+a†)²⟩ ≪ 1 (a state-dependent condition), this reduces to Ω for the carrier, ηΩ√n for the first red and ηΩ√(n+1) for the first blue sideband **[verified]**, and the first-order Hamiltonian is (RMP Eq. 72) H_LD = (ħ/2)Ω₀σ₊{1 + iη(a e^{−iνt} + a†e^{iνt})}e^{i(φ−δt)} + h.c., with the red sideband a Jaynes-Cummings and the blue an anti-Jaynes-Cummings coupling **[verified]**. Because the builder uses D(iη) exactly, the Debye-Waller factors are included automatically: the carrier operator on each spectator mode is D̂_{im} = e^{−η_{im}²/2} L_{n_m}(η_{im}²) ≈ 1 − η_{im}²(n_m + 1/2) + … (Monroe Eq. 11) **[verified]**, its thermal average is exp[−η_p²(n̄_p + 1/2)] per mode, and Wineland 1998 Eqs. 122-127 give both the mean and the rms shot-to-shot fluctuation of the Rabi frequency when spectator modes are thermal and frozen during a run (their worked example: 100 modes at n̄ = 0.1, η = 0.01) **[verified]**. The sideband Hamiltonians with driven-mode and spectator-mode Debye-Waller operators are Monroe Eqs. 16-19 **[verified]**.

**Two-level propagators for tests.** Within the subspace {|↓,n⟩, |↑,n′⟩} at detuning Δ from the (n′−n)-th sideband the exact propagator has generalized Rabi frequency (Δ² + 4Ω²_{n′,n})^{1/2} in Wineland's convention and f_n^l = √(δ′² + Ω²_{n+l,n}) in the RMP's, with the drive phase entering as φ + (π/2)|n′−n| (Wineland Eq. 21; RMP Eq. 84) **[verified]**; these closed forms are the unit tests for the builder. The resonant map is |↓⟩|n⟩ → cos(Ω_{n,n′}t)|↓⟩|n⟩ − i e^{iφ} sin(Ω_{n,n′}t)|↑⟩|n′⟩ in Wineland's half-Rabi convention (Wineland 2003 Eq. 2.4), which reads cos(Ω_{n,n′}t/2) and sin(Ω_{n,n′}t/2) in the plan's convention of the next paragraph, the conversion made once at ingest (2026-09-04 critique); the companion Eq. 2.5 is misprinted in the source (its left side should read |↑⟩|n′⟩) and must not be implemented as printed **[corrected]**.

**Rabi-frequency conventions.** Wineland 1998 defines ħΩ = −μ_M B₁/4, half the usual Rabi frequency, so its carrier π pulse is at Ωt = π/2 and its blue-sideband flopping appears at 2Ω_{n+1,n} (Eq. 42) **[verified]**; Monroe 1995 uses P = sin²(Ωτ) likewise **[verified]**; the RMP, Monroe 2020, Lee 2005 and Mount 2015 write H = (ħΩ/2)σ₊ + h.c. so that a π pulse is Ωt = π. The simulator uses the latter, Ω defined so that the resonant carrier flops as sin²(Ωt/2), and converts imported numbers.

#### 4.3.2 Stimulated-Raman drives

Two beams (fields E_j cos(k_j·x − ω_{Lj}t + φ_j), j = 1, 2) coupling |↓⟩ and |↑⟩ through far-detuned excited states |i⟩ produce, after adiabatic elimination (Δ_i ≫ γ_i and Δ_i ≫ the two-photon coupling), an effective Hamiltonian of the single-photon form with the substitutions (Wineland 2003 Eqs. 2.2-2.3; RMP Eq. A12; Wineland 1998 Eqs. 37-41) **[verified]**

k → Δk = k₁ − k₂,  ω_L → ω₁ − ω₂,  φ → φ₁ − φ₂,  Ω e^{iφ} → e^{i(φ₁−φ₂)}/(2ħ²) Σ_i ⟨↑|d·E₂ε̂₂|i⟩⟨i|d·E₁ε̂₁|↓⟩/Δ_i,

which is twice the coupling matrix element that Wineland 2003 Eq. 2.3 prints with 1/(4ħ²), because Ω here is defined by H = (ħΩ/2)σ₊ + h.c. while that paper's Ω is defined by ħΩσ₊ (Section 4.3.1); for a single intermediate state it is Ω₁Ω₂/(2Δ) in single-photon Rabi frequencies of the same convention (Section 4.5.4), and the same 1/(4ħ²) prefactor is correct for the ac Stark shift below, because a level shift and a half-Rabi-frequency share it (the first version of this plan substituted the 1/(4ħ²) form into the (ħΩ/2) slot; caught by the 2026-09-04 critique) **[corrected]**.

The Lamb-Dicke parameter becomes η = Δk z₀ (counter-propagating beams: |Δk| ≈ 2k; co-propagating: Δk ≈ 0, no motional coupling **[verified]**). Factor-of-2 traps: with single-photon Rabi frequencies defined through (ħΩ/2)-type couplings, the RMP's Eq. A13 reads Ω = −2|Ω_{g3}Ω_{e3}|/Δ_R, which is twice "the product divided by Δ_R" and four times a coupling-strength reading **[corrected]**; the arXiv v1 preprint of Lee et al. 2005 (quant-ph/0505203v1) prints the ac Stark shift χ_{m,i} with denominator 2 where 2Δ is required, dimensionally a frequency squared, and swaps the beam-to-level assignment in its Eq. 2, whereas the published J. Opt. B version prints both correctly; the errata check of 2026-09-04 traced the first revision's reading to the preprint (pdftotext also drops the Δ glyph from the journal PDF, which is the likely route in) **[corrected: preprint only]**. The simulator computes the two-photon Rabi frequency and the light shifts from the dipole matrix elements, detunings and intensities in one function, with tests against the 9Be+ closed forms below.

**ac Stark shifts.** Each beam j shifts each qubit level m_S by δ(m_S, j) = (1/4ħ²) Σ_i |⟨m_S|d·E_jε̂_j|i⟩|²/Δ_i (Wineland 2003 Eq. 2.10) **[verified]**; for the 9Be+ |2,2⟩ ↔ |1,1⟩ qubit the eight-term differential shift (Eq. 2.11) collapses for ω₀ ≪ Δ, ω_F to Eq. 2.12 and has a polarization null **[verified]**. For clock qubits the differential shift is the difference of four detuning-weighted diagonal amplitudes, Δ_St = g²[a^{(1/2)}_{↑↑}/(Δ+Δ_hf) + a^{(3/2)}_{↑↑}/(Δ+Δ_hf−Δ_f) − a^{(1/2)}_{↓↓}/Δ − a^{(3/2)}_{↓↓}/(Δ−Δ_f)] (Ozeri 2005 Eq. 3) **[verified]**; for far-detuned clock-qubit Raman drives the ratio of total scattering to Stark phase saturates at 0.9579 γ/Δ_hf ≈ 0.0154 photons per radian for 9Be+ **[verified]**. In a bichromatic drive with asymmetric detunings μ_{i+}, μ_{i−} the Stark term is s_i = μ_{i+} − μ_{i−} (Monroe Eq. 20) **[verified]**. Stark shifts enter the builder as time-dependent σ_z terms proportional to the instantaneous intensity envelope, so amplitude-shaped pulses shift the qubit frequency during the pulse exactly as in the laboratory; the third-tone cancellation used by Innsbruck (Section 4.4) is a scheduled drive like any other.

**Clock-qubit Raman coupling and scattering** (Ozeri et al. 2007) **[verified]**. For the |F = I−1/2, m_F = 0⟩ ↔ |F = I+1/2, m_F = 0⟩ transition driven by a blue/red beam pair detuned Δ from P₁/₂ (Δ − ω_f from P₃/₂):

Ω_R = (g_b g_r/3)(b₋r₋ − b₊r₊) ω_f/[Δ(Δ − ω_f)],

so the coupling interferes destructively between the two fine-structure paths and falls as 1/Δ² for |Δ| ≫ ω_f, exactly like the Raman scattering amplitude **[verified]** (Ozeri 2005: the P₁/₂ and P₃/₂ Raman amplitudes are equal in magnitude and opposite in sign, while the Rayleigh amplitudes share a sign but not a magnitude **[corrected]**). The probability of any scattering event during a π rotation depends only on γ, ω_f and Δ, with all power and waist dependence cancelling:

P_total = (πγ/ω_f)(2Δ² + (Δ − ω_f)²)/|Δ(Δ − ω_f)|,  minimum at Δ = (√2 − 1)ω_f,  P_∞ = 3πγ/ω_f **[verified]**;

the single-qubit gate error is the Raman-scattering probability, ε_S = P_Raman (Eq. 11), whose minimum inside the fine-structure interval is 8πγ/(3ω_f) **[corrected]** (the extracted item called it a maximum), and which in laboratory quantities reads ε_S = 2π|Ω_R| ħ ω_{3/2}³ w₀²/(3c²P) with P the power per beam and w₀ the waist (Eq. 17), the paper's "λ_{3/2} = c/ω_{3/2}" being a typo for 2πc/ω **[verified flag]**. Scattering into low-lying D levels is not protected by the interference: ε_D = f P_total with branching ratio f, giving an irreducible floor ε_D∞ = 3πγf/ω_f (Eqs. 19-21); Table I of that paper gives f⁻¹ = 17 (43Ca+), 14 (87Sr+), 3 (137Ba+), 290 (171Yb+), 700 (199Hg+), and no D level for 9Be+, 25Mg+, 67Zn+, 111Cd+ **[verified]**. Rayleigh scattering at large detuning does not decohere a hyperfine qubit but imparts recoil, which matters for gates through motion (Section 4.4) **[verified]**. The species module stores Ozeri's Table I constants (for 9Be+: I = 3/2, γ/2π = 19.6 MHz, ω₀/2π = 1.25 GHz, ω_f/2π = 0.198 THz, λ = 313.1/313.0 nm; 43Ca+: I = 7/2, 22.5 MHz, 3.23 GHz, 6.68 THz, 396.8/393.4 nm) and the η convention warning that Ozeri's z₀ carries a factor 4 under the root **[verified flag]**. The scattering channels are implemented as Lindblad operators on the extended internal space (d > 2) with rates Γ_{i,f} = g²γ|a^{(1/2)}_{i→f}/Δ + a^{(3/2)}_{i→f}/(Δ − Δ_f)|² summed over final states **[verified]**, and as an estimated per-pulse error when d = 2.

**Frequency-comb Raman drives** (Mount et al. 2015) **[verified]**. A mode-locked comb drives the qubit through the resonant tooth pair and, with weights 1 : 2 : 1, through off-resonant pairs at δ′ and 2δ′; a second-order Magnus average turns these into a static detuning tilt, so the imperfect pulse propagator is R = exp[−(i/2)θ(1 + ε)(σ_φ + δZ)] with a multiplicative area error ε and a dimensionless detuning δ **[verified]**. This is the error model the simulator uses for composite-pulse tests, and it is all that Section 4.3.5 needs; Section 4.3.7 builds the comb drive itself from first principles, as a set of tones over tooth pairs of which Mount's three-term model is the truncation, together with the comb's own resonance conditions, ac Stark shifts and noise channels.

#### 4.3.3 Microwave drives

A microwave field drives the hyperfine transition directly with η ≈ 0 (no motional coupling without a field gradient). Harty et al. 2014 fix the archetype **[verified]**: 43Ca+ clock qubit S₁/₂^{4,0} ↔ S₁/₂^{3,+1} at B₀ = 146.094 G, f₀ = 3,199,941,077 Hz (after removing a −5 Hz differential ac Zeeman shift from trap rf currents), curvature d²f/dB² = 2.4 mHz/mG², μ_I = −1.31535 μ_N with inverted ordering (F = 3 above F = 4); drive from a frequency-octupled 400 MHz DDS with Ω/2π = 21 kHz, t_{π/2} = 12.1 µs, 14 µs dead time for DDS phase switching, deliberate detuning +4.5 Hz and amplitude stability |ΔΩ/Ω| ≲ 5 × 10⁻⁴. The imperfectly polarized field couples off-resonantly to spectator Zeeman transitions Δ_Z/2π ≈ 50 MHz away, giving spectator flopping of order (Ω/Δ_Z)² ≈ 10⁻⁷ and an ac Zeeman shift of the qubit transition of −1.0 Hz; with the convention δ = ω_drive − ω₀ a negative transition shift increases the detuning, δ_eff = δ − δ_ac = +5.5 Hz **[corrected]** (the extracted item had the sign wrong). The paper's own error model, a product of imperfect π/2 propagators and 14 µs dead-time propagators over the longest randomized-benchmarking sequences, predicts 0.81(14) × 10⁻⁶ per gate against the measured 1.0(3) × 10⁻⁶ **[verified]**; the simulator reproduces this as a test (Section 9.2). Memory: Ramsey contrast exp(−t_R/T₂*) with T₂* = 50(10) s at the clock point, without shielding or decoupling **[verified]**.

#### 4.3.4 Phases, frames and virtual Z

The drive phase φ = φ₁ − φ₂ (plus the geometric term δk X̄_i for the ion's equilibrium position) may be set to zero once per ion, after which every operation on that ion must be referenced to that phase **[verified]** (Wineland 2003). The carrier, red-sideband and blue-sideband Hamiltonians all carry the same optical phase factor e^{i(ΔkX_{0,i} − Δφ)} (Lee 2005 Eqs. 6-8) **[verified]**, so a carrier rotation axis at angle β_i in the xy plane is set by the beat-note phase (Monroe Eq. 10) **[verified]**. In a bichromatic drive the two optical phases recombine into a spin phase, the half-sum of the tone phases, that sets the axis σ_{φ_s} of the spin-dependent force, and a motion phase, the half-difference, that sets the phase of the force on the mode (Lee Eqs. 28-30; Monroe Eqs. 20-22) **[verified]**. The simulator's convention is Haljan's, φ_s = +(φ_b + φ_r)/2 for the half-sum (Sections 4.4.2 and 13); Lee writes φ_S = −(half-sum), whose minus sign the extracted prose had dropped **[corrected]**, and it is converted at ingest (the first version of this plan adopted Lee's sign here and Haljan's in Section 4.4.2, a contradiction caught by the 2026-09-04 critique). The axis of the force is not the half-sum itself: expanding the plan's own drive factor e^{i(Δk·x̂ − μt + φ)} to first order gives the sideband coupling an explicit i (iη(a + a†)), and i[σ₊e^{iψ} − h.c.] = σ_{ψ+π/2}, so in the same-Δk (phase-sensitive) geometry the force axis sits at π/2 + Δk·X̄_i + (φ_b + φ_r)/2, a quarter turn from the carrier axis at the same beat-note phase, while in the crossed-Δk (phase-insensitive) geometry η_r = −η_b adds a further π and Δk·X̄_i cancels; the constant offset is what the phase-frame alignment of Section 7.5 step 3 measures and the native MS(φ₀, φ₁) definition absorbs, so it changes no gate, but the Section 9.4 pin must carry it: an MS pulse with φ_b = φ_r = φ on ions at the frame origin entangles about the azimuth φ + π/2 in the same-Δk geometry (the second revision's '+φ' would have certified a quarter-turn error; derivation audit of 2026-09-04) **[corrected: derivation audit, 2026-09-04]**. Whether the gate is phase sensitive or insensitive is decided by beam geometry: with two beat notes on the same beam pair both above (or below) the third beam's frequency, Δk_r = Δk_b and the optical phase enters the spin phase (sensitive); with one above and one below, Δk_r = −Δk_b and the optical phase enters only the motion phase, which cancels when the loop closes (insensitive) **[verified]**. Virtual Z rotations are frame updates: RZ(θ) on qubit i shifts the phase reference of every later pulse on i by −θ in time order (Section 7.6), costing no error beyond bookkeeping and no time beyond the hardware's phase-switching dead time **[corrected]**, and identity gates in benchmarking are implemented as delays of one pulse duration so that idle errors are counted **[verified]** (Harty 2014).

#### 4.3.5 Single-qubit gates and composite pulses

A native GPi(φ) or GPi2(φ) is a resonant carrier pulse of area π or π/2 with phase φ relative to the ion's frame; the target unitary is R(θ, φ) = exp(−iθσ_φ/2), σ_φ = X cos φ + Y sin φ **[verified]** (Mount 2015 Eq. 1). The half-Pauli generator is load-bearing for everything below: a 2π segment is exactly −I, and R(π, φ)R(α, β)R(π, φ)† = R(α, 2φ − β) is the in-plane reflection every corrector phase descends from, so halving the tabulated angles to "pulse areas" θ/2 breaks the 2π quantization and with it every sequence **[verified]**, **[recomputed here]**. Products run right to left, so a scheduler emitting (θ_l, φ_l) in time order folds them in reverse (Section 13), and a negative nominal area is played as a positive area at phase φ + π - exact under amplitude error and **not** under detuning error, the σ_z term not reversing with the drive phase.

**The static error primitive and the four channels.** Run 5 reconciled five composite-pulse sources onto one object **[verified]**: M(θ, φ; ε_a, ε_d) = exp[−iθ{(1 + ε_a)ρ̂(φ)·σ + ε_d σ_z}/2], ρ̂(φ) = (cos φ, sin φ, 0), exact for a square segment under time-independent errors. It is Section 6.4's R = exp[−(i/2)θ(1 + ε)(σ_φ + δZ)] (Mount 2015) and also Cummins' quaternion pulse, whose axis (cos φ, sin φ, ε_d)/√(1 + ε_d²) and angle θ√(1 + ε_d²) identify ε_a = g and ε_d = f = δ/ν₁ with no rescaling on ingest **[verified]**. Four systematic channels map onto it (Merrill and Brown Sec. 2.2.1) **[verified]**: **amplitude** ε_A = Ω′/Ω at fixed duration, M = R(θ(1 + ε_A), φ); **pulse length** ε_T = δt/Δt, the same propagator for a square pulse but kept separate so the coupled model v_{x,y} = u_{x,y}(1 + ε_T), v_z = (u_z + ε_D)(1 + ε_T) is reproducible; **addressing** ε_N = Ω′/Ω on a spectator, whose zero is **inverted** (ε_N = 0 is ideal, U_T = I, M = R(θε_N, φ)), so R(θ(1 + ε_N), φ) by analogy with ε_A is wrong; and **detuning** ε_D = Δ/Ω, whose error generator +ε_D θ H_z scales with pulse area and keeps its plus sign even where the drive phase is π and the in-plane term is negative - flipping it along with the middle CORPSE pulse makes all three generators commute and returns the bare-pulse infidelity to six digits **[verified]**. Amplitude and addressing errors have δu ∝ u, so the leading error is a vector sum; detuning does not commute with the ideal generator, which is why CORPSE needs a Magnus expansion and BB1 does not.

**Order bookkeeping and the fidelity measure.** Order n means residual O(ε^{n+1}), hence distance slope n + 1 and **infidelity slope 2(n + 1)**, never 2n; two source abstracts write O(ε^n) loosely against their own equations **[corrected]**. Three measures appear and differ by 2 and 4/3 in infidelity: F_C = |Tr(U_id†U)|/2 (Cummins' quaternion fidelity and Merrill-Brown's minimized-state form are the same number), F_K = ¼|Tr(U_id†U)|² = F_C², and F_avg = (2F_C² + 1)/3, so 1 − F_K = 2(1 − F_C) and 1 − F_avg = (4/3)(1 − F_C) to leading order **[verified]**, **[recomputed here]**. The simulator adopts F_K internally, the normalization Section 6.9's filter functions are built to, and converts on ingest; every published c-coefficient carries the same factor 2. Two printed "fidelities" must be replaced rather than implemented: Low-Yoder-Chuang's F = ‖UV†‖ is identically 1 for a qubit under its own normalized trace norm and Brown-Harrow-Chuang's 1 − ‖V†U‖ identically 0 for unitaries **[corrected]**, **[recomputed here]**. Below E ≈ 10⁻⁸ a subtracted 1 − F hits the float64 floor, so sequences are scored by the operator distance and converted as E²/2, or run in mpmath as the check script does.

#### 4.3.7 Frequency-comb (mode-locked) Raman drives

Every 355 nm Raman system on 171Yb+ drives the qubit with a mode-locked pulse train, not two continuous-wave tones, so the chain of Sections 4.3.2 and 4.5.4 becomes a sum over comb-tooth pairs. B companion of 2014, Campbell 2010, Lee 2016 - for 63 model entries, a third of them corrected on transcription or on the sources' own printed errors. The set is one species at one wavelength; the module takes (ν_rep, τ, Δ, species, geometry) as inputs, and Section 9.15 holds its numbers.

**The idealized train and its comb.** A train of N identical pulses at a fixed point is E(t) = Σ_{n=1}^{N} f(t − nT) e^{iω_c t} (Hayes Eq. 1) **[verified]**, whose unnormalized forward transform f̃(ω − ω_c) Σ_n e^{−i(ω−ω_c)nT} has teeth at ω_c + 2πmν_R spaced by ν_R = 1/T. The carrier exponential sits **outside** the sum: that is the zero-carrier-envelope-offset idealization, and writing e^{iω_c(t−nT)} instead reintroduces a per-pulse phase e^{−inω_cT}, a nonzero offset frequency **[verified]**. A Λ-configuration Raman process needs no carrier-envelope phase at all - the qubit sees only tooth-frequency *differences*, and f_ceo cancels between two copies of one comb **[verified]** - so the simulator carries only the inter-comb (AOM) phase, which is the spin-dependent-force phase. Tooth width follows the Dirichlet kernel |sin(Nϑ/2)/sin(ϑ/2)|: exact FWHM 1.206716 ν_R/N in amplitude and 0.885899 ν_R/N in power at N = 256 **[recomputed here]**; the widely quoted 0.886 is the intensity value, so code holding amplitudes uses 1.2067. **Comb noise channels and where they enter.** Three channels are comb-specific, each mapping onto one entry point of Section 6.1.

- **Harmonic-amplified repetition-rate drift.** The beat note is a tooth-index *difference*, so ν_sb = nν_rep − |Δν_M| has ∂ν_sb/∂ν_rep = n and δν_sb = n δν_rep: at n = 157 a free-running 1 Hz/min becomes 157 Hz/min = 2.617 Hz/s at the qubit **[verified]**, and a 10⁻⁷ fractional drift at 80 MHz becomes 1.26 kHz **[recomputed here]**. It is driven from a ν_rep(t) process, not an ad-hoc qubit-frequency noise term, so the gain emerges rather than being asserted: the slow part is option (c), a quasi-static per-shot sample, the 1 Hz to 10 kHz band option (d), a sampled trajectory. Drift alone is insufficient - it accumulates 0.008 Hz over the measured 3 ms unlocked Ramsey time - so a fast jitter is fitted to that 3 ms, ~53 Hz rms on the beat note (derived, not sourced) **[corrected]**.
- **Feed-forward per-tooth residuals.** The lock cancels drift algebraically to all orders, but only for the selected tooth's lower sideband: on tooth m the residual is (m − n) δν_rep for the lower sideband, (m + n) δν_rep for the upper (worse than unlocked) and m δν_rep for a bare tooth **[corrected]**. A tooth-pair sum for a Stark shift or a spin-dependent force applies these coefficients pair by pair, not as a global lock - no source does this - and for the off-resonant Stark drive the sensitivity exceeds the bare tooth-index lever, 360× fractional on δω^{(4)}, of which jν_rep/δ_{00,10} = 294× is the detuning **[recomputed here]**.
- **Intensity noise.** Ω(t) = Ω₀(1 + ξ(t)) with one-sided white S_ξ = α_lin is option (d), with error ε ≈ (π²/2) α Ω₀² T and **Ω₀ in Hz, never rad/s**: at α = −115 dB/Hz, Ω₀ = 600 kHz, T = 1 ms the Hz reading gives 5.6179 × 10⁻³, consistent with the source's own ε < 1%, while rad/s gives 0.2218, wrong by (2π)² = 39.478 **[recomputed here; corrected]**. The relation is dimensionally consistent either way, so only this test settles it; structurally π²/2 = (2π)²/8 = 4.9348022005, the conversion already absorbed. The PLL-achieved −120 dB/Hz is the default, and the prefactor is order-unity-loose, strict power-to-field propagation giving π²/8 **[contested]**.

The static fourth-order shift and any retained off-resonant tones are option (a); the shot-constant beat-note phase φ₀ is option (c), sampled once per shot and never averaged inside a trajectory. Option (b), a Lindblad rate, gets nothing new from this layer, which is the first gap.

**What the comb sources do not supply.** **No spontaneous-emission rate exists in the comb layer at all**: every Hamiltonian in all six sources is post-adiabatic-elimination and carries no Lindblad term (γ is not even defined in one of them except inside Ω₀ = sγ²/(2Δ)), so per-gate Raman and Rayleigh probabilities, branching ratios and P-manifold decay during the pulse are absent. The plan does not take them from these sources: the scattering channels of Section 4.5.5 are computed from the *same* tooth sums, the residual excited population being Σ_k |g_k|²/(4Δ²) over teeth exactly as Section 4.5.4's adiabatic-elimination residual, so drive and dissipator cannot drift apart; until that is validated the simulator bounds rather than computes the fidelity ceiling of a 355 nm comb drive. **No multi-ion comb Mølmer-Sørensen construction exists**: every comb equation in the set is single-ion and single-mode, so modes, participation factors, per-mode η_{im} and phase-space closure come from Sections 4.1.3 and 4.4.3, and the reported two-ion fidelities are consistency checks, not targets. **No wave-front phase-gradient tolerance closes**: only ΔL ≪ cτ ≈ 3.0 mm (printed as c/τ, dimensionally an acceleration **[corrected]**) and the λ′ formula above, whose own numbers do not close. Section 12 carries these, plus the missing noise spectra, the missing numerical η, the missing fourth-order shift for a *resonant* carrier drive, and the (τ, bandwidth) inconsistency that moves C_{00,10} from 0.9438 to 0.7174 **[recomputed here]**.

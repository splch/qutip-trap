## 8. Readout, SPAM and results

Readout is modelled in four layers that the code keeps separate: an atomic-rate layer, a stochastic photon-record layer, a discriminator layer, and an error-budget layer. Three conventions caused most of the factor errors the verifiers found in the sources and are pinned first: bright/dark polarity is a property of the readout scheme, not of the qubit (direct fluorescence and shelving readout invert it within the same papers); Γ is stored as an angular population decay rate in s⁻¹ with an assertion that Γ/2π reproduces the quoted linewidth; and the system detection efficiency ε_sys enters exactly once, at the conversion from scattered to detected rate **[verified]**.

### 8.1 Atomic-rate layer

**Bright-state scattering rate.** For the 171Yb+ cycling transition ²S₁/₂|F=1⟩ → ²P₁/₂|F=0⟩ with the three ground sublevels destabilized against coherent population trapping, the optimally polarized scattering rate is (Noek et al. 2013 Eq. 1; Crain et al. 2019) **[verified]**

R_∘ = (Γ/18) s_∘ / [1 + (2/9) s_∘ + (2Δ/Γ)²],  s_∘ = 2Ω²/Γ² = I/I_sat,  equivalently R_∘ = (Γ/4) s̃/[1 + s̃ + (2Δ/Γ)²] with s̃ = (2/9)s_∘ = I/(229 mW/cm²),

with Γ the partial rate of the S₁/₂ ↔ P₁/₂ line, Γ_S = 2π × 19.62 MHz (0.99499 of the 19.72 MHz total from the 8.07(9) ns lifetime, Section 9.12; the 19.6 MHz reading the second revision carried here and in the 8.4 preset is retired everywhere, and a CI check greps the tree for retired constants), Ω the full J = 1/2 → J′ = 1/2 line Rabi frequency Ω = |⟨J‖d‖J′⟩|E₀/ħ with no angular factor, I_sat = πhcΓ/(3λ³) = 50.8 mW/cm² at 369.5 nm, saturating at Γ/4 = 30.79 µs⁻¹ (three ground plus one excited level), not Γ/2, with half-maximum at s_∘ = 4.5 **[verified]**, **[corrected: derivation audit, 2026-09-04]**. **Leakage rates.** Off-resonant pumping between the bright and dark manifolds is the dominant error channel for direct hyperfine readout; R_d is the rate at which a bright ion (F = 1) is pumped dark and R_b the rate at which a dark ion (F = 0) is pumped bright, Noek's and Crain's names, so R_d carries the bright state's off-resonant F = 1 → F′ = 1 channel at Δ_HFP with its 1/3 branching into F = 0 and R_b the dark state's only dipole-allowed channel, F = 0 → F′ = 1 at Δ_HFP + Δ_HFS (a reader who takes R_d for the dark-to-bright rate finds the two detunings interchanged, which one comparer of the 2026-09-04 derivation audit did). R_d ≈ (2/3)(1/3)(Γ/2)(2Ω²/Γ²)(Γ/(2Δ_HFP))²,  R_b ≈ (2/3)(Γ/2)(2Ω²/Γ²)(Γ/(2(Δ_HFP + Δ_HFS)))²,

with Δ_HFP = 2π × 2.1 GHz (P₁/₂ hyperfine splitting) and Δ_HFS = 2π × 12.6 GHz (qubit splitting). **The metastable manifold during detection** (added after the 2026-09-04 experimentalist critique) **[corrected]**. ### 8.2 Photon-record layer

Given the joint internal outcome sampled after the circuit (Section 5.7), each ion's count record over a window t is a piecewise-constant-rate Poisson process driven by the bright-dark(-shelf) continuous-time Markov chain plus background; the fast path samples that chain exactly, alternating Exp(R_d) and Exp(R_b) holding times until the window closes and drawing Poisson counts at the piecewise rates, so that windows with kτ not small (Myerson's 420 µs, the 133Ba+ 4.5 ms) carry as many transitions as the physics gives them, and the single-jump forms below are the analytic oracles in their small-kτ regime (the first version of this plan restricted the record to one jump; caught by the 2026-09-04 critique). The single-jump count distribution is (Crain et al. 2019 Eq. 1 with its missing no-jump branch restored **[corrected]**):

P(n; t) = e^{−R_T t} P_p(n; R₁ t) + Σ_{k=0}^{n} ∫₀^t dτ P_p(k; R₁ τ) R_T e^{−R_T τ} P_p(n−k; R₂(t−τ)),

where R₁, R₂ are the detected rates before and after the jump, R_T the jump rate, and P_p the Poisson pmf; as printed the equation sums to 1 − e^{−R_T t} and carries only about 1% of the probability in the paper's own regime. Acton's Poisson-exponential mixtures for the dark and bright states with leak probabilities α₁/η and α₂/η per detected photon are the equivalent closed forms **[verified]**, normalized to machine precision by the verifiers. The zero-threshold error formulas for a hyperfine qubit with background (Crain Eqs. 2-3) are used with ε_sys restored in the final exponent, e^{−(ε_sys R_∘ + R_bg)t} **[corrected]**; the printed omission changes the bright-state error at 11 µs by a factor of about 9. The idealized limit of all this is Wineland's P_N(0) = (1 − η_d)^N ≈ e^{−n_d} **[verified]**, a binomial over a fixed photon number that needs only η_d ≪ 1.

The simulator generates records two interchangeable ways and asserts they agree with the closed forms: a fast path that samples the Markov chain exactly as above (or per-sub-bin Bernoulli(t_s/τ) to match the discriminators' linearization) and then Poisson counts at the piecewise rates plus Poisson(R_bg t); and a trajectory path that runs `mcsolve` on the reduced bright/dark(/shelf) space with a photon-counting collapse operator, recording arrival times. The mean-count calibration curve for a bright-prepared ion, n̄(τ) = ε R_∘ [(R_b/k) τ + (R_d/k²)(1 − e^{−kτ})] with k = R_b + R_d, follows from the two-state rate equation ṗ₁ = R_b p₀ − R_d p₁ **[verified]** and is the fit the calibration layer uses to extract (R_d, R_b, ε R_∘).

### 8.3 Discriminator layer

Implemented as a strategy interface over the same record:

- **Threshold.** Sum counts over t_b and declare bright iff n > n_c with n_c half-integer; scan (n_c, t_b) for the interior optimum, which exists because the overlap error falls with t_b while leakage, shelf decay and background rise **[verified]**. Myerson's 40Ca+ optimum: n_c = 5.5, t_b = 420 µs, ε = 1.8(1) × 10⁻⁴ **[verified]**.
- **Time-resolved maximum likelihood** (Myerson et al. 2008 Eqs. 1-2) **[verified]**. With sub-bin counts n_i, p_B = Π B(n_i) and p_D = (1 − t_b/τ) Π D(n_i) + (t_s/τ) Σ_j Π_{i<j} D(n_i) Π_{i≥j} B(n_i), evaluated by the exact O(N) recursion M_k = M_{k−1} D(n_k), S_k = (S_{k−1} + M_{k−1}) B(n_k), in the log domain with log-sum-exp and a common rescaling of the running p_B (M_N underflows near N ≈ 227 sub-bins at the paper's rates). The mixture weights are first-order in t/τ and normalize exactly; ties go to bright as in the source. This reaches 0.87(11) × 10⁻⁴ on 40Ca+ **[verified]**.
- **Adaptive (Bayesian early termination).** After each sub-bin compute e_D = p_B/(p_B + p_D), e_B = p_D/(p_B + p_D) and stop when the smaller falls below a cutoff or at a worst-case time; the posterior form assumes equal priors, and the printed middle expression omits the prior factor **[corrected]**. Myerson: ε = 1.0(1) × 10⁻⁴ at 145 µs average; Noek's first-photon protocol (0 counts → dark, ≥ 2 → bright, 1 count before τ_c = ln(R_d/R_dc)/(ε R_∘) → bright) gives 99.85% at 28 µs **[verified]**; Crain's stop-on-first-photon with SNSPDs gives 99.931(6)% at 11 µs **[verified]**. The per-shot posterior error is exposed as a soft-decision confidence for clients.
- **Camera (spatial) discriminators** (Burrell et al. 2010) **[verified]**: per-pixel Poisson likelihoods in brightness-sorted order, R = |ln(p_B/p_D)|, register error ≈ Σ_k e^{−R_k}; a spatio-temporal variant with shelf decay across exposures; and neighbour-conditioned likelihoods B_{νki}, D_{νki} for the four nearest-neighbour states, decoded by iterated conditional modes seeded all-dark **[corrected]**. Crosstalk then emerges from PSF overlap without a free parameter; the source's finding that time resolution gives no improvement on a camera, unlike a PMT, is a test target **[verified]**. Camera SNR uses read noise as (kr)², linear in the number of readouts **[corrected]**, and the Airy scale 1.22 λ/NA **[contested: the source prints 1.22 λ/tan α]**.

### 8.4 Error-budget layer and the measurement operator

The budget is reported as named channels, never one scalar: preparation (optical pumping, mapping pulses), shelving or mapping transfer, shelf decay during the window, discrimination (count overlap plus background), and register crosstalk; ε_B and ε_D are reported separately and averaged only at the end as ε = ½(ε_B + ε_D), because real systems are strongly asymmetric (Burrell's threshold optimum has ε_B = 0) **[verified]**. Two figure-of-merit conventions exist in the sources, Acton's worst-case min(F_B, F_D) and the unweighted mean used by Myerson, Burrell, Harty and Christensen, and tables in those papers are state-averaged so single-state errors appear halved **[verified]**; the simulator states which it reports. Validated budgets: Harty's 43Ca+ 6.8(5) × 10⁻⁴ closes exactly from its Table I rows (1.8 + 1.8 + 1.7 + 1.5, ×10⁻⁴) **[verified]**; Christensen's 133Ba+ table totals 3.4 × 10⁻⁴ against a measured 2.9(6) × 10⁻⁴ **[verified]**; for 133Ba+ the threshold overlap at bright mean 39 versus dark mean 1 contributes below 10⁻⁶, so the simulated SPAM must be dominated by shelving and pumping errors, not counting statistics **[verified]**. Register scaling is F_s = (1 − ε_s)^{N_q} under i.i.d. errors, with usable size N_q < ln 2/ε_s **[verified]**.

The whole readout can be summarized as a classical POVM applied after the coherent evolution: E_bright and E_dark built from the per-prepared-state confusion probabilities, verified to sum to the identity, with a non-rank-one dark element for shelving schemes; the post-measurement state is discarded because shelving readout is not QND **[recipe]**. The POVM is the summary of the record and discriminator layers, computed once per device by the calibration layer, and applying it directly to the joint state is the fast path that replaces those layers (Section 5.7); the full path and the fast path must agree in confusion matrix, and a Bell state's bit correlations must survive both, which Section 9.5 tests. Species presets: 40Ca+ PMT (R_B = 55,800 s⁻¹, R_D = 442 s⁻¹, ε = 0.19%, τ_D = 1168 ms); 43Ca+ (50,000 detected counts/s, ε = 0.3%, B₀ = 146.094 G, inverted hyperfine ordering with F = 3 above F = 4 **[corrected]**); 171Yb+ (Γ_S = 2π × 19.62 MHz partial, 19.72 total, Δ_HFP = 2.1 GHz, Δ_HFS = 12.642812118 GHz, branching P₁/₂ → D₃/₂ 0.00501(15), τ(D₃/₂) = 52.7 ms, ε_sys = 2.2% Noek / 4.356% Crain); 133Ba+ (shelving through ²P₃/₂, τ_D ≈ 30 s, window 4.5 ms, threshold n ≤ 12) **[verified]**.

### 8.5 Readout crosstalk and mid-circuit measurement

Wineland's mechanism is a degradation of state-discrimination efficiency by a neighbour's scattered light of different polarization, not a change in the instrumental η_d **[corrected]**; it is modelled as added counts on a dark neighbour plus depumping-reduced N. Radiative pumping of a neighbour by a bright ion is bounded by I_ion/I_sat = 3λ²/(8π²x²) with I_sat = πhcΓ/(3λ³) (Acton's printed I_sat has ħ for h and is 2π too small, making the printed ratio and its 7 × 10⁻⁴ estimate 2π too large; the corrected 111Cd+ value at 4 µm is 1.1 × 10⁻⁴) **[corrected]**. Camera PSF overlap gives about 4.0% nearest-neighbour and 0.9% next-nearest signal leakage at 14 µm spacing **[verified]**. During mid-circuit readout a spectator qubit dephases with a Gaussian, not exponential, coherence decay, exp(−τ²/α²) with α = 94 ms at 200 µm and 814 ms at 370 µm from the detected ion **[verified]** (Crain 2019); the simulator implements this as a quasi-static frequency offset sampled per shot, since a constant-rate σ_z Lindblad term would give the wrong lineshape. Mid-circuit measurement itself is out of scope for the first release (Section 7.2), and its extension is not a scheduler change: 10³ to 10⁴ scattered photons at the 1.1 × 10⁻³ quanta per photon of Section 4.2.8 heat every mode the detection beam projects on by 1 to 10 quanta and leave the measured ion in a bright-manifold mixture, so a mid-circuit measurement requires recooling and re-preparation of the measured ion in the schedule, and the physics for it (the recoil channel, optical pumping, sympathetic cooling) is what Sections 4.2 and 4.5 supply, and the IR and the scheduler carry measure, reset and recool as operations from the first release (Section 7.2), so implementing the stage changes no pipeline shape; the spectator model here covers the neighbours (the first version of this plan called the extension a scheduler change; caught by the 2026-09-04 experimentalist critique) **[corrected]**.

### 8.6 Results

A `Result` carries per shot: the bitstring, the photon-count record per ion (and per sub-bin when time-resolved), the discriminator's posterior confidence when available, and the sampled quasi-static noise parameters of that shot's dynamical sample. Aggregates: counts and probabilities per bitstring; per-qubit ε_B and ε_D estimated from calibration runs; the diagnostics block (fidelity level, truncation-boundary populations, included and dropped modes, calibration table used, solver tolerances, seeds). Optional heavy outputs: the final density matrix or the trajectory ensemble. The IonQ exporter emits the cloud formats exactly as the vendor SDKs consume them, established from the IonQ OpenAPI v0.4 specification and the `qiskit-ionq`, `ionq-core-python` and `PennyLane-IonQ` client code read on 2026-09-04 **[verified against the client sources]**: the legacy probability result (`ionq.result.probabilities.json.v1`) is a flat object keyed by the **decimal integer of the measured register with qubit 0 as the least-significant bit**, for example `{"0": 0.5, "1": 0.25, "3": 0.25}`; PennyLane reverses the bit order because it is big-endian and Qiskit does not because it is little-endian, and the pinned client test maps `{"5": 0.5, "7": 0.5}` on three qubits to the Qiskit keys `101` and `111`. The histogram variant carries shot counts with the same keys, the per-shot format is an ordered list of the same decimal strings (`["6", "1", "0", "7"]` on three qubits is `110, 001, 000, 111`), and the v2 register-nested format is keyed by bitstrings per named register. The simulator's `Result` stores bitstrings with an explicit `bit_order` attribute and converts at the exporter, with round-trip tests on these examples (Section 9.13), so that client code written against the cloud API runs unchanged against the simulator.

### 8.7 Gaps the sources leave

No source gives a continuous-time arrival-record likelihood (all discriminators are sub-bin based); detector dead time, afterpulsing and SNSPD recovery are excluded everywhere; state preparation is quantified per apparatus but never as a first-principles rate model; correlated SPAM errors across a register are unmeasured; the neighbour-state assignment for end ions and the decoder's initialization for large registers are unspecified; and the temperature dependence of shelving fidelity through Doppler broadening has no functional form in the sources. Each is a documented limitation of the first release, with the preparation-error model built from the optical-pumping rates of Section 4.2 as the first planned extension.

### 8.8 Additions from the Run 2A readout consolidation

The cross-source consolidation of the readout brief (Appendix D) recomputed the contested numbers and adds five requirements to the layers above:

- **Two saturation parameters, never aliased.** Noek's s = 2Ω²/Γ² in his (Γ/6, 2/3) form is s_∘/3 = I/(3I_sat) in the plan's full-line convention (Section 8.1): pinning it from Crain's measured detected rate (472 kcps at ε_sys = 4.356%) gives s = 0.815, that is s_∘ = 2.45, against the quoted I/I_sat = 1.10 at 56.2 mW/cm² (the two-level I_sat = 51 mW/cm² convention), a factor 2.2 in intensity (the second revision, with a prefactor one factor of 1/3 too large, made it 1.35 in the other direction). Acton's s is I/I_sat. Because the corrected R_∘ is the maximum of the exact four-level steady state over polarization, field and detuning at fixed intensity, a measured rate above it can only mean more light at the ion or a higher ε_sys than stated, so the residual is an apparatus quantity (intensity calibration of a focused beam, polarization impurity, detection efficiency), not a missing factor in the physics; the device model stores both parameters and the intensity-to-Ω chain is marked uncalibrated until the optical-Bloch check (Section 8.1) closes the gap **[corrected: derivation audit, 2026-09-04]**.
- **Leakage-rate prefactors are derived, and the apparatus residual is calibrated.** With that s_∘, Crain's R_d prefactor predicts 364 Hz against the measured 341(13) Hz and Noek's predicts 243 Hz; the (2/3)(Γ/2) form of R_b predicts 14.9 Hz against 16.4(5) Hz; the measured ratio R_d/R_b = 20.8 sits between the two predictions (24.5 and 16.3). Neither prefactor pair is fully consistent with the measured triple, so the atomic-rate layer computes the (R_d, R_b) prefactors from the angular algebra of Section 4.5.2, which settles the Clebsch-Gordan bookkeeping in Noek's favour, and reports the residual against Crain's measured triple as a discrepancy attributed to apparatus inputs (intensity calibration, detuning, polarization purity) that the device model exposes as calibration parameters with provenance, never as species constants; the first version of this plan carried a fitted species scale here, which contradicted Section 3.1 **[corrected]**.
- **Closed-form angular factors** for the general σ⁺ stretch-state scheme, normalized so the cycling line has unit strength: M₁(I) = 4I(3 + 2I)/(9(1 + 2I)²), M_2π(I) = 4I/(9 + 18I), M_2−(I) = 16I/(9(1 + 2I)³), giving 2/9, 1/9, 1/9 at I = 1/2; the clock-state P₁/₂ scheme has M₁′ = M₂′ = 2/9 with α₂′ > α₁′ always **[corrected]**.
- **Detector non-idealities are a named gap, not a negligible one.** The corrected closed-form optimum for Crain's apparatus is 5.84 × 10⁻⁴ against the measured 6.9 × 10⁻⁴; dead time, afterpulsing and SNSPD recovery at a 2.1 µs mean inter-arrival time are the plausible remainder, and the photon-record layer therefore carries an optional dead-time and afterpulse model with parameters the user must supply **[extracted]**.
- **Motion-to-readout coupling has no functional form in any source.** Harty et al. observe that heating degrades 393 nm shelving in 43Ca+ through Doppler broadening but give no equation; the simulator implements the coupling from first principles through the thermal Doppler profile of the shelving transition and flags it as untested against data **[background]**.
- **Micromotion on the detection beam.** R_∘ carries J₀(β)² and a first-sideband channel J₁(β)², like every other drive (Section 4.1); no readout source applies it **[corrected]**.
- **Doppler broadening from residual motion, made computable.** With v_rms = √(Σ_m ħω_m(2n̄_m + 1)/(2m)) over the modes the detection k-vector projects onto, k v_rms/2π for 171Yb+ is 257 kHz at n̄ = 0.1, 425 kHz at n̄ = 1, 1.57 MHz at n̄ = 20 and 3.48 MHz at n̄ = 100 against the 19.7 MHz natural linewidth, so detection or shelving error from first-order Doppler broadening is negligible until n̄ ≳ 100 and the Lamb-Dicke guard η²(2n̄ + 1) fails first; derived, not sourced **[background]**.

# Part III. Numerics, noise, control and readout

## 5. Hilbert space, frames and numerical strategy

### 5.1 Composite space

Each ion is a qudit of dimension d. Default d = 2 (the qubit pair). When photon scattering or imperfect optical pumping is enabled, d grows to include the leakage levels the species module marks as reachable (for a 171Yb+ hyperfine qubit: the two other F = 1 Zeeman states at minimum; for optical qubits: the other D-state Zeeman sublevels populated by decay). Each motional mode is a truncated oscillator. The space is the tensor product of ions then modes; operators are built once per `HilbertSpace` and cached.

Two truncation schemes are supported and selectable per run:

- **Per-mode cutoff** n_max,m, the highest Fock index kept, so that a mode's dimension is d_m = n_max,m + 1 and the motional dimension is Π_m d_m (Section 11 quotes d_m). Simple, exact for any product of per-mode populations, wasteful when many modes are cold.
- **Excitation-number restriction (ENR):** total excitation across a group of modes capped at N_exc, using QuTiP's `enr_state_dictionaries`, `enr_destroy`, `enr_fock`, `enr_thermal_dm` (present in 5.3.1). For M modes near the ground state this reduces the motional dimension from (n_max+1)^M to C(M + N_exc, N_exc). The displacement operators D(iη) needed by the Hamiltonian are built inside the ENR space from the ENR ladder operators by matrix exponential, so no operator is ever formed in the unrestricted space; Section 5.1.1 gives the measured accuracy of that construction.

The default for joint-exact runs is per-mode cutoffs for the modes a pulse resolves dynamically (its driven modes and the spectators selected by the contribution criterion of Section 11.3), with the remaining modes carried as frozen spectators (Section 5.2), because a product space is what the exact displacement operator, the mode-factorized kernel of Section 11.3 and the boundary monitor all assume. ENR is an option for a group of cold, undriven modes that must nevertheless be carried dynamically, with the caveats of Section 5.1.1 and three more measured on 5.3.1: `ptrace` on any space containing an ENR factor raises `NotImplementedError`, so an ENR run cannot produce the reduced qubit state that `Result.spam`, the channel summary and GATE_LOCAL tomography need, or a per-mode reduced density matrix, unless the module builds the marginal itself from `enr_state_dictionaries`, which it does; `tensor(sigmap(), D_enr)` reports `dims` whose product (98 for two modes at N_exc = 6) disagrees with its `shape` (56), so every dimension computation reads `shape` behind an assertion and never `dims`; and the ENR displacement is unitary to 8 × 10⁻¹⁵ while silently wrong near the cap (⟨n₀⟩ = 2.2412 against 2.25 after a displacement α = 1.5 at N_exc = 6), which is why its cost is quoted at N_exc ≈ 2n_phys with the top-shell population as the only convergence diagnostic (2026-09-04 numerics critique) **[corrected: critique, 2026-09-04]**: an ENR space is not a product space, so the exponential of its sum generator differs from the product of per-mode exponentials near the excitation cap, it cannot be applied mode by mode, and the factorized kernel does not apply to it. A Mølmer-Sørensen gate transiently displaces its target mode by |α| up to about 1, which that mode's cap must accommodate with the margin of Section 5.1.1; the first version of this plan made ENR over one axis the default, which the 2026-09-04 critique showed to be incompatible with the factorized kernel and with the two-family closure of Section 4.4.7.

#### 5.1.1 Constructing the displacement operator in a truncated space

Two constructions of D(iη) exist in a space of n_max Fock levels, and they are not the same matrix. The **matrix exponential of the truncated generator**, expm[iη(a + a†)] with the truncated a (QuTiP's `displace`, and likewise the ENR ladder operators in an ENR space), is exactly unitary (defect below 10⁻¹⁵ in every case measured) but its matrix elements near the truncation boundary are wrong. The **analytic elements** ⟨n′|D(iη)|n⟩ = e^{−η²/2} (n_<!/n_>!)^{1/2} (iη)^{|n′−n|} L^{(|n′−n|)}_{n_<}(η²), the exact infinite-space elements of Section 4.3.1 written into the truncated matrix, are exact element by element (the modulus in the exponent is specific to a purely imaginary displacement, for which the lower branch's (−α*)^{n−n′} equals (iη)^{n−n′}; for a general complex α the two branches are α^{n′−n} where n′ > n, below the diagonal of the matrix ⟨n′|·|n⟩ with row n′ and column n, and (−α*)^{n−n′} where n′ < n, above it (the second revision had the two labels interchanged; derivation audit of 2026-09-04); the first version of this plan printed n′ − n without the modulus, which is wrong above the diagonal by (iη)^{−2|n′−n|} and was caught by the 2026-09-04 critique, the benchmark script having used the correct branches), but the truncated matrix is not unitary: its top column loses 9% of its norm at n_max = 10 and η = 0.1, 30% at n_max = 40, and about half at η ≥ 0.5, so a state that reached the boundary would lose norm rather than be reflected. Measured on this machine on 2026-09-04 (`bench_numerics.py`, Appendix D) **[recomputed here]**:

| d_m (Fock levels) | η | max element difference, block n, n′ < d_m/2 | analytic norm loss, column d_m/2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 0.1 / 0.5 / 1.0 | 2 × 10⁻¹³ / 2 × 10⁻⁶ / 1 × 10⁻³ | 3 × 10⁻⁸ / 6 × 10⁻³ / 0.3 |
| 10 | 0.1 / 0.5 / 1.0 | 3 × 10⁻¹⁶ / 7 × 10⁻⁸ / 2 × 10⁻⁴ | 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ / 1 × 10⁻³ / 0.18 |
| 20 | 0.1 / 0.5 / 1.0 | 1 × 10⁻¹⁶ / 1 × 10⁻¹⁵ / 3 × 10⁻⁹ | 1 × 10⁻¹⁶ / 3 × 10⁻⁸ / 3 × 10⁻³ |
| 40 | 0.1 / 0.5 / 1.0 | 7 × 10⁻¹⁶ / 8 × 10⁻¹⁶ / 7 × 10⁻¹⁶ | 1 × 10⁻¹⁵ / 4 × 10⁻¹⁶ / 3 × 10⁻⁹ |

In an ENR space of two modes, the exponential of the ENR sum generator Σ_m iη_m(a_m + a_m†) agrees with the analytic elements, for states with n₁ + n₂ ≤ N_exc/2, to 2 × 10⁻¹⁰, 6 × 10⁻¹⁶ and 2 × 10⁻¹⁶ at η = 0.1 and to 1 × 10⁻⁶, 4 × 10⁻¹⁰ and 7 × 10⁻¹⁶ at η = 0.3 for N_exc = 6, 10 and 16, while remaining unitary to 10⁻¹⁵. That exponential of the sum generator is the operator definition in an ENR space: because the space is not a tensor product, the per-mode generators do not commute there (maximum commutator element 0.33 at N_exc = 10, η = 0.3 and 0.2, against exactly 0 in the product space) and the product of per-mode exponentials is a different operator near the cap (maximum element difference 1.3 × 10⁻² for the three modes of Section 11.1 at N_exc = 10, 7 × 10⁻¹⁶ inside n_total ≤ N_exc/2; 2026-09-04 critique, measured on 5.3.1), so the product form, which is the physical operator and the one the factorized kernel of Section 11.3 applies, is used in product spaces only, and the two are never swapped as an optimization. The rules the code follows: (i) the operator in the Hamiltonian is the matrix exponential, in the per-mode or the ENR space, because unitarity is what keeps the norm and the boundary monitor meaningful; (ii) the analytic elements are the test oracle: a `HilbertSpace(modes, expected_n_range, η_max)` asserts at construction that the interior elements of its exponentials match them over the declared expected occupation range to a tolerance read from the table above (10⁻¹² where the table reaches it, the table's own value otherwise), the truncation monitor of Section 5.5 reports when the actual population exceeds the declared range, and the margins the table implies, about 6 levels at η ≤ 0.1, 10 at η ≤ 0.5 and 20 at η ≈ 1, are stored as a fixture with interpolation in η rather than as a rule of thumb; (iii) the Rabi-frequency table Ω_{n′,n} of Section 4.3.1 and every closed-form test are evaluated from the analytic form, never from the exponential; (iv) the boundary monitor of Section 5.5 is what makes either construction trustworthy, since both are wrong at the boundary.

The exact displacement operator is dense within each mode's Fock space: |n⟩ couples to every |n′⟩ with weight of order η^{|n′−n|}(n_>!/n_<!)^{1/2}/|n′ − n|!, and the elements above 10⁻¹⁰ reach beyond |n′ − n| = 8 for η = 0.1 at n ≈ 8. The drive operator σ₊^i ⊗ Π_m D_m therefore has 2^{N−1} Π_m n_max,m² non-zeros, that is Π_m n_max,m/2 per row rather than "a few", and this, not the step count, is what sets the cost model of Section 11. Sparsifying by dropping elements below a threshold is an approximation of the truncation kind and is offered with the dropped weight reported, but at n_max ≈ 8 and η ≈ 0.1 it removes almost nothing; the structural remedy is to apply D as a product of mode-local factors (Section 11.3).

### 5.2 Frames and the form of the Hamiltonian

The simulator works in the frame rotating at each ion's qubit frequency (optical rotating-wave approximation) but in the **Schrödinger picture for the motion**. In this frame the free Hamiltonian is H_0 = Σ_m ħω_m a_m†a_m (plus any qubit-frequency offsets that are being modelled as static detunings), and every drive on ion i is a term of the form

H_drive,i(t) = (ħ/2) Σ_tones Ω_i,tone(t) e^{−i(μ_tone t − φ_tone(t))} σ_+^{(i)} ⊗ D_i(Δk) + h.c.,  φ_tone ≡ Δk·X̄_i − Δφ_i,

where D_i(Δk) = Π_m D_m(iη_im) is the product of displacement operators over all modes with the ion's Lamb-Dicke parameters for the drive's effective wavevector, μ_tone is the tone's detuning from the carrier (Section 13), and φ_tone is the tone's phase in the ion's frame: the beam-phase difference Δφ_i offset by the geometric phase Δk·X̄_i of the ion's equilibrium position, which is the e^{+i(Δk·X̄_i − Δφ_i)} of Section 13 and the e^{i(δkX̄_i − μ_i t − δφ_i)} of Section 4.3.1 written as one symbol; Δφ_i is defined as the phase of the beat note written E ∝ cos(ω_L t − Δk·r + Δφ_i), so that for a Raman pair with Δk = k₁ − k₂ and beam fields E_j ∝ cos(k_j·r − ω_j t + φ_j) it is Δφ_i = φ₂ − φ₁ (the relative sign of Δk·X̄_i and the optical phase is physical, the field depending only on their sum in the other ordering, and a virtual RZ(θ) shifts every later φ_tone by φ → φ − θ, the time-order rule of Section 7.6 and the one pinned sign, tested on a concrete sequence (RZ(0.1) then GPi2(0) on |0⟩ against independently multiplied matrices, Section 9.6) rather than by asserting that a rule is pinned; the second revision printed +θ here against 7.6's −θ, which the 2026-09-04 architect critique caught, and the derivation audit of 2026-09-04 had found the definition missing) **[corrected: derivation audit, 2026-09-04]**. A native gate's phase parameter φ (Section 7.6) is φ_tone in the qubit's phase frame; the virtual-Z rule, the MS spin phase of Section 4.4.2 and the vector addition of crosstalk drives in Section 6.6 all refer to this one sign, and the Section 9.6 test that GPi2(0) on |0⟩ produces the state of the Section 7.6 matrix pins it. The reasons for this choice, rather than the interaction picture with respect to the motion:

- The operator part σ_+ ⊗ D_i is **time independent**, so it is built once (a sparse matrix) and the only time dependence is a complex scalar coefficient per tone, which is exactly what QuTiP's `QobjEvo([H0, [A, c(t)]])` handles efficiently.
- The exact displacement operator includes the carrier, all sidebands, and all Debye-Waller factors automatically; nothing is expanded in η.
- Off-resonant terms (carrier during a sideband drive, spectator modes, counter-rotating sideband terms) are present without special handling. Their neglect, when a user wants the textbook model, is done by the builder's `lamb_dicke_order` and `rwa` options, which replace D_i by its expansion to a given order and keep only tones near a chosen resonance.
- The cost is that H_0 sets the fastest timescale: the component |n⟩ of the state rotates at nω_m, so the stiffness grows with the truncation cap. Measured with DOP853 at rtol 10⁻⁸ and atol 10⁻¹⁰ (`bench_numerics.py`, Appendix D) **[recomputed here]**, a 2 µs carrier π pulse on a 3 MHz mode costs 1.3 × 10³ right-hand-side evaluations at n_max = 6 and 3.2 × 10³ at n_max = 41, and a 10 µs detuned spin-dependent force 5.1 × 10³ and 1.6 × 10⁴: a factor 2.5 to 3 for a sevenfold larger cap, because the error control weighs the small high-n amplitudes lightly. That is 14 to 45 steps per period of the mode, at 12 evaluations per DOP853 step, so a 100 µs gate on a 3 MHz mode needs 4 × 10³ to 1.3 × 10⁴ steps. The alternative, the interaction picture with respect to H_0, replaces σ₊ ⊗ D by Σ_k e^{ikω_m t} σ₊ ⊗ A_k over the 2n_max + 1 sideband operators A_k (the elements of D with n′ − n = k); it holds the evaluation count flat (0.6 × 10³ and 3.7 × 10³ to 4.2 × 10³ in the same tests, agreeing with the Schrödinger-picture state to 10⁻⁸) but needs 2n_max + 1 operators per drive term, Π_m(2n_max,m + 1) for M modes, and ran 5 to 10 times slower in wall time at n_max = 41. It is therefore the option `frame="interaction"`, whose useful form truncates the sideband sum at |k| ≤ k_max and reports the dropped weight Σ_{|k|>k_max}‖A_k‖, an approximation of the truncation kind validated against the default; the default stays the Schrödinger picture. Section 11 gives the measured wall times.

Coefficients are supplied as Python functions with the QuTiP 5.3 signature `f(t, **kwargs)` or as sampled NumPy arrays with `tlist` (cubic spline by default, `order=0` for step functions); string coefficients are avoided because compiling them requires Cython, which is optional in this environment, and the fallback is a slow `eval`.

When a laser phase noise realization or an amplitude noise realization is part of a shot, it enters as a sampled array coefficient, so the same builder serves noisy and noiseless pulses.

**Spectator modes that are not resolved dynamically.** A mode may be frozen for a pulse when the closed-form residual displacement and entangling-angle contribution of the Section 4.4.3 integrals, evaluated with the pulse's detuning schedule and the mode's n̄, fall below the run's tolerances, |α_m(τ)|²(2n̄_m + 1) below the residual-displacement tolerance and |χ_m(τ)| below a freeze tolerance that is looser than Section 11.3's dropped-mode rule and distinct from it: every mode is assigned to exactly one of three classes, dropped when |α_m|²(2n̄_m + 1) < 10⁻⁶ and |χ_m| < 10⁻⁴ (nothing absorbs a dropped mode's loss), frozen when above that pair but with |χ_m| below the miscalibration the entangling-gate calibration of Section 7.5 absorbs (default 0.05 rad, the χ_m loss folded into the calibration target and reported per mode), and resolved otherwise, the class recorded per mode in `Diagnostics.mode_class`; the second revision said freezing shared the dropped-mode criterion while describing a frozen spectator that loses 0.03 rad of χ, 300 times the angle tolerance, so that freezing was unreachable as written and with it the default of Section 5.4 (2026-09-04 architect and numerics critiques) **[corrected: critique, 2026-09-04]**; the detuning guard |μ_tone − ℓω_m| > 20 η_m Ω √(n_m + 1) for all ℓ ≠ 0 is kept only as a sanity check whose implied off-resonant excitation (1/20)² = 2.5 × 10⁻³ per sideband is reported. The first version of this plan used the guard as the criterion, which is both too loose (7 × 10⁻² summed over fifteen spectators against the 10⁻⁶ threshold Section 11.3 demands of a dropped mode) and never satisfied in a long chain, whose neighbouring transverse modes lie 10 to 160 kHz apart against a 286 kHz window at n̄ = 20 with the numbers below (caught by the 2026-09-04 experimentalist critique) **[corrected]**. When the criterion holds the mode's state does not change during the pulse beyond the off-resonant excitation, and the drive sees only the Debye-Waller operator D_m projected onto the mode's Fock state, e^{−η_m²/2} L_{n_m}(η_m²) (Section 4.3.1). The option `spectators="frozen"` removes such modes from the joint space, samples n_m once per shot from the mode's thermal distribution, and multiplies Ω_i by the exact Debye-Waller factor of that Fock state: Wineland 1998's frozen-spectator statistics (Eqs. 122-127, Section 4.2.7) made an engine option. Its error bound is the summed off-resonant excitation probability (η_m Ω √(n_m + 1)/(μ − ℓω_m))² over the nearest sidebands, reported together with the frozen mode's closed-form residual displacement α_m(τ) and its contribution χ_m(τ) to the entangling angle from the Section 4.4.3 integrals, because for a two-qubit gate the leading effect of freezing a mode is the loss of χ_m, a coherent miscalibration of first order in the coupling (about 0.03 rad, 4% of π/4, for η = 0.05, Ω = 2π × 62.5 kHz, δ_m = 2π × 100 kHz and τ = 100 µs), which the calibration target absorbs so that a frozen-spectator run is calibrated to the same entangling angle as the joint run; Section 9.9 tests χ and the final probabilities against the fully joint run. It is what makes Doppler-cooled spectator modes (n̄ ≈ 10 to 20) affordable at all, since resolving them dynamically would need n_max ≈ 100 per mode; it applies to the modes of other families and to axial modes far from the tones, not to the transverse spectators of a long chain within tens of kilohertz of the tones, which cannot be frozen and must be resolved, which is why long chains run in GATE_LOCAL with the mode set the contribution criterion of Section 11.3 selects.

### 5.3 Solvers

- **Pure-state evolution** (`sesolve`) whenever the run has no continuous dissipation to represent, or when dissipation is handled by quantum trajectories. Integrator: `dop853` by default with atol 10⁻¹⁰, rtol 10⁻⁸ (tightened by the convergence check) and `nsteps` raised to 10⁷; it was 10 to 25% faster than `vern9` on the Mølmer-Sørensen benchmark of Section 11 with final states agreeing to 6 × 10⁻⁷ in norm (`bench_ms_timing_v4.py`; the second revision said 'identical', which at these tolerances means 10⁻⁷ and not 10⁻¹⁵), while `adams` (scipy's zvode multistep method) aborts with "excess work" at n_max ≈ 40 unless `nsteps` is raised and is not used for oscillatory Hamiltonians **[recomputed here]**. The adaptive step density measured in Section 5.2, 14 to 45 steps per period of the highest mode, is the budget; the output grid is separate and only needs to resolve what is stored. The measured failure mode is stiffness at large caps: with atol 10⁻¹⁰ `dop853` aborts ("problem is probably stiff") at d_m = 121 on the Section 11.1 pulse while succeeding at 101, and succeeds at 121 with atol 10⁻⁸ or with `vern9` (2026-09-04 critique, checked on 5.3.1), so the solver applies an escalation ladder `dop853` → `vern9` → a larger `max_step` budget at atol 10⁻⁸, and never a multistep method: QuTiP's `bdf` is scipy's zvode with a dense finite-difference Jacobian (D extra right-hand sides per Jacobian and an O(D³) factorization, 67 MB at D = 2048), and the failure is not stiffness anyway, since the spectrum of −iH lies on the imaginary axis, where BDF of order 3 and above is unstable and BDF2 strongly damped, so a damping multistep rung would destroy unitarity on exactly the oscillatory problem it is asked to rescue; atol is keyed to the measured points, 10⁻¹⁰ up to d_m ≈ 100 and 10⁻⁸ above, validated at d_m = 121, 151 and 201 (the second revision's rule atol ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ × d_m/50 returned 2.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ at the very case that motivated it, 40 times tighter than the 10⁻⁸ measured to clear the abort, so the ladder as written still aborted there; 2026-09-04 numerics critique) **[corrected: critique, 2026-09-04]**; the solver records the integrator used in the diagnostics and reports a tightened convergence run that aborts rather than losing it.
- **Density-matrix evolution** (`mesolve`) for small spaces (dimension below about 50 to 100, the Liouvillian below 10⁴ × 10⁴). This is the reference mode for validating heating, dephasing and scattering channels and a reference-only path, not a default: one Liouvillian application costs about 2D times one H·ψ, the measured `mesolve`/`sesolve` wall-time ratio at dimension 64 on the Section 11.1 Hamiltonian is 69.5, and with the operator construction the v3 benchmark used (the exponential of the joint generator, which returns a Dense `Qobj`) `liouvillian` returns Dense, 130 GB at D = 300, so the drive operator is built as a tensor product of per-mode exponentials (CSR with exactly the predicted non-zeros, `bench_ms_timing_v4.py`) with an explicit `.to("CSR")` guard before any `liouvillian` call (the second revision put the boundary at 300 and made `mesolve` the default there; 2026-09-04 numerics critique) **[corrected: critique, 2026-09-04]**; hot-mode cases such as a Doppler-cooled n̄ ≈ 20 need d_m ≳ 150 for a boundary population below 10⁻⁴ and run as trajectories.
- **Quantum trajectories** (`mcsolve`, `improved_sampling=True`, parallel `map`) for larger spaces with Lindblad channels: heating quanta, scattering events and leakage appear as jumps; the ensemble over trajectories and over quasi-static noise samples is the physical ensemble that shots are drawn from. With `improved_sampling=True` QuTiP evolves the no-jump trajectory once as a deterministic member carrying weight p_no-jump and gives the stochastic trajectories the residual weight, so shots must be drawn from the weighted mixture (`deterministic_weight_info`), never uniformly from the stored trajectories, which would bias per-shot observables by the no-jump probability (0.62 against the correct 0.32 on a damped-oscillator check; 2026-09-04 critique); the per-trajectory seeds are passed explicitly as a list spawned from the run's root seed (Section 3.4).
- **Piecewise propagators** (`propagator` with `piecewise_t`) only on internal-state-only spaces (Section 11.3 item 6), where the dimension is small and one propagator serves many initial states; on the joint space a propagator is a dense object costing D state evolutions and the calibration scans vary H rather than the initial state, so none is built there, and because `piecewise_t` requires a piecewise-constant generator, segmenting a smooth envelope for it is a declared approximation whose segmentation error is reported.
- **Floquet** (`FloquetBasis`, `fmmesolve`) is reserved for the micromotion module when the rf drive is included explicitly (Section 4.1 option); it is not on the default path.
- **Bloch-Redfield** (`brmesolve`) is available for colored-noise dephasing where a spectral density rather than a white Lindblad rate is the right description (magnetic-field noise with a known S_B(ω)); it is an option for static-H idle intervals and for internal-state-only spaces (the optical Bloch layer of Section 4.2.8), never inside a driven pulse, where the Bloch-Redfield tensor would have to be rebuilt in the dressed basis at every step and the secular approximation fails for a strongly driven system; during pulses coloured noise enters as the sampled time series of Section 6.1(d).

### 5.4 Fidelity levels

- **JOINT_EXACT.** The full state of all N ions and all M included modes is evolved through every pulse. This is the only mode with no approximation beyond truncation. Practical range, from the measured cost model of Section 11: a joint dimension of about 2 × 10³, that is two or three ions with two or three dynamically resolved modes, or more ions with one or two resolved modes and frozen spectators, at minutes per entangling pulse, with a hard ceiling set by the drive operator's memory (Section 11.5); the range "N ≤ 8 under ENR" of the first version of this plan is withdrawn, since at N = 8 with eight axial modes and N_exc = 6 the drive operator alone has 1.15 × 10⁹ non-zeros and 18.5 GB per ion (2026-09-04 critique, measured). Every validation result in Section 9 is produced in this mode, with the resolved-mode set and the frozen-spectator contribution stated per test (Section 9 preamble).
- **GATE_LOCAL.** Used when the joint space is too large. For each pulse, the simulator builds the exact joint space of the addressed ions (plus any ions that receive crosstalk light above a threshold) and all modes with non-negligible coupling to them, initializes the motional part from the tracked motional model, evolves exactly, and then extracts (a) the reduced operation on the addressed ions' internal states as a completely positive map by state-based process tomography: 16 linearly independent pure internal inputs for two qubits are propagated as state vectors (`mcsolve` when channels are active, with the trajectory count fixed from a stated map-accuracy rule, n_traj ≈ 1/ε_map per input state so that the multinomial error of each output's populations sits below the target ε_map, the sixteen population `e_ops` passed explicitly because `mcsolve` raises `ValueError` on `target_tol` without `e_ops`, and `mesolve` as the deterministic cross-check where the gate-local dimension is below about 100), the Choi matrix is reconstructed by least squares and projected onto the intersection of the completely positive cone and the trace-preserving affine set by alternating (Dykstra) projection with the residual against both constraints reported, because a positive-semidefinite projection alone leaves Tr_out(Choi) ≠ 𝟙 and the extracted map then changes the register's normalization on every application (2026-09-04 numerics critique) **[corrected: critique, 2026-09-04]**, the map is applied to the register, by Kraus sampling when the register is a pure-state ensemble, and the extraction is recomputed per quasi-static sample and cached by (pulse parameters, motional-model hash, noise sample), so that GATE_LOCAL costs 16 × n_traj state evolutions of the gate-local space, a few hundred trajectories per input at ε_map = 10⁻³ and therefore more than one `mesolve` of the same space where `mesolve` fits (the second revision counted 16; 2026-09-04 numerics critique), which is what Section 11.2 charges; and (b) the updated motional model: the reduced density matrix of every mode that was in the pulse's joint space, an (n_max + 1)² matrix per mode and so cheap to carry, from which the mean occupation, the Fock distribution and the residual coherent displacement follow, together with the untouched thermal states of the modes that were not in the space. The N-qubit register is carried as a density matrix for N ≤ 12 or as a stochastic pure-state ensemble beyond that, and the map is applied to it. The approximation is that spin-motion and mode-mode correlations left after a pulse are traced out rather than carried to the next pulse; the loss is quantified by the residual displacement |α_m(τ)| per spin eigenstate and by the purity deficit of the reduced motional state that the extraction reports, and by direct comparison with JOINT_EXACT on 3- and 4-ion circuits in the test suite.
- Both levels share prep, readout, noise sampling and result formats. A result records which level ran. Replaying derived per-gate channels for large circuits is not a core fidelity level: the separate application package of Section 14 builds that on its own side from the channel summaries the core already exports (Section 6.8), and the core neither knows nor needs to know about it.

### 5.5 Convergence and truncation policy

- The truncation monitor records, for each mode and each pulse, the population in the top two Fock levels (or the top ENR shell). If it exceeds a threshold (default 10⁻⁶ of the population the pulse moves, with a per-test override for hot-mode cases, since a thermal tail at n̄ = 20 falls below 10⁻⁶ only near n ≈ 275), or if the cap's margin above the populated range falls below the Section 5.1.1 margin for the pulse's η, the run is repeated with the cap raised, up to a configured limit, and the final report states both the boundary population and the margin reached.
- Tolerance convergence: a run can be asked to repeat with tolerances tightened by a factor of 10 and to report the change in final probabilities; the test suite does this for every validation case.
- Time-step convergence for array coefficients: programmed envelopes are sampled at 20 times their bandwidth and interpolated with the default cubic spline; noise realizations are generated by the exact Ornstein-Uhlenbeck update on an independent fixed grid with Δt ≤ τ_c/10, stored in the run record and interpolated with `order=1` (never on the solver's accepted steps, which `dop853` chooses adaptively, so that a realization generated there could not be reproduced; 2026-09-04 architect critique), because a cubic spline through a rough sample path injects spectral content the process does not have, and a test compares the realized coefficient's periodogram with the configured spectrum over the band the pulse is sensitive to; the schedule module checks both.

### 5.6 Units and constants

Internal units are SI with angular frequencies in rad/s and time in seconds; the Hamiltonian is divided by ħ so that all energies are rad/s. User-facing APIs accept frequencies in Hz (cycles) with `2π` applied internally, and document it at every entry point, because factor-of-2π errors are the single most common bug in this domain. Physical constants come from CODATA with their source recorded in `units.py`; atomic masses from the AME tables with citation in each species module.

### 5.7 The governing equations in one place

Everything the simulator integrates is one master equation on the joint space of Section 5.1,

ρ̇ = −i[H(t), ρ] + Σ_k 𝒟[L_k]ρ,  𝒟[L]ρ = LρL† − ½{L†L, ρ},  H(t) = H_phys(t)/ħ in rad/s (Section 5.6), L_k = √γ_k A_k in s^{−1/2},

(the terms listed below are written as H_phys with their ħ; the second revision wrote −(i/ħ)[H, ρ] one paragraph after declaring H already divided by ħ, a units-layer slip caught by the derivation audit of 2026-09-04) run as `sesolve`/`mcsolve` on pure states or `mesolve` on ρ (Section 5.3), with the terms below; each points to the subsection that specifies and tags it, so this list is an index, not a new claim. Every term is switchable and every switch is recorded in the run's diagnostics.

**Hamiltonian, H(t) = H_mot + H_int + Σ_i H_drive,i(t) + Σ_i H_Stark,i(t) + H_anh + H_curv.**

- H_mot = Σ_m ħω_m a_m†a_m: the pseudopotential-frame modes of Section 4.1.3 with the calibrated (noise-shifted) frequencies of 4.1.5, the micromotion factor C₀ already inside every η as `Crystal.lamb_dicke` delivers it (4.1.1; never reapplied here), and per-sample frequency offsets from the quasi-static noise of 6.2.
- H_int = Σ_i (ħΔ_i/2) σ_z^i (generalized to Σ_p ħΔ_{i,p}|p⟩⟨p| when d > 2): the offset of ion i's true transition frequency from the frame frequency, from the magnetic-field sample of 6.3, the ac Zeeman shift of 4.3.3 and the Zeeman structure of 4.5.
- H_drive,i(t) = (ħ/2) Σ_tones Ω_{i,tone}(t) e^{−i(μ_tone t − φ_tone(t))} σ₊^i ⊗ Π_m D_m(iη_{i,m}) + h.c. (4.3.1, 5.2), with Ω from the beam intensities and dipole matrix elements of 4.3.2 and 4.5; the same term on a neighbour j with Ω → ε_{ij}Ω is addressing crosstalk (6.6); the coefficient multiplied by e^{iβ_mm cos(Ω_rf t + δ)} is excess micromotion (4.1.1, 4.3.6); Ω_i → Ω_i(1 + (Ω″/2Ω)x̂_i²) is beam-curvature coupling (6.2); a sampled φ(t) or Ω(t) time series is laser phase or intensity noise (6.3, 6.4); the hardware chain of 7.10 shapes Ω(t) and quantizes μ and φ. When d > 2, σ₊ becomes the set of transition operators |p⟩⟨q| with the pair's own Rabi frequency (4.5).
- H_Stark,i(t) = (ħ/2) δ_St,i(t) σ_z^i, proportional to the instantaneous intensity (4.3.2); the compensating third tone of 4.4.1 is an ordinary drive term.
- H_anh: the cubic Coulomb coupling of 4.1.4, opt-in because it couples all three mode families into one space; the resonance checker and the estimated accumulated phase are on by default.
- H_curv: the static-field anharmonicity and mode-mode resonances of 4.1.4, optional.
- The explicit rf Hamiltonian of 4.1.1 (Landa's construction) replaces H_mot as a validation option only.

**Collapse operators L_k.**

- Heating: √(Γ(N̄+1)) a_m and √(ΓN̄) a_m† per mode (4.1.5, 6.2).
- Motional dephasing (fast part): √(2/τ_m) a_m†a_m (6.2).
- Qubit dephasing (fast part): √(γ_φ/2) σ_z^i, under which the coherence decays at γ_φ, so γ_φ = 1/T₂ for the white component (Section 13); the slow, quasi-static part is a sampled Δ_i in H_int, and a measured T₂ is split into the two before either is used (6.3).
- Photon scattering: √Γ_{i: p→q} |q⟩⟨p| ⊗ R_k for every Raman channel p → q, including channels out of the qubit pair when d > 2, and the elastic (Rayleigh) channel with its differential-amplitude dephasing (4.3.2, 4.5, 6.5), where R_k is the recoil operator of 4.2.8 (a displacement D(ik_photon·x̂) averaged over the dipole emission pattern) or the identity when recoil is switched off.
- Shelf decay during readout: √(1/τ_D) |S⟩⟨D| (8.1).
- Photon counting for time-resolved readout trajectories: the fluorescence collapse operator of 8.2.

**Stochastic elements, sampled once per dynamical sample from one seeded generator (3.1, 6.1):** mode-frequency offsets, qubit-frequency offsets with their inter-ion correlation length, Rabi-frequency scale factors, beam and beat-note phases, frozen spectator Fock states (5.2), and the time series for laser phase and intensity noise.

**Initial state (4.2):** ρ(0) = ⊗_i ρ_int,i ⊗ ⊗_m ρ_th,m(n̄_m), with ρ_int,i the optical-pumping output including its preparation error and ρ_th,m the per-mode thermal (or level-B Fock-distribution) state of the cooling model.

**Measurement (8.2-8.4):** the joint internal outcome is sampled from the final joint state as a projective measurement in the computational basis, so that Bell-state correlations survive; each ion's photon record is then generated conditioned on that outcome, with neighbour crosstalk coupling the records (8.5), and discriminated into a bit. The product POVM of 8.4 is the summary of the record and discriminator layers, computed once per device by the calibration layer, and the fast path that applies it directly to the joint state replaces those layers rather than preceding them, so the readout error is never applied twice (the first version of this plan listed the POVM before the record layer; caught by the 2026-09-04 critique). The fast path's domain is stated: the product POVM is exact only at zero readout crosstalk, because Section 8.5 makes the records neighbour-coupled (added counts on a dark neighbour, camera PSF leakage, neighbour-conditioned likelihoods), so the full path's joint confusion matrix is not a tensor product of per-ion POVMs; at non-zero crosstalk the fast path carries a register-wide 2^N × 2^N confusion tensor with its own size guard (dense to N = 12, factored by neighbour range beyond), and Section 9.5's test is agreement at zero crosstalk plus a bounded, reported discrepancy at the configured crosstalk (2026-09-04 architect critique) **[corrected: critique, 2026-09-04]**.

### 7.6 Native gates, exactly

Two equivalent native sets are supported; the simulator stores both and converts.

**Research notation** (Debnath et al. 2016; Maslov 2017; Trout 2018; Egan 2021) **[verified]**:

R_φ(θ) = [[cos(θ/2), −i sin(θ/2)e^{−iφ}], [−i sin(θ/2)e^{iφ}, cos(θ/2)]] = exp[−i(θ/2)(cos φ σ_x + sin φ σ_y)],  R_x = R₀, R_y = R_{π/2};

XX(χ) = [[cos χ, 0, 0, −i sin χ], [0, cos χ, −i sin χ, 0], [0, −i sin χ, cos χ, 0], [−i sin χ, 0, 0, cos χ]] = exp(−iχ σ_x⊗σ_x),

with |χ| ∈ [0, π/2] set by laser power and the sign α_ij = sgn(χ) fixed per ion pair by which modes the pair couples to most strongly and the detuning chosen; χ = ±π/4 is maximally entangling **[verified]**. The optical-phase-insensitive variant is ZZ_ij(χ), an XX flanked by π/2 single-qubit pulses on each side driven by the same beam pairs (Chen et al. 2023; the Quantinuum wrapper construction) **[verified]**.

**IonQ cloud notation** (IonQ documentation) **[verified]**, all parameters in turns (1 turn = 2π rad):

GPi(φ) = [[0, e^{−2πiφ}], [e^{2πiφ}, 0]] = cos(2πφ)X + sin(2πφ)Y (a π rotation about the equatorial axis at azimuth 2πφ, up to the global phase i);

GPi2(φ) = (1/√2)[[1, −ie^{−2πiφ}], [−ie^{2πiφ}, 1]] = exp[−i(π/4)(cos 2πφ X + sin 2πφ Y)], so φ = 0 gives R_x(π/2), φ = 0.25 gives R_y(π/2), and φ = 0.5 gives R_x(−π/2); the documentation's prose statement that φ = 0.5 gives R_x(+π/2) contradicts its own matrix **[corrected]**;

MS(φ₀, φ₁) = (1/√2)[[1, 0, 0, −ie^{−2πi(φ₀+φ₁)}], [0, 1, −ie^{−2πi(φ₀−φ₁)}, 0], [0, −ie^{2πi(φ₀−φ₁)}, 1, 0], [−ie^{2πi(φ₀+φ₁)}, 0, 0, 1]], and MS(φ₀, φ₁, θ) = exp[−iπθ GPi(φ₀)⊗GPi(φ₁)] with θ ∈ [0, 0.25] turns, reducing to the fully entangling form at θ = 0.25 **[verified]**;

ZZ(θ) = exp(−iπθ Z⊗Z) = diag(e^{−iπθ}, e^{iπθ}, e^{iπθ}, e^{−iπθ}) **[verified]**;

VirtualZ(θ) = diag(e^{−iπθ}, e^{iπθ}) = RZ(2πθ), implemented by shifting the phase parameter of every later gate on that qubit and discarding the Z rotation before measurement. With the documented matrices and the gates read in time order, the shift is φ → φ − θ for every later GPi, GPi2 and MS phase (recomputed here: RZ(θ), GPi(0.5), GPi2(0) equals GPi(0.5 − θ), GPi2(−θ) followed by RZ(θ)); the documentation's printed rule with +θ holds when its diagram is read in matrix order, right to left. The sign is therefore a circuit-ordering convention, and the simulator pins it with this test rather than with the prose **[corrected]**. The frame update is free, but the scheduler still charges the physical dead time the hardware inserts between pulses (14 µs of DDS phase switching in Harty's system, where identity gates are physical delays and a zero-duration Z would change the detuning sensitivity that the measured 1.0(3) × 10⁻⁶ error depends on), and the residual frame phase commutes through ZZ but must be folded into the φ₀ and φ₁ arguments of every later MS gate **[corrected]**. Note that the phase-type parameters appear as e^{±2πiφ} while the angle-type parameters appear as cos(πθ), sin(πθ), because one is a phase and the other a half-angle **[corrected]**.

Conversions: XX(χ) = MS(0, 0, θ) with χ = πθ; R_φ(θ) with θ = π is GPi(φ/2π) up to the global phase i. The `control/native` module holds these matrices with unit tests against the documentation and against `ionq_core`'s helpers.

**JSON encoding of the native set** (IonQ OpenAPI v0.4 specification and the `qiskit-ionq`, `ionq-core-python` and `PennyLane-IonQ` client code, read 2026-09-04) **[verified against the client sources]**: the gate names are `gpi`, `gpi2`, `ms`, `zz` and `nop`; `gpi` and `gpi2` take a scalar `target` and a scalar `phase` in turns; `ms` takes `targets` (two qubits), `phases` (two entries, turns) and `angle`, the entangling angle in turns with default 0.25 (maximally entangling), a range 0 to 0.25 that only the PennyLane plugin's docstring states and the specification does not enforce, and an "in units of π" wording in `ionq_core` that describes the same number (its matrix uses cos πθ, sin πθ); `zz` takes `targets` and `angle` and no phases. The job body is `{"type": "ionq.circuit.v1", "backend": …, "shots": …, "input": {"gateset": "native" | "qis", "qubits": N, "circuit": […]}}`, with `noise: {model, seed}` for the vendor simulator and `settings` for error mitigation and compilation. The specification's other circuit format is OpenQASM 3 (`ionq.qasm3.v1`), not OpenQASM 2, so the package's OpenQASM 2 importer serves general interchange while the IonQ path is native JSON or OpenQASM 3; the OpenQASM 2 export of the client SDKs declares `gpi`, `gpi2`, `ms` and `zz` as custom gates built from `u`, `rz`, `rxx` and `rzz`, which the importer accepts.

### 7.7 Compilation templates, verified

- **CNOT from one XX and four pulses** (Maslov 2017 Fig. 1; Debnath 2016 Fig. 2a) **[verified]**. In time order: RY(vπ/2) on the control; XX(sπ/4); RX(−sπ/2) on the control together with RX(−vsπ/2) on the target; RY(−vπ/2) on the control, with s = sgn(χ) fixed by the ion pair and v = ±1 free. This equals CNOT up to the global phase e^{iπvs/4} for all four sign combinations (recomputed here; the source writes the phase as (−1)^{−vs/4}) **[corrected: phase]**. Debnath's operator-ordered template R_{−z}(π/2)_i R_{−αy}(π/2)_i R_{−x}(π/2)_j XX(απ/4) R_{αy}(π/2)_i equals e^{iπ/4}CNOT for both signs of α **[verified]**. Cost (2τ_1q + τ_2q, 4ε + E) against two XX gates and eight pulses in the older construction of Nebendahl, Häffner and Roos **[corrected reference]**. Trout et al.'s Fig. 8 template with its printed rotation signs is not a CNOT at all, equalling (Z⊗I)·CNOT for s = +1 and (Z⊗X)·CNOT for s = −1; the compiler tests keep it as a negative control **[corrected]**.
- **Controlled phase CP(θ)** from a partially entangling XX(α_ij|θ|/4) plus single-qubit rotations (Debnath Fig. 2b), not exact as drawn: the template is diagonal with the right conditional phase but carries an extra R_z((sgn(θ)π − θ)/2) on both qubits, with overlap 0.854 with CP(θ) at θ = π/2 and 0.691 at π/4 (both reproduced by that rotation; the first version of this plan described the defect as R_z(sgn(θ)π/2), which would give an overlap of 0.5 for every θ), so the compiler appends R_z((θ − sgn(θ)π)/2) on both ions **[corrected]**. The Methods sentence that puts χ_ij = π/4 at θ = ±π/2 contradicts the figure caption's χ = sgn(θ)θ/4, which gives π/8; building CP(π/2) from XX(π/4) yields CZ **[corrected]**.
- **Sign table.** Debnath's published α_ij list covers nine of ten pairs and puts α₂₅ in both sign groups; Maslov's list for the same machine is complete. The compiler takes s from the simulator's own mode structure and detuning and uses neither table **[corrected]**.
- **Controlled X^α** for any real α ∈ [−1, 1] from one XX(sαπ/4) plus four single-qubit pulses (three on the control, one on the target), halving the two-qubit gate count of the two-CNOT construction; controlled Y^α and Z^α by conjugating the target **[corrected: four pulses, not five]**.
- **Arbitrary single-qubit unitary in at most two pulses**: U ≡ R(−π, −c − π/2)·R(2b + π, a − c − π/2) for U = e^{id}[[e^{ia}cos b, e^{ic}sin b], [−e^{−ic}sin b, e^{−ia}cos b]], where "·" is matrix multiplication and the circuit applies the right factor first; degenerate to one pulse when 2b + π ≡ 0 **[corrected: ordering]**.
- **RZ** needs no pulse (virtual) or, where a physical realization is wanted, two: R(π, x)·R(π, x − θ/2) with a free phase x, exact up to a global sign (±1 depending on θ and x, recomputed here) **[verified]** (Maslov's free x is not the virtual-Z technique **[corrected]**).
- **Cost model** for the compiler's choices: single-qubit duration |θ|τ_1q/π with error ∝ |sin θ|ε; two-qubit duration τ_2q with error ∝ |sin 2χ|E (Maslov) **[verified]**; circuit fidelity as Π(1 − e_i) for a single-qubit wire **[verified]**.

Every template is checked numerically against its target unitary, up to global phase, at compile time.

### 7.8 Pulse-shaping practice

Published shapes the scheduler reproduces: nine-segment AM at τ_g = 235 µs for a five-ion chain with ν_x = 3.07 MHz and ν_z = 0.27 MHz, one precomputed shape per pair, gate time scaling roughly as ν_x/ν_z² ∝ n^{1.7} (Debnath 2016) **[verified]**; sixteen-segment AM with linear interpolation between segments (chosen to avoid exciting axial motion) at 225 µs with η ≈ 0.08 for a 15-ion chain at (0.193, 3.077, 3.234) MHz, robust to mode-frequency errors below 1 kHz; the centre ion of an odd chain participates only in spatially symmetric modes (b_{8,2n} = 0) and so needs high power in most of its gates (Egan 2021) **[verified]**; FM designed from only the six highest of 17 transverse modes with AM suppressing unwanted Fourier components (Landsman 2019) **[verified]**; AMFM Fourier-sine designs with about 300 basis functions and K-th-order stabilization on a seven-ion chain (Blümel 2021) **[verified]**; and, on a 36-ion chain with about 10 kHz between neighbouring transverse modes, AM pulses closing all transverse modes with MS durations of 550-883 µs (median 672 µs) and ZZ durations of 770-1103 µs, the detuning placed above the 21st mode (Chen 2023) **[corrected: mode index]**. Single-qubit pulses use SK1 composite sequences with a Gaussian envelope on chip traps to avoid exciting axial motion through light-induced charging (Egan) **[verified]** and are otherwise square with smooth edges. The SK1 phases and order claims of Section 4.3.5 are derived for **square** pulses only, and the exact amplitude filter function of Section 6.9 is pulse-shape independent at fixed total sequence time while the detuning one is not, so a Gaussian-enveloped SK1 keeps its dc amplitude cancellation (the closed polygon Σ_l θ_l ρ̃_a^{(l)} = 0 depends only on the areas and the toggled axes) but not its detuning behaviour; the scheduler therefore emits the shaped pulse as a piecewise-constant approximation whose segment count is chosen by the Section 5.5 convergence policy, and reports the residual against the square-pulse 2 × 2 result.

### 7.9 Calibration recipes, as the sources run them

- **Carrier Rabi frequency**: Rabi flopping on each ion; fit with the thermal Debye-Waller envelope (Section 4.2.7); for chains with tightly focused beams fit Cetina's algebraic (non-exponential) contrast loss and phase lag instead of an exponential **[corrected]**.
- **Mode frequencies and Lamb-Dicke parameters**: blue-sideband spectroscopy, P_{|1⟩} = [Ω_i^{p2}/(Ω_i^{p2} + δω_p²/4)] sin²(t√(Ω_i^{p2} + δω_p²/4)) with Ω_i^p = √(n̄+1)Ω_iη_i^p, remembering that this Ω is half the usual Rabi frequency (Blümel 2021 Eq. S28), a half-Rabi form doubled on ingest and never the fit function of Section 7.5 **[verified]**; the extracted |η_i^p| = |Ω_i^p|/(√(n̄+1)Ω_i) feeds the pulse solvers, and the Leung discrepancy (measured Ω four times the prediction) is the failure mode this step guards against.
- **Entangling gate**: prepare |00⟩, apply XX, measure P₀₀ and P₁₁ and scan the AM pulse height until P₀₀ = P₁₁; measure the parity oscillation P₀₀ + P₁₁ − P₀₁ − P₁₀ under an analysis π/2 pulse of scanned phase; the Bell fidelity F = ½(P₀₀ + P₁₁ + Φ) with Φ the parity contrast is a lower bound (Wright 2019) **[verified]**; for partially entangling angles use Debnath's interference sequence, control in |1⟩, target in (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2, CP(θ), then R_x(π/2), giving P(|1⟩) = ½(1 − sin θ) **[verified]**.
- **Randomized benchmarking variants**: Knill-style sequences of π/2 gates with interleaved π or identity gates and a final π/2 into the computational basis, fit to Bp^L + ½ with p the per-π/2-gate fidelity in the authors' convention (Wright) **[verified]**; Clifford RB with the whole random sequence followed by its inverse, fit to a linear or exponential decay (Egan; Mount F = A₀p^L + B₀ with error (1 − p)/2) **[corrected: inverse of the sequence, not of each Clifford]**; direct RB with random layers of XX at probability p_2Q and single-qubit π/2 rotations, fit to a + bp^d, and the two-instance extraction of (r_1Q, r_2Q), whose printed matrix in Chen et al. is singular (both rows [0.75, 0.25]; the equation entered with the November 2024 revision that is the version of record, Quantum 8, 1516, so here the newest text is the one not to copy) and whose right-hand side must be error rates r = (4ⁿ − 1)(1 − p)/4ⁿ rather than raw decay bases **[corrected]**.
- **Detection**: bright and dark histograms, threshold and window (Section 8); Debnath removed detection crosstalk by decomposing into the known 32-state detector response **[verified]**.
- **Application-level**: the Hellinger circuit fidelity F_c = (Σ_i√(p_iq_i))², the #AQ pass criterion F_c − ε_c > 1/e on the QED-C circuits with plurality-vote error mitigation starting at threshold t = 7 and decrementing (Chen 2023) **[verified]**, and the composite expected-fidelity product F_2Q^n F_1Q^{2(n+1)} F_SPAM^{10} used as a crosstalk-free floor (Wright) **[corrected: the caption's qualifier applies to one figure only]**.

### 7.10 The control hardware chain

The pulse a `Schedule` specifies is not the field the ion sees; between them sit a direct digital synthesizer (DDS), an rf amplifier, an acousto-optic or electro-optic modulator and the beam path, and each leaves a signature the simulator must be able to reproduce, because published error budgets attribute errors to them: Harty's 14 µs DDS phase-switching dead time and 5 × 10⁻⁴ amplitude stability (Section 4.3.3), Egan's Gaussian-envelope SK1 pulses chosen so as not to excite axial motion (Section 7.8), Chen's mechanical spin-phase noise (Section 12). None of this has a first-principles source in the research runs; it is standard instrumentation physics **[background]** exposed as user-supplied parameters with documented defaults, and every effect below enters through the drive coefficient Ω(t)e^{−i(μt − φ(t))} of Section 5.2, so no new Hamiltonian term is needed.

- **Frequency, phase and amplitude quantization.** A DDS with a 32-bit frequency tuning word at clock f_clk has a resolution f_clk/2³² (0.23 Hz at 1 GHz), a 14- to 16-bit phase word (2π/2¹⁴ = 0.38 mrad) and a 10- to 14-bit amplitude word; the scheduler rounds the requested tone parameters to the configured grid and the simulator uses the rounded values, so the residual detuning after rounding is part of the physics, not a tolerance.
- **Phase coherence between pulses.** Each tone is generated by an oscillator that runs continuously at its programmed frequency, so the phase of a pulse starting at t₀ is φ_prog + ω_tone t₀ unless the hardware resets the phase at each pulse; the simulator models both, per tone, as `phase="continuous"` or `"reset"`. The frame bookkeeping of Section 4.3.4 and the virtual-Z rule of Section 7.6 assume phase-continuous operation at the nominal qubit frequency, which is the mode the rule was pinned in.
- **Modulator response.** An acousto-optic modulator's rise time, about the beam diameter divided by the acoustic velocity (100 ns per 0.4 mm of beam at 4.2 km/s in tellurium dioxide), low-pass filters the amplitude envelope; the simulator convolves the programmed envelope with a first-order response of that time constant and, optionally, models the amplifier's saturation as a static nonlinearity Ω = Ω_sat tanh(V/V_sat), so that a nominally square pulse acquires the finite edges that determine its off-resonant excitation of neighbouring sidebands. The rf-power-to-Ω map is calibrated by the Rabi scan of Section 7.5 exactly as in the laboratory.
- **Timing.** A per-pulse dead time (DDS phase switching, sequencer latency) and a timing jitter σ_t are scheduler parameters; the dead time is idle evolution under H₀ with the noise channels of Section 6.7 active.
- **Beam pointing and power.** Slow pointing drift changes each ion's intensity through the beam profile of `light/beams.py`, and with it both Ω and the crosstalk ratios ε_ij; it is sampled quasi-statically. Fast intensity noise is the sampled time series of Section 6.4. The optical path difference between two Raman beams sets the beat-note phase, and its mechanical drift is the spin-phase noise that Chen et al. invoke, entered as a user-supplied phase spectrum (Section 12).
- **Detection electronics.** Detector dead time, afterpulsing and SNSPD recovery are the readout-side counterparts (Section 8.8), with the same user-supplied status.

A device preset that omits these parameters gets ideal electronics and says so in its `derived()` report, so a fidelity that depends on them cannot be claimed by accident.

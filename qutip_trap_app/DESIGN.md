# qutip-trap-app: design of the learning tool

The application of PLAN.md Section 14 shows one simulated trapped-ion quantum computer at five levels of abstraction, from a cloud customer's histogram down to the matrix elements the solver integrates. This document fixes how that ladder is made into a learning tool that a newcomer can use without help and a physicist can use without irritation.

Two rules from the plan are also the two rules of the pedagogy. Every screen is a view over one run record, so nothing on any screen is an illustration: a histogram bar is a pile of recorded shots, a Bloch arrow is the reduced density matrix of the recorded state, a pulse is the scheduler's own tones (Section 14.1, rule 1). And every number carries its provenance chip and every result its convergence badge (rules 3 and 4), so the app teaches epistemic hygiene by construction: the learner sees which statements were verified against a source, which were corrected, and whether the simulation itself converged.

Evidence labels below follow the pedagogy skill: **verified** survived an adversarial three-verifier check against primary sources; **lead** is a quoted but unvoted claim; **UX** is a design-reference rule (NN/g, WCAG 2.2, Material 3, Apple HIG). The learning-science evidence is strongest for declarative material; where the app teaches procedures (reading a schedule, judging convergence) the same mechanics are used but the gains are not promised.

## 1. Who learns here, and what they must be able to do

The pedagogy design starts from four questions.

**Prior knowledge.** Unknown per person, and it flips the sign of instructional support (verified: high-assistance instruction helps low-prior-knowledge learners, d = +0.505, and harms high-prior-knowledge learners, d = -0.428; Tetzlaff et al. 2025). So the app asks once, on first launch, with three plain self-descriptions and a visible way to change the answer at any time:

| Answer | What it changes |
|---|---|
| "New to quantum computing" | explanations open at the one-sentence depth; the worked example runs first; plain labels first, the physics term on hover; numerics panel closed; chips collapsed to their glyph |
| "I know circuits, not the hardware" | Levels 0 and 1 as for a physicist, Levels 2 to 4 as for a newcomer; explanations open at the picture depth |
| "Physicist; I know the hardware" | explanations collapsed, opening at the equation depth; problems before examples; numerics panel and chips expanded; symbols first |

When the learner has not answered, the app assists (verified as the authors' rule: the reversal is asymmetric, so assist when in doubt). The choice is a preference, never a gate: every level and every panel is reachable at every setting.

**Retention target.** Learner-set in the Learn view, default 90 days, phrased as the task: "explain what happens between a circuit and its histogram to a colleague, three months from now". The review schedule is derived from it (Section 4, spacing).

**Kinds of knowledge.** Facts (what a shot is; the native gate set), concepts (superposition as an arrow; entanglement from a spin-dependent force; quanta of vibration; truncation), procedures (read a histogram against its target; read a pulse against the mode spectrum; judge a numerics panel), and discriminations (target against simulated; estimate against calibrated; resolved against frozen against dropped modes; verified against extracted). The mechanics differ by kind: block to build, interleave to discriminate (verified pooled effect g = 0.42 for inductive learning; the per-material direction is only directional).

**The delayed, unaided task.** Given a circuit the learner has not seen and a histogram bar that is off its target, follow the run from the bar to the Hamiltonian term responsible, with the explain panel closed, and name the device parameter that would change it. This six-click path is the assessment. The app measures nothing else as "learning": in-session correctness and "that makes sense" are shown, labelled as in-session, and never reported as mastery (verified: in-session fluency is a poor proxy).

## 2. The zoom ladder is the learning ladder

Each level introduces exactly the concepts needed to read the next, and every thing on a screen that has an inside can be opened: that is what "dig deeper, literally" means here. The zoom-in targets are the plan's (Section 14.2); the concepts are the learning layer's (`viewmodel/learn.py`).

| Level | The question it answers | Concepts introduced | Open this to go deeper |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Machine | What did the machine return, and how does it compare with a perfect one? | shot, histogram, bit order, target against simulated, readout error | a bar opens its shots; a shot opens its photon counts; a gate name opens Level 1; the device card opens the Level 4 pages |
| 1 Circuit | What did each gate do to the qubits? | Bloch arrow, native gates, compilation, virtual Z, entanglement by MS | a gate opens its pulses at Level 2; a channel opens its tomography; the phase register opens the virtual-Z rule |
| 2 Schedule | What light hit which ion, when, and what did it talk to? | pulse, modes, tones and sidebands, loop closure, crosstalk | a pulse opens Level 3; a tone opens the Hamiltonian term it produces; a mode opens the crystal page |
| 3 Dynamics | What happened inside one pulse? | spin-dependent force, Fock states, Debye-Waller, quantum jumps, truncation and convergence | a term opens its matrix elements at Level 4; a jump opens its collapse operator |
| 4 Physics | Where do the numbers come from? | the Hamiltonian, Lamb-Dicke parameter, trap and Mathieu, atomic structure, noise as physics, calibration | any parameter re-derives every level above (Section 14.4) |

The ladder is also the app's answer to Hick's law (UX): the whole physics is never on one screen. Each level has one job, one primary action, and a small set of things to click.

## 3. Pedagogical mechanics

**Predict, then reveal** (verified: retrieval practice beats restudy, g about 0.5). Before a result appears, the app asks for it. Level 0: sketch the histogram by dragging bars, or pick one of three sketches, before Run reveals the target and the simulated bars; the sketch is scored against the record (total variation, and which bars miss by more than two error bars), never against a script. Level 1: point the Bloch arrow before the gate's state is drawn. Level 2: pick which mode the tones sit beside. Level 3: predict whether the loop closes. Every prompt is one tap to skip, and the skip is not recorded as wrong. The feedback names the task ("the sketch differs beyond the uncertainty on 01"), never the person (lead: Kluger and DeNisi 1996, feedback about the self attenuates the effect).

**Worked example first, then faded, then free** (verified: novices profit from full guidance that fades; experts from problems first). The Bell-state job is the worked example: a six-stop tour with every stop annotated (`learn.BELL_TOUR`). The faded version is a three-ion GHZ job with the same six stops and the annotations withheld until asked. The free version is "build your own circuit, predict, run, explain one off-target bar". A physicist starts at the free version and can open the tour from it.

**Three depths of explanation** (UX: progressive disclosure). Every concept explains itself in one plain sentence with no symbols, then as a picture ("what to look at on this screen and what it shows"), then as the equation the ledger states with the plan section and the source. The starting depth follows prior knowledge; the deeper button is always present. The equation depth is the same text the provenance chip opens, so the explanation and the number it explains share one source.

**Self-explanation with a computed reveal** (moderate utility: Dunlosky et al. 2013, thin evidence in 2013 rather than a small effect). At natural pauses the app asks for one sentence ("why are the 01 and 10 bars not exactly zero?") and then shows the simulator's own answer: the intrinsic error budget with its terms and the readout errors, every number a chip. The learner compares their sentence with the physics, and the app does not grade prose.

**Interleaved discrimination drills** (verified pooled effect; block when building, interleave when telling apart). The Learn view mixes short discrimination items in immediate succession: is this number an estimate or calibrated; is this chip verified or extracted; is this mode resolved, frozen or dropped; is this bar's deviation inside its error bar. Concept-building content stays blocked by level.

**Spacing** (verified: 47.3 % recall spaced against 36.7 % massed; the optimal gap is a declining share of the retention target, 20 to 40 % at one week and 5 to 10 % at one year, and overshooting costs little). The Learn view has a review tray. A concept becomes due when its last exposure is older than the long end of the gap computed from the learner's retention target (`learn.review_gap_days`), and the tray re-asks that concept's prompt without the explanation open. The mastery log keeps session accuracy and delayed unaided accuracy apart, and only the second is called evidence. The tray is a place the learner visits, never a notification; the app has no notifications.

**Every explanation ends in a prompt** (the pedagogy skill's rule). `learn.ExplainCard` carries its prompt, and a concept with no prompt fails the build (`tests/test_learn.py`).

**What the app refuses to do.** It does not optimize for in-session accuracy or perceived helpfulness (lead: an unguarded tutor raised practice scores and lowered unaided exam scores, Bastani et al. 2025). It does not draw anything it did not compute. It does not report a number without a tag or a result without a badge. It does not collect engagement metrics; the mastery log is local, exportable by the learner, and never uploaded.

## 4. Usability

**Routes and the zoom bar** (Section 14.6). `/job/{id}` is Level 0 and the home screen; `/job/{id}/circuit/{gate}`, `/job/{id}/schedule/{pulse}`, `/job/{id}/dynamics/{pulse}/{sample}` are Levels 1 to 3; `/device/{page}` are the Level 4 pages; `/learn` is the tour, the drills and the review tray. A breadcrumb zoom bar shows the path (job, gate, pulse, sample) and the level; zooming out is one click on any crumb, the Escape key, or Cmd/Ctrl and minus; zooming in is a click on the thing itself or Cmd/Ctrl and plus. Browser history works in the served mode because the routes are real.

**One primary action per screen** (UX: exactly one primary button per view). Level 0: Run. Levels 1 to 3: Zoom in on the selected item, with Verify deeper as the secondary action. Level 4: Apply (a parameter change), which immediately re-derives the analytic layer and marks the calibrated numbers stale. Learn: Start the tour, or Review, whichever is due.

**Response budgets** (UX: under 400 ms keeps flow; 1 to 10 s a working state; over 10 s progress and cancel), mapped to Section 14.7:

| Action | Budget | What the learner sees |
|---|---|---|
| open any level from the record | under 400 ms | the view, no indicator |
| zoom into a pulse at joint dimension at or below 256 | at or below 1 s | the recorded coarse trace at once, the fine trace replacing it |
| zoom into a pulse at larger dimension | seconds to minutes | recorded trace at once; a progress bar with a cancel button; the view fills in |
| verify deeper, jump ensemble, process matrix | background job | progress, cancel, and the result appearing beside the shallow one when done |
| Level 4 parameter change | immediate for the analytic layer | derived numbers update; calibrated numbers get a stale badge until the user starts the recalibration job (the surrogate calibration of Section 7.5, seconds) |

**Layout** (UX numbers). Material window classes: on a compact window the levels are a bottom bar of five labelled icons and the explain panel is a sheet; on medium and expanded windows a navigation rail with the five levels and Learn, the explain drawer on the right, the numerics panel as a collapsible strip at the bottom. Spacing on an 8 pt grid with 4 px half-steps; body text 16 px, reading measure at most 66 characters in the explain drawer; primary controls at least 44 px; every interactive element with hover, focus, active and disabled states; every color pair tested to 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for outlines in both themes; dark surface #121212 with text at 87 % white and accents desaturated; motion 150 to 250 ms, honoring the platform's reduced-motion setting, never flashing. Charts follow the `dataviz` skill's rules: the categorical palette for series, sequential for heatmaps, the same colors for the same quantity on every level.

**No meaning by colour alone.** Tags are a glyph and a word (`✓ verified`, `✎ corrected`, `❝ extracted`, `○ background`, `⌗ recomputed`, `→ derived`, `? contested`); the badge is the word pass, not checked, or fail, with its reasons; the target bars are hatched and labelled, not merely a second colour.

**Plain language, then the term, then the symbol, then the chip.** Every label in the catalogue (`viewmodel/catalogue.py`) has a plain form ("how fast the ions vibrate together in this mode") and a term form ("mode frequency, omega_m/2pi"); frequencies are shown in Hz with the 2pi conversion one hover away; units are always printed. Errors say what happened and what to do ("no live run for this record: run the job again to re-simulate").

**Empty, loading and error states are designed.** No job yet: the Bell preset ready to run with one sentence on what will appear. A view whose data the record does not hold says so rather than showing a blank. A failed re-simulation shows the recorded trace and the error message beside it.

**Keyboard and screen readers.** A full tab path per screen, visible focus, Escape closes drawers and zooms out, every chart with a table alternative (the same `Shown` values), every chip with its text on focus.

## 5. Screens

**Level 0, the machine.** Left: the device card (species, ion count, native gate set, SPAM per qubit, gate errors labelled estimate or calibrated, the device hash and seed). Centre: the circuit builder (R16): the qubits as wires, the gates as tiles placed from a palette by a tap, a drag or the + at a wire's end, the selected gate's qubits and angles beneath, the OpenQASM 2 text with an OpenQASM 2 or IonQ JSON import behind Code, the shot count, the engine and the readout (fast, or every ion's photon record, which only the full simulation keeps), and Run. Right, after Run: the histogram with the target bars beside the simulated ones, the error bars, the total variation to the target, and the discarded-shot count. Clicking a bar lists its shots; clicking a shot shows its bits, the sampled levels, the heralds and, on the full readout path, every ion's photon count with the threshold and window. The predict-then-reveal sketch sits where the histogram will appear.

**Level 1, the circuit.** The compiled native timeline on ion lanes; each gate shows its name, frame-applied phases, duration, target unitary, calibrated parameters (Rabi frequency or entangling angle with the table entry's status) and its error estimate or tomography summary. Below, the register after the selected gate: populations, Bloch arrows per ion, Pauli expectations, purity, and the fidelity to the target state so far (read from the recorded traces of a JOINT_EXACT run; derived from the replay's register sequence for a channel-replay run, or from the recorded step channels composed in time order for a GATE_LOCAL run, each labelled derived on the card's info button; a record with none of the three says so in the register's place); the phase register per ion with the stark increments. The compile report shows the block residuals.

**Level 2, the schedule.** Pulses on a time axis per ion, idle intervals and the measurement event marked; under it the mode spectrum with the selected pulse's tones drawn against it and each two-indexed detuning labelled carrier, red, blue or far; the waveform's segment table; the closure indicators per mode with chi_m, |alpha_m| and the residual against the drop threshold; crosstalk onto neighbours; the beams' directions.

**Level 3, the dynamics.** For one gate step, one dynamical sample and one branch of the initial mixture (both selectable): the recorded coarse trace opens at once (the run's own stored points inside the step) and the one primary action, Re-simulate, replaces it with the fine trace from the worker. P1 per ion and the coherence |rho_01| against time; the concurrence and Pauli correlators for two qubits; the mean occupation per mode; the spin-branch loops alpha_im(t) of the played waveform on the run's own modes (Section 4.4.1: two ions times the coupled modes, drawn from the closed-form kernel the solver closed the pulse with; 2 Im of the integral of conj(alpha_a) d alpha_b over the pair's two loops is the closed-form entangling angle, shown beside the table's exact angle so that the Debye-Waller gap of the exact spot check is a number) with how far each failed to close, and the exact simulation's spin-averaged <a_m>(t) as a labelled residue, since the branches' displacements cancel in it for a register with <S_phi> = 0; the Fock distributions at the step's start and end, and P(n, t) as a heatmap from the Fock populations the fine zoom stores at every point; the jumps, each a link to its collapse operator on the Hamiltonian page; the quasi-static values drawn for the sample; the frozen spectators' Debye-Waller contribution; the process matrix of the finished pulse (state-based tomography from the recorded motional state, a background job with the 16 inputs of two qubits) with its Pauli twirl and its |Choi| beside the ideal gate's; and the Section 5.5 re-checks on the zoomed step, whose result colours the badge in the numerics strip. Before the loops are drawn the learner is asked whether they close (predict-then-reveal); the loops stay hidden until the answer or a skip, and the pick is scored against the branch loops, never against the spin-averaged <a_m>(t), which closes trivially. The numerics panel is open here by default for everyone.

**Level 4, the physics.** Eight pages over one device layer that the worker derives from the device model alone: species (identity, the level diagram on a compressed and labelled scale, the qubit pair with its field sensitivities and clock points, the magnetic sublevels at the operating field, the Zeeman shifts against the field, the tabulated transitions with their saturation intensities), trap (secular frequencies, the rf record, a, q, beta and C0 per axis by the monodromy method, the stability diagram with the first region's boundary bisected from the core's `is_stable` and the device's operating point per radial axis, stray field, displacement and excess micromotion), crystal (positions to scale with the selected mode's eigenvector arrows, the 3N-mode spectrum with each mode's uniform-field weight and heating rate, the Lamb-Dicke matrix for the entangling pair's Delta k, the Coulomb length scale, the spacing and the zigzag threshold), light (beam geometry through the ions with the quantization axis, every beam's power, waist, direction, angle to B and intensity at each ion, every derived drive's Rabi frequency, pi time, light shift, residual excited population and scattering budget, the crosstalk ratios of the single-qubit beams, and the scattering error per pulse against wavelength between the fine-structure lines), noise (every spectrum the model stores on a log grid with its apparatus, the heating and dephasing rates they imply, the slow drifts with their correlation times and servos, mains, collisions and the provenance sentence), cooling (the recipe, the stages and what each leaves, the Doppler rate coefficients per mode, the pulsed sideband staircase of <n> after every pulse from the exact transfer matrices, the pumping populations against time with the preparation error and the recoil), readout (the detector, R_o, R_d and R_b per ion with the manifold's ceiling, the exact count histograms at the device window, the error against the window with its interior optimum, the error budget by channel, the three rates against the saturation parameter, and the table's calibrated threshold and window beside them), and the Hamiltonian builder for the zoomed step (H_mot per mode with the sample's offsets, H_int and the Stark terms, every drive term with its operator, its tones, its eta per mode, its frozen-mode Debye-Waller factors, its micromotion and crosstalk factors and the Omega_{n',n} table of every resolved mode as heatmap and table, the collapse operators grouped by channel with their rates and whether the run integrated them, the segments and the recorded approximations), plus the entangling pulse solutions re-solved at once for every pair beside the table's calibrated waveform.

Every page carries the knobs that belong to it (Section 14.4): the rf drive frequency and amplitude, the axial frequency, the stray field and the axis angle on the trap page; the magnetic field on the species page; beam power, waist and wavelength on the light page; the field-noise and magnetic-noise densities, the correlation length and the Rabi drift on the noise page; the stage durations on the cooling page; the detection window, efficiency and background on the readout page. Moving a slider re-derives the whole layer in the worker (about a hundred milliseconds of physics, plus about three seconds when the preparation recipe must be re-derived after a trap, field or beam change) and marks the calibration table stale; Recalibrate, the primary action while stale, runs the surrogate calibration for the edited device (about 15 s) so that the next Run uses it, and the Level 0 device card shows the edited device's re-derived numbers with the stale badge until then. The rf amplitude knob on a trap declared by its secular frequencies scales q at fixed a and lets beta follow from the Mathieu equation, so it needs the rf drive frequency declared first; the page says so. The pulse-solver rows show the closed-form solution beside the waveform the table plays for the same device: the exact spot check's amplitude factor and what the closed form missed (Section 7.8), so the gap between the Lamb-Dicke closed form and the exact integration is a number the learner can open rather than a discrepancy hidden inside a tolerance. Every drawn element is computed; the one exception, a level diagram's vertical spacing, is labelled not to scale.

**Learn.** The prior-knowledge setting; the retention target; the tour (six stops with their prompts); the faded GHZ exercise; the free exercise; the discrimination drills; the review tray of due prompts; the mastery log with its two accuracies labelled.

**The numerics panel** (every level). Fidelity level, joint dimension and dims, caps per mode, mode classes, boundary population against the policy threshold, margins, integrator and tolerances, samples, trajectories and branches, wall time, the convergence report, and the badge with its reasons and the checks not run.

**The explain drawer** (every level). The Part II subsection governing the screen, the concept cards at the learner's depth with the deeper button, the chips of the quantities on screen, and the card's prompt at the end.

## 6. The layers

Every screen reads one run record (`record.py`), built from a run's `Result` and the core's own `RunRecord`; what re-simulation computes later is cached on the record by key. `resim.py` rebuilds the engine as the run built it, chains it over the gate steps to reach the initial state of any step, zooms into one step at fine resolution with the Section 5.5 re-checks, lists the step's Hamiltonian terms and computes its process matrix. `replay.py` is Level 0's default engine: each gate kind's channel is extracted once by the core's GATE_LOCAL tomography from the prepared motional state and conjugated to the played phase, with the frame covariance of that conjugation measured per kind and added to the residual; `verify.py` runs the same job at the next engine and compares. `workers.py` runs all of this in one worker process that keeps the live state per record key (the device, the table, the core's run record, the channel library), caches built presets and tables, and restarts on a hard cancel. `knobs.py` and `device_layer.py` rebuild the preset with the Level 4 overrides and derive every physics page from it. `provenance.py` builds the chip and section index from the ledger and PLAN.md. `viewmodel/` turns records into frozen view dataclasses without importing Flet; `views/` draws them.

## 7. What counts as success

- The rows of PLAN.md Section 14.6 (Testing) that the app owns pass (Section 9 below).
- The delayed, unaided six-click task, tried by a newcomer at least one review gap after the tour, with the explain panel closed. This is the only measure the app will report as learning.
- No engagement metrics. Session accuracy is displayed as such and never aggregated.

## 8. Decisions left to the author

- The three prior-knowledge wordings above, and whether a fourth ("teaching this to others") should unlock the tour's annotations as speaker notes.
- The default retention target (90 days here) and whether the review tray may ever be surfaced outside the Learn view.

## 9. Tests

The rows of PLAN.md Section 14.6 (Testing) live in `qutip_trap_app/tests/`, most of them on one two-ion Bell run per session. Coarse-graining identity: Level 1's populations and Pauli expectations equal the partial trace of the recorded joint state to 1e-12. Re-simulation cache: zooming twice recomputes once, and the cached trace equals a fresh one to solver tolerance. Convergence badge: an under-truncated run turns the badge red and fails the cap re-check. Provenance coverage: every displayed quantity, concept and preset resolves to a tagged ledger record. Channel derivation: the channel replay matches JOINT_EXACT within the residual it reports, and a hotter motional state widens both (`-m slow`). Downward propagation: an rf amplitude change at Level 4 moves beta, nu and eta as Section 4.1 says and the recalibrated table closes the loops at the new modes. Request semantics: XX(0.3) requested at Level 1 is read back from the step's process matrix within 5e-3 rad, and a detuning set by hand at Level 2 opens the loops while the target stays the requested unitary. Presets: every published value resolves to a ledger record with the row's tag, and every experiment preset runs and reports its comparison. Navigation: the six-click path from a histogram bar to a matrix element over the routing model (`views/shell.py`), and the same path under `flet test` over control keys (`tests/test_main.py`, skipped unless `QUTIP_TRAP_APP_UI_TESTS=1`). Text budget: the word count outside the places prose is allowed stays under each screen's budget.

## 10. The information hierarchy

Prose competes with the object it describes for the same attention (UX: the Doherty threshold and Hick's law are about what the eye must process before it can act; NN/g's reading studies put first-glance attention on pictures and headings, not body text), so the picture that answers the level's question, the histogram, the arrows, the tones against the spectrum, the loop, comes first and the text moves behind disclosure. The rules below fix the hierarchy. `tests/test_text_budget.py` checks them without a Flutter client: it walks every view module's `ft.Text` and `status_line` literals by the AST and counts the words outside the places prose is allowed (the drawer's components, the prompts, the empty state).

**R1. One focal picture per screen, first and largest.** Level 0: the histogram with the target beside it. Level 1: the timeline with the selected gate and, under it, the Bloch arrows drawn as arrows. Level 2: the tones against the mode spectrum. Level 3: the phase-space loop beside the qubit populations against time. Level 4: each page's computed drawing (level diagram, stability diagram, crystal, beams, spectra, cooling staircase, count histograms, the terms of H). Learn: the ladder of activities. Before the first run, Level 0's focal object is the Run button. Nothing sits above the focal picture except the zoom bar and the level's title and question.

**R2. Card titles are nouns, and cards have no subtitles.** What a card shows is explained by the concept card behind it in the explain drawer; every card title carries a small "why" button that opens the drawer at that concept.

**R3. Numbers are stat tiles, at most six per card, the rest behind Details.** A stat tile is the value large, its plain label small, and its chip; the physicist plan shows the term instead of the label. Tables, key-value walls, matrices, segment lists and method notes live in a Details disclosure closed by default (open for the physicist plan, `LevelPlan.chips_expanded`). Every visible number is still a zoom-in target where the plan names one.

**R4. Notes, approximations and methods are one info button.** A page's notes (the layer's notes, the cooling approximations, the derivation timing) are the tooltip of an info icon beside the page title, or the last tile of Details. They never appear as body text.

**R5. A sentence outside the drawer is one of five things**: the level's question, a control label, a status line under twelve words (a progress stage, "recorded coarse trace, re-simulate for the fine one"), a refusal or error with its reason, or a prompt of the learning layer. Everything else is a concept card's text, at the learner's depth, in the drawer.

**R6. The numerics strip is a badge.** Collapsed, it shows the word pass, not checked or fail, the engine and the joint dimension. The engine note, the caps, the margins and the re-check results are inside.

**R7. The explain drawer shows one concept at a time.** The concepts of the level are a row of chips; the selected one is open at the learner's depth with its chips, its deeper button and its prompt; a "why" button on a card selects the matching concept. The Part II subsection that governs the screen is a collapsed Specification tile at the bottom, rendering the subsection's own text from the provenance index (which carries every section's Markdown, so a packaged build shows it and a chip's section opens the paragraph it cites).

**R8. Chips are buttons.** A provenance chip is focusable by Tab, shows its tag, source and equation on hover and focus, and on click opens the drawer's Specification tile at the chip's section. No meaning by colour alone: the glyph and word stay.

**Learner-adaptive density.** The plans of Section 1 govern the density: the newcomer sees plain labels, closed Details, one sentence of hint under the focal picture (the concept's sentence depth) and an open drawer; the physicist sees terms, open Details, no hint and a closed drawer; the circuits plan follows the physicist on Levels 0 and 1 and the newcomer on Levels 2 to 4.

**The screens under these rules.**

- Level 0 puts the Results card first after a run (the circuit editor follows), the Circuit card first before it, and the device card becomes six stat tiles with its rows behind Details. A circuit preset (Section 9.6's Bell state and the three-ion GHZ) shows the Section 9.6 check's numbers beside the run's, chip and status included.
- Level 1's register shows each ion's Bloch vector as an arrow in a disc (the x-z projection, the y component written beside it), computed from the reduced state. The MS gate card carries the request control of Section 14.4: a requested angle XX(chi) rebuilds the job with that angle, the scheduler rescales the calibrated waveform by sqrt(chi/(pi/4)) (the pulse solver's s-squared law), the job runs at the full engine and the finished pulse's process matrix is compared with the requested unitary; a request the device cannot satisfy is refused with its reason (the rescaled amplitude above the calibrated carrier Rabi frequency of the drive, or an angle outside the calibrated range). The record of such a run says what was requested.
- Level 2's pulse card carries the second request control: a beat-note detuning set by hand shifts every leg of the pair's calibrated waveform by that offset (blue up, red down, the symmetric scan of Section 7.5) and the job runs with the waveform as written; the loops on Level 3 then fail to close by the amount the physics says, and Level 1 shows the actual unitary of the played gate (its process matrix) beside the requested one.
- Level 3 keeps the loop and the populations as the focal pair; the Fock bars, jumps, process matrix and re-checks are sections below.
- Level 4 puts each page's drawing first and folds the knob panel into a compact strip with the stale badge and Recalibrate.
- Learn is a ladder of activities: the tour (worked example), the faded three-ion GHZ exercise (the same six stops, the annotations withheld until asked), the free exercise (build, predict, run, explain one off-target bar), the discrimination drills generated from the current record (estimate or calibrated; verified or extracted; resolved, frozen or dropped; inside or outside the error bar), the review tray, the published experiments (below), and the mastery log with in-session and delayed unaided accuracy labelled apart. The settings (who, how long) are one compact row.

**Published experiments (the presets of Section 14.5).** Seven entries of Section 9 run as experiments on the physics the core exposes and place the published number beside the simulated one, each number with its own chip: the published value carries a `published.*` ledger record whose tag is the Section 9 row's tag for the source (verified for every entry shipped), and the simulated value carries the `anchor.*` record of the check that recomputed it. A preset states its question, its section row, what it computes, how long it takes, and whether the simulated number is expected to agree: Kirchmair's fidelity is shown with the ledger's corrected form saying that the first-principles terms sit three orders below the measurement and the technical terms dominate, because a learning tool that showed only agreements would teach the wrong lesson about validation. The entries: Harty 2014 microwave randomized benchmarking (Section 9.2), James 1998 equilibrium positions and axial modes (9.1), Monroe 1995 Doppler cooling (9.3), Roos 2000 Lamb-Dicke parameters of 40Ca+ (9.3), Kirchmair 2009 thermal Mølmer-Sørensen populations (9.4), Myerson 2008 readout optimum (9.5) and Crain 2019 SNSPD readout (9.5); plus the two circuit presets of Section 9.6, the Bell state and the three-ion GHZ, which run through the machine itself.

**Packaging.** `flet build macos` (and `windows`, `linux`, `web`) is configured in `pyproject.toml` (`[tool.flet]`); it needs the Flutter SDK, which Flet installs on demand, and on macOS the full Xcode, which the Command Line Tools do not provide. `flet pack` (PyInstaller) is the fallback that needs neither. A packaged build ships the provenance index the README says how to write.

## 11. The visual system

The application has one visual system, kept in `views/theme.py`, on which the rules R1 to R8 stand. Every colour below was chosen by hand and checked by computation: every text on its background at or above 4.5:1 and every outline at or above 3:1, in both modes (the WCAG 2.2 AA thresholds), and the categorical chart colours pass the dataviz validator for colour-vision deficiency against each mode's card surface.

**R9. Neutral surfaces, one accent.** The page is an off-white (`#F7F7F5`) or a near-black (`#131314`); cards are hairline-bordered surfaces one step brighter (white, `#1B1B1D`), never shadows or tints. The accent is one blue (`#2B5FC7`, `#8FB6F5` in the dark scheme): the primary action of the screen, the selected tab, the simulated series. Coral (`#C2325A`, `#F393AE`) is the tertiary: the ideal beside the simulated, the operating point, a highlight. Status colours (pass green, stale amber, fail red, a neutral for not checked) appear only on pills that also carry an icon and the word, so colour never carries meaning alone (R8 kept). The grey-blue secondary container marks selection (the rail, a chosen chip, a clicked tile).

**R10. Chips are quiet.** A provenance chip is the tag's glyph at 10 px in a 24 px hairline pill in the muted ink, still a focusable button with the full record on hover; the glyph with its word appears only in the drawer, where the chip is the subject. The chip certifies the number; it must never compete with it.

**R11. Tiles are unboxed.** A stat tile is the value at 22 px semibold with its chip beside it and the plain label at 12 px in the muted ink wrapping at 150 px beneath; it is as wide as its value needs. Only a tile that is a zoom-in target has a fill, so a fill means "click".

**R12. Layout follows the content width.** Two stacks of cards sit side by side when the content column is at least 960 px wide and one above the other when it is not; the content column is the window less the rail, the explain drawer when open and the margins, capped at 1280 px and centred beyond it. A window resize and a platform brightness change both re-render, so drawings and layouts never keep a stale width. (Flet's `ResponsiveRow` reads the window, not the column, so it is not used.)

**R13. One header, one crumb line.** The level's title (24 px) stands over its question (14 px, muted) and, beneath that, the selection the level looks at (the step, the gate count); nothing sits to the right of them. The zoom bar holds the two zoom buttons, the crumb trail with the level's name first ("Dynamics › job 9459e080 › gate ms[2] › pulse 4 › sample 0") and the explain toggle; the convergence badge lives in the numerics strip only (R6), not twice.

**R14. Sibling pages are tabs.** The eight physics pages and the seven Learn activities are one underlined tab strip that scrolls sideways when narrow, never a row of buttons wrapping into two.

**R15. Words, not tuples.** Ion lists, phases and beams are written ("on ions 0 and 1", "phase -1.5708 rad", "beam 2, 355.0 nm"), never as Python's tuple text.

**R16. The circuit is built, not typed.** Level 0's circuit is a diagram the learner edits (`views/builder.py` over the pure `viewmodel/builder.py`): the qubits as wires labelled q0, q1, ..., every gate a 40 by 34 px tile in a column of the greedy layout (each gate takes the first free column on every wire it spans, and a two-qubit gate spans the wires between its qubits, so no connector crosses a gate), a two-qubit gate a 2 px connector between its ends (a dot and a ring for CNOT, two dots for CZ, two crosses for SWAP, two labelled tiles otherwise). The palette groups the gates by family and the family colours the tile (fixed one-qubit gates on the primary container, rotations on the tertiary, two-qubit gates on the secondary, the native set outlined); the name printed on every tile carries the meaning (R8 kept). A gate arrives by a tap on the palette (it lands at the end of the selected wire, whose label sits on the secondary container), by a drag onto a wire or onto the gate it should precede, or from the + at the end of a wire, which lists the palette; a placed tile is dragged to move it and tapped to open the inspector beneath the wires: its qubits as dropdowns, its angles as fields that take any OpenQASM 2 expression (pi/2, -3*pi/4, sqrt(2)) with quick picks beside them, and earlier, later, delete. Every tile is a button, so Tab reaches it. The store's text stays the truth: each edit re-serialises the circuit to OpenQASM 2 (angles written as fractions of pi when they are one), the worked example is a fixed point of that round trip so opening the builder never rewrites a circuit, Undo takes every edit back (the presets and the worked example included), and the text with an OpenQASM 2 or IonQ JSON import sits behind Code, one disclosure away for the physicist who wants it.

**The type scale.** 24/600 titles with -0.3 letter spacing, 15/600 card titles, 22/600 values, 14 body, 12 small, 11 captions, 10 for chip glyphs and axis titles; the platform's sans. Flet replaces rather than merges a theme text style, so every style in the theme names its colour.

**Charts.** Bars at most 18 px wide with a 4 px rounded top and a square baseline, the target bar a 12 percent wash with a 1.5 px outline (the hatched-target rule of Section 4 kept as an outline); lines 2 px with round caps; gridlines a solid hairline one step off the surface; axis titles and legends in the muted ink; the categorical order blue, orange, aqua, yellow, magenta, violet, assigned by entity and never cycled past the sixth, with a darker mode-specific set for the dark scheme. The sideband roles keep the physics convention: red and blue tones on the mode spectrum, the carrier and far tones neutral.

**The theme choice.** A button at the foot of the rail cycles system, light and dark; the choice persists with the learner (`Learner.theme`), and the default follows the platform. Both schemes are complete: no colour has its only definition in one of them.

**The first launch.** The question "Who is learning?" offers the three self-descriptions of Section 1 as equal-width options, each with one line of what it changes ("Plain words first, explanations open, the worked example first"), and the skip that means assist. The saved learner is read with an 8 s bound so that a slow client never sees the question twice.
